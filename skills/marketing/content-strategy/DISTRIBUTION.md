# Distribution

Making a piece is half the job; getting it seen is the other half. From coreyhaines31/marketingskills `content-strategy` and its `content-distribution.md` reference (MIT). Platform-native posting is [social-media](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/social-media/SKILL.md); launch-day channel playbooks are [product-launch](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/product-launch/SKILL.md); sending to the list is [email-practices](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/backend/email-practices/SKILL.md).

## Create Once, Distribute Twice

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, content-strategy]` "One exceptional piece, reformatted across channels, not a fresh piece per platform." Design the piece to be distributed:
- Write subheads that read as standalone social posts.
- Structure sections so they can be lifted out and stand alone.
- Pick the pull quotes, stats and frames you'll turn into graphics or clips before you write.
- Know the atomized outputs before drafting, so the source piece contains them.

The flagship is the master; every channel gets a cut derived from it.

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, content-strategy]` Use the [ORB channel definitions](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/product-launch/LAUNCH-PLAN.md#the-orb-channels) with this distribution-specific funnel mapping:

| Layer | Funnel role |
|---|---|
| Borrowed | Discovery |
| Rented | Engagement and reach |
| Owned | Conversion and retention |

The source's "Everything, the durable asset" is ownership shorthand, not an exemption from the rules of a platform hosting the channel; keep the canonical owner's qualification.

"Use borrowed and rented reach to funnel strangers into owned channels." Moving people into the email list needs their consent ([email-practices LISTS-AND-CAPTURE.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/backend/email-practices/LISTS-AND-CAPTURE.md)).

## Failure modes

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, content-strategy]`
- **Spray-and-pray:** posting everywhere with no flagship and no repurposing plan; effort scatters and nothing compounds.
- **Platform dependency:** building the audience on rented land; any rented channel can throttle or deprioritize you with no recourse. "Never let rented be the endpoint."
- **The ownership paradox:** most effort goes to channels the team doesn't control while the owned assets that convert get the least attention.

The source attaches two figures to these ("Facebook organic reach fell from ~20% to under 2%", teams spend "~90% of effort" on rented and borrowed channels) with no source for either; don't repeat them as facts.

## Platform half-lives

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, content-strategy]` The source's own rough estimates, not measured data:

| Channel | Rough half-life | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| X post | Minutes to hours | Post often; repost; thread for reach |
| Instagram, Facebook | About a day | Frequent cadence; stories are ephemeral by design |
| LinkedIn post | About a day, longer for strong performers | Fewer, higher-effort posts |
| TikTok, Reels, Shorts | Days to weeks (algorithmic resurfacing) | Evergreen hooks can resurface long after posting |
| YouTube video | Months to years | Search-driven; compounds like a blog post |
| Blog post, SEO | Years | The long tail, the compounding asset |
| Email | Sent once, but archived and repurposable | One-shot attention; harvest into other formats |

Short half-lives reward frequency and repetition; long ones reward depth and evergreen framing.

## The flywheel

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, content-strategy]`
1. **Create** one flagship (guide, video, podcast, original research) with distribution hooks built in.
2. **Atomize** it into channel-native cuts: clips, threads, carousels, quote graphics, email, subhead posts.
3. **Distribute** across owned, rented and borrowed, routing everything back to owned.
4. **Engage** with the responses; capture the questions, objections and reactions.
5. **Feed back:** what resonated and what got asked picks the next flagship; top-performing atoms show what to make more of.

## Atomization checklist per flagship

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, content-strategy]`
- [ ] 3 to 5 standalone social posts from the subheads or key points
- [ ] 1 thread (X) or carousel (LinkedIn, Instagram) of the core argument
- [ ] 2 to 4 short-form video clips (if the source is video or a podcast)
- [ ] 1 or 2 quote or stat graphics
- [ ] 1 email to the owned list linking the flagship (to subscribers who opted in)
- [ ] A repost and reshare schedule across the piece's half-life

## Prove it

Each flagship has its checklist filled before publishing, every cut links back to an owned page, and the email cut goes only to a consented list with its unsubscribe working.
