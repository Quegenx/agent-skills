## Ask first

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, co-marketing]

1. Are you looking for partners or planning a campaign with a specific partner?
2. What type of co-marketing interests you most? (content, events, integrations, community)
3. What's your audience size? (email list, social following, traffic)
4. Do you have existing integration partners?
5. Have you done co-marketing before? What worked or didn't?
6. What's your timeline and budget?

## The ideal partner

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, co-marketing] "The best partners share your audience but don't compete for the same budget."

- Same buyer persona, different problem solved.
- Adjacent in the workflow (before, after, or alongside your tool).
- Similar company stage and customer size.
- Complementary, not competitive.

Questions that surface them: What tools do your customers already use? What do they use before and after your product? Who else sells to your ICP? Which integrations do customers request most?

## Scoring partners

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, co-marketing] Rate each candidate 1 to 5:

| Criterion | What to evaluate |
|---|---|
| **Audience fit** | How closely their audience matches your ICP |
| **Audience size** | Reach worth partnering for |
| **Brand alignment** | Would you be proud to be associated? |
| **Engagement quality** | An active, engaged audience |
| **Reciprocity potential** | Can you offer them equal value? |
| **Ease of execution** | A partnerships team, a history of co-marketing |

The source's eval for scoring adds: weight the criteria by the goal of the campaign, and start with a low-effort format before a big one.

## Where to find partners

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, co-marketing]

- **Integration ecosystem:** your existing integration partners; tools in the same marketplace category; platforms your product plugs into.
- **Adjacent categories:** tools that solve the problem before yours, after yours, or for the same role in a different workflow.
- **Community signals:** who sponsors the same podcasts and newsletters, exhibits at the same conferences, is active in the same communities, and whose content your audience shares.
- **Data sources:** account overlap in Crossbeam; customer surveys ("what else do you use?"); G2 and Capterra category neighbors; job postings that name your tool alongside others. The source also names Reveal; Crossbeam and Reveal merged in June 2024 under the Crossbeam name, so use Crossbeam. [https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/crossbeam-and-reveal-are-joining-forces-to-disrupt-go-to-market-strategy-as-we-know-it-302180673.html]
- **Tools the source lists:** Crossbeam (account overlap), Introw (partner program management, deal registration), PartnerStack (partner and affiliate programs).

## Brainstorming with a partner

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, co-marketing]

1. **Shared audience moments:** trigger events that matter to both audiences; seasonal moments that align with both products; industry trends that affect both customer bases.
2. **Combined value:** what customers can achieve with both tools that they can't with one; the workflow the combination enables; the pain point the integration solves.
3. **Assets each side brings:** audience size and engagement, content expertise, product capabilities, brand credibility, customer stories (yours in one column, theirs in the other).
4. **Idea prompts:**
   - "What would we create if we had to launch something in 2 weeks?"
   - "What content do both our audiences desperately need?"
   - "What would make customers say 'finally, someone did this'?"
   - "What exclusive thing could we offer together?"
   - "What data do we both have that would make a compelling story?"

## Outreach

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, co-marketing] The source's template, with the two lines CAN-SPAM requires added at the end (the source has neither):

```
Subject: [Your Company] + [Their Company] co-marketing idea

Hey [Name],

I'm [Role] at [Your Company]. We [one-line description].

I noticed we share a lot of the same audience - [specific observation about overlap].

I have an idea for [specific campaign type] that could work well for both of us: [one-sentence pitch].

Would you be open to a quick call to explore?

[Your name]
[Company postal address]
[How to opt out of further emails from us]
```

- **US:** CAN-SPAM covers every commercial message and "makes no exception for business-to-business email"; each violating email can cost "up to $53,088". A pitch whose primary purpose is promoting a joint campaign is commercial. [https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business]
- **Canada:** CASL requires consent "before sending them commercial electronic messages". Consent is implied when the address was "conspicuously published" without a statement refusing unsolicited messages, and only when "the message is relevant to the person's business, role, functions or duties" (CASL s.10(9)(b), [email-practices COMPLIANCE.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/backend/email-practices/COMPLIANCE.md#cold-and-unsolicited-email)); so email a Canadian partner contact only at an address they published, and only about their role. [https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email; https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-2.html]
- The full rules, and the EU and UK, are in [email-practices COMPLIANCE.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/backend/email-practices/COMPLIANCE.md); outreach volume and sequences are [cold-outreach SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/cold-outreach/SKILL.md). Send one-to-one, never a mass blast, and never to a scraped list.

**Prepare for the call** [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, co-marketing]: account overlap data (from Crossbeam, if available); 2 to 3 specific campaign ideas ("not just 'let's do something'"); your audience metrics (list size, traffic, engagement); examples of past partnerships; a clear ask (what you want, what you'll provide).

## The agreement

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, co-marketing]

Align first on: lead ownership (how leads are split or shared); promotion commitments; asset creation and approval; timeline; success metrics; follow-up if it works.

The outline:

1. **Campaign description:** what you're doing together.
2. **Responsibilities:** who does what.
3. **Timeline:** key dates and deadlines.
4. **Lead handling:** how leads are captured, shared and followed up. The source's eval names three options: each side keeps its own registrations, all leads shared, or split by source.
5. **Promotion:** minimum commitments from each side.
6. **Branding:** logo usage and the approval process.
7. **Costs:** who pays for what, if anything.
8. **Metrics sharing:** what data you'll share after the campaign.

If registrants' details go to the partner, the signup form says so and gets consent for it; consent must be "specific, informed" under GDPR Art. 4(11) (https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/; [email-practices LISTS-AND-CAPTURE.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/backend/email-practices/LISTS-AND-CAPTURE.md#consent-checkboxes-and-forms)). When one email promotes both companies, CAN-SPAM lets them "designate one of the marketers as the 'sender'" for compliance. [https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business]

## Metrics

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, co-marketing]

- **Quantitative:** leads generated (total and per partner); lead quality (MQL and SQL conversion rate); revenue attributed ([marketing-attribution SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/marketing-attribution/SKILL.md)); audience growth (new subscribers, followers); content engagement (views, downloads, shares).
- **Qualitative:** ease of collaboration; partner responsiveness; audience reception; brand lift; a relationship strengthened for future campaigns.

## Checklist

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, co-marketing]

- **Identification:** list the tools customers already use; check Crossbeam for account overlap; score the top 5 candidates; research their past co-marketing.
- **Planning:** agree on campaign type and goals; define the lead-sharing arrangement; assign responsibilities and deadlines; set success metrics.
- **Execution:** create shared assets (landing page, content); coordinate promotion schedules; brief both teams on talking points.
- **Post-campaign:** share metrics with the partner; debrief on what worked; discuss future collaboration.

## Prove it

- Each candidate partner meets the four ideal-partner traits and has a 1 to 5 score on all six criteria, weighted by the campaign goal.
- The first campaign with a new partner is a low-effort format.
- Every outreach email is one-to-one, names a specific overlap, and carries a postal address and an opt-out; Canadian contacts were emailed only at a published business address about their role.
- The signed agreement covers all eight parts, the lead-handling option is named, and the registration form discloses any sharing with the partner.
- Every giveaway, contest or takeover brief includes the disclosure wording.
