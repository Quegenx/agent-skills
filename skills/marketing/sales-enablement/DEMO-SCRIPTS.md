# Demo scripts and talk tracks

## Structure and call types

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, sales-enablement]

1. **Opening** (2 min): context setting, agenda, confirm goals for the call.
2. **Discovery recap** (3 min): summarize what you learned, confirm priorities.
3. **Solution walkthrough** (15-20 min): 3-4 key workflows mapped to their pain.
4. **Interaction points:** questions to ask during the demo, not just at the end.
5. **Close** (5 min): summarize value, propose next steps with timeline.

The first-demo script below merges the opening and recap into one 5-minute scene; the total is the same.

| Type | Duration | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery call | 30 min | Qualify, understand pain, map buying process |
| First demo | 30-45 min | Show 3-4 workflows tied to their pain |
| Technical deep-dive | 45-60 min | Architecture, security, integrations, API |
| Executive overview | 20-30 min | Business outcomes, ROI, strategic alignment |

- **Demo after discovery, not before.** If you don't know their pain, you're guessing which features matter.
- **Customize to their use case.** Use their terminology, their data (if possible), their workflow.
- **Leave time for questions.** A demo where the prospect doesn't talk is a demo that doesn't close.

## Discovery call

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, sales-enablement] 30 minutes. Goal: qualify the opportunity, understand pain, map the buying process.

**Scene 1, Opening (3 min).** Talk track:

> "Thanks for taking the time, [Name]. I've done some research on [Company] but I'd love to hear from you directly. My goal for today is to understand what you're working on and see if there's a fit, and if there's not, I'll tell you that too. Sound good?"

Establish: the agenda and time expectation; yourself as a peer, not a pitch person; permission to ask questions.

**Scene 2, Situation questions (7 min).**

- "Can you walk me through how your team handles [relevant process] today?"
- "What tools are you currently using for this?"
- "How many people are involved in this workflow?"
- "How long has this been in place?"

Listen for: current process and tools; team size and structure; how established (and how entrenched) the current approach is.

**Scene 3, Pain identification (10 min).**

- "What's the biggest challenge with that process today?"
- "When that breaks down, what happens?"
- "How much time does your team spend on [specific task] per week?"
- "What have you tried to fix this?"
- "If you could wave a magic wand, what would change?"

Listen for: specific, quantifiable pain points; emotional frustration (not just logical problems); failed attempts to solve this (shows urgency); the "magic wand" answer reveals their ideal state. Take notes visibly and repeat back what you hear: "So if I understand correctly, the biggest issue is [X], which costs you about [Y] per month. Is that right?"

**Scene 4, Impact and priority (5 min).**

- "Where does solving this sit on your priority list this quarter?"
- "What happens if you don't solve this in the next 6 months?"
- "Who else is affected by this problem?"
- "Is there budget allocated for solving this?"

Listen for: priority level (nice-to-have vs. must-solve); urgency and consequences of inaction; organizational breadth of the problem; budget signals.

**Scene 5, Buying process (3 min).**

- "If you decided this was the right solution, what does the evaluation process look like?"
- "Who else would be involved in the decision?"
- "Have you evaluated solutions for this before?"
- "What's your timeline for making a decision?"

Listen for: decision-making process and stakeholders; past evaluation experience (and why they didn't buy); timeline for decision.

**Scene 6, Close (2 min).** Talk track:

> "Based on what you've shared, I think there's a strong fit, specifically around [pain point 1] and [pain point 2]. What I'd suggest as a next step is a 30-minute demo where I can show you exactly how we'd address those. I'll customize it to your workflow. Does [specific date/time] work?"

Summarize the 2-3 key pain points, propose a specific next step with a date, and send a calendar invite before you hang up.

## First demo

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, sales-enablement] 30-45 minutes. Goal: show how your product solves their specific pain; advance to evaluation or pilot.

**Scene 1, Opening and recap (5 min).** Talk track:

> "Last time we spoke, you mentioned [pain point 1], [pain point 2], and [goal]. I've put together a demo focused on those three areas. If I've missed anything, flag it and we'll adjust. Sound good?"

Recap discovery findings to show you listened; confirm priorities haven't changed; set expectation for what they'll see.

**Scene 2, Workflow 1, primary pain point (10 min).** (1) Restate the pain: "You mentioned [specific problem]..."; (2) show the solution step by step; (3) highlight the outcome: "This means [specific benefit]..." At the 5-minute mark: "How does this compare to how you're handling it today?" Avoid showing every feature of this section, getting lost in settings or configuration, and talking for more than 3 minutes without asking a question.

**Scene 3, Workflow 2, secondary pain point (8 min).** Same structure: restate pain, show solution, highlight outcome. Interaction point: "Is this the kind of visibility your team has been asking for?"

**Scene 4, Workflow 3, differentiator (7 min).** Show something they can't do today and can't get from competitors. Talk track:

> "This is where we're really different from [competitor/status quo]. [Explain the unique capability]. For example, [Customer] uses this to [specific outcome]."

Interaction point: "How would your team use this?" The capability must be one competitors verifiably lack ([BATTLE-CARDS.md](BATTLE-CARDS.md)).

**Scene 5, Proof point (3 min).** Talk track:

> "Let me share a quick example. [Customer similar to them] was in a similar situation, [brief challenge]. After implementing, they saw [specific metrics]. Their [role] said [quote]."

Choose a case study that matches their industry, size, or use case; keep it brief, as reinforcement, not a presentation.

**Scene 6, Close (5 min).** Talk track:

> "Based on what we've covered, here's what I'd recommend as next steps: [specific next step]. This typically takes [timeline]. Who else on your team should be involved? I can set up a [follow-up meeting type] for [date]."

Propose a specific next step (not "let me know"), identify additional stakeholders, set a follow-up date before ending the call, and send the recap email within 2 hours.

## Technical deep-dive

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, sales-enablement] 45-60 minutes. Goal: satisfy technical evaluation criteria; address architecture, security, and integration concerns. Attendees typically include their technical evaluator (engineer, architect, IT lead) plus your SE or solutions engineer.

**Scene 1, Opening (3 min).** Talk track:

> "I know your goal today is to understand the technical details: architecture, security, integrations, and how this fits your stack. I'll walk through each area and leave plenty of time for questions. What's your top priority for this session?"

**Scene 2, Architecture overview (10 min).** Cover: high-level architecture diagram; infrastructure and hosting (cloud provider, regions); data flow and storage; scalability approach; uptime SLA and reliability track record. Interaction point: "How does this compare to your current infrastructure requirements?"

**Scene 3, Security and compliance (10 min).** Cover: certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, etc.); data encryption (at rest, in transit); access controls and authentication (SSO, RBAC); audit logging; data residency and privacy (GDPR, CCPA); penetration testing cadence. Name only certifications and practices the company has. Interaction point: "What are your must-have security requirements? I want to make sure we address them specifically."

**Scene 4, Integrations and API (15 min).** Cover: native integrations relevant to their stack; API capabilities (REST, GraphQL, webhooks); authentication methods; rate limits and data sync frequency; a live demo of a relevant integration. Interaction point: "Walk me through your current stack. I want to map out exactly how we'd fit in." The substance of these answers (rate limits, webhooks, pagination) is in [api-design SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/backend/api-design/SKILL.md).

**Scene 5, Implementation and migration (5 min).** Cover: implementation timeline and phases; data migration process; configuration requirements; training and onboarding; ongoing support model. Interaction point: "What does your team's capacity look like for implementation? That helps me scope the right timeline."

**Scene 6, Q&A and close (10 min).** Talk track: "What questions do I need to answer for you to feel confident about the technical fit?" Answer directly; if you don't know, say so and follow up. Document all questions, propose the next step (security review, proof of concept, pilot), and send a technical documentation summary within 24 hours.

## Executive overview

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, sales-enablement] 20-30 minutes. Goal: executive buy-in on the business case; advance to budget approval or decision.

**Scene 1, Opening (2 min).** Talk track:

> "Thanks for your time, [Name]. [Champion] has been evaluating [your product] and the results look strong. I'll keep this focused on the business impact and what a partnership looks like. I know your time is valuable so I'll aim to leave 10 minutes for questions."

Be concise ("executives punish rambling"), reference the champion and work done so far, set a clear agenda.

**Scene 2, The problem and cost (5 min).** Talk track:

> "Based on what [Champion] shared, your team is spending [X hours/$ amount] on [problem]. That's [annual cost]. It's also creating [secondary impact: risk, delays, churn]. This isn't unique to you, it's an industry-wide challenge, and the companies solving it are seeing [outcome]."

Use their numbers, not generic benchmarks; connect to metrics they care about (revenue, cost, risk); keep it to 2-3 key points.

**Scene 3, The solution and differentiation (5 min).** Talk track:

> "Here's what we do differently. [One-sentence explanation]. For your team specifically, this means [specific benefit 1] and [specific benefit 2]. [Champion]'s team has already seen [early result or reaction from evaluation]."

High-level, not feature-level; tie to their strategic priorities; reference the champion's evaluation.

**Scene 4, ROI and business case (5 min).** Talk track:

> "Here's the business case. Based on your team's numbers: [walk through ROI calculation]. Expected payback period is [X months]. Over 3 years, the total value is [$ amount]. [Customer similar to them] saw [specific result] within [timeframe]."

"Show the math, not just the conclusion"; "use conservative estimates (executives discount inflated numbers)"; one strong case study, not three weak ones. The calculator is in [SALES-ASSETS.md](SALES-ASSETS.md#roi-calculators).

**Scene 5, Q&A and decision (5-10 min).** Talk track: "What questions do you have? And, assuming the business case holds up, what does the decision process look like from here?" Listen more than talk, answer concisely, get a clear next step and timeline, and "thank the champion in front of the executive."

## Interaction points

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, sales-enablement]

When to ask:

- **After showing each workflow:** "How does this compare to your current process?"
- **When you see a reaction:** "I noticed you reacted to that. What are you thinking?"
- **Before moving to the next section:** "Any questions on this before we move on?"
- **When showing a differentiator:** "How would your team use this?"
- **At the midpoint:** "Are we covering the right things, or should we adjust?"

Not to ask: "Does that make sense?" (patronizing); "Are you still with me?" (implies they're lost); "Isn't that cool?" (salesy); rhetorical questions that don't invite real dialogue.

When a prospect asks "Can you show me X?":

1. **If it's quick:** show it now, then return to your flow.
2. **If it's a tangent:** "Great question. Let me note that and show you after the main flow so we stay on track."
3. **If it's not possible:** "We don't do that today. Here's how customers handle it: [alternative]."

Never say "I'll get back to you" without writing it down and following up within 24 hours.

## Prove it

Each script has scene timings that add up to its call type's duration; no scene runs more than 3 minutes of talk without a question; the demo maps 3-4 workflows to pains named in discovery; the close proposes one dated next step; the recap goes out within 2 hours of a first demo and technical docs within 24 hours of a deep-dive; every promised follow-up is written down.
