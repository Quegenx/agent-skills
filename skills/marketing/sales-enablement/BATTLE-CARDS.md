# Battle cards

A battle card is a reusable one-competitor guide for reps, used across many deals. For a single live objection, use [OBJECTION-HANDLING.md](OBJECTION-HANDLING.md#live-deal-objection); for public comparison pages, [comparison-pages SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/comparison-pages/SKILL.md). Every line follows the "claims reps can defend" rule in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md#the-rules). From coreyhaines31/marketingskills, skill `sales-enablement` (MIT).

## Ask which competitor first

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, sales-enablement] If the request doesn't name one and context doesn't identify it, ask in one short question. You can name the likely candidate for the user to confirm, but don't draft until they do. "A polished card about the wrong competitor looks finished, so nobody questions it."

## The eight sections

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, sales-enablement]

1. **When you'll see them:** segments, deal sizes, and triggers where they show up.
2. **Why buyers consider them:** their real appeal, in the buyer's words.
3. **Where they're strong:** honest, so reps aren't blindsided.
4. **Where we win, and for whom:** tied to evidence. Without win-loss evidence ([WIN-LOSS-ANALYSIS.md](WIN-LOSS-ANALYSIS.md)) write it as a hypothesis for reps to test, with the evidence that would confirm it.
5. **Discovery questions:** questions that surface the fit criteria where you're stronger, without trashing them.
6. **Top objections:** with one-line responses and proof.
7. **When to walk away:** deal profiles where they're the better fit.
8. **Last verified:** date, and what changed since the previous version.

Competitor facts are dated and sourced; write "not listed on their site" rather than "they don't have it"; a guess about why they did something is labelled as a guess.

## Keeping it current

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, sales-enablement]

- Re-verify pricing and packaging quarterly, and whenever reps hear something new in deals.
- Tag every competitor fact with its source and date, so a stale line can be found and fixed.
- One page. If reps can't find the answer mid-call, cut until they can.

Pull competitor facts from competitor dossiers when they exist. The repo's research pipeline builds sourced battle cards (landmines, objection handlers, talk tracks, each with an evidence URL, after a fact-check of the feature matrix): [web-research COMPETITORS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/web-research/COMPETITORS.md#battle-cards).

## Prove it

The card names one confirmed competitor; all eight sections are present on one page; every competitor fact carries a source and a date; "where we win" cites evidence or is labelled a hypothesis; "when to walk away" is not empty; the last-verified date is within the quarter.
