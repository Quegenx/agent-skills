# Example plan, condensed

The source ships a full worked plan (973 lines) for an anonymized client, "Quietude": a hybrid hardware and software wellness company, Tier 1, mid-raise on a seed round, $0 paid budget. This file keeps the patterns that make it good, not its client data. Source: [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, marketing-plan] `references/example-quietude.md` (v1.2.2, MIT, Corey Haines). Its names, prices, KPIs, keyword difficulties, outlets, podcasts, venues and the fCMO's tool list are example data and are left out; its figures are the example client's, not benchmarks.

## What the source says was strong

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, marketing-plan]

- The strategic frame leaned on the founder's own framing as the content pillar.
- Current state scored the 17-section rubric from existing materials (no formal audit run).
- The 90-day roadmap had owner-assigned moves, not just actions.
- The ops stack had a concrete operational proof point.
- The idea bank cross-referenced all 139 ideas, including explicit skips with reasons.

## Patterns worth copying

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, marketing-plan]

- **Three big bets, ranked by leverage,** in the executive summary, each a paragraph with a thesis ("Fix the leak before pouring water in"; "Compound the moats the company already has"; "Build the founder-and-fCMO operating system that lets a 4-person team market like a 20-person one"), followed by a plausible twelve-month state and about six numbered 90-day priorities.
- **State the budget's implication plainly:** "The 90-day plan must produce gains without any paid lever pulled."
- **Three honest tables in Current state:** what's already done (Asset | Status | Marketing leverage), what's in flight (Item | Status | Blocker, with "None - ship-ready" where true) and what's stuck (Issue | Cost of inaction | Action).
- **Read the funnel's shape, not its absolutes:** in the example, day 1, day 7 and day 35 paid conversion rose about fourfold over 35 days, which the plan read as "the product converts given time and contact"; in a throttled beta, optimize against shape and cohort comparison.
- **Paid gated on named preconditions:** "Don't fire until: (a) onboarding bedrock fix is shipped, (b) Flow 6 is live, (c) at least one Pillar landing page is in voice. Paid amplifies what already works - premature paid amplifies what's broken."
- **A bedrock fix before any test:** remove what is broken (in the example, a hard gate in onboarding with a confirmed conversion drop) instead of rewording it.
- **Sequential onboarding tests,** each variant "a pure expression of one belief about what drives activation": bedrock baseline → V3 vs. baseline → winner vs. V1 → winner vs. V2, about seven weeks to a winner.
- **Lifecycle ship order by content stability:** the hardware post-purchase flow first (it references no app screens), the lapsed-user flow second, the onboarding emails held "until app UI stabilizes"; "Don't ship copy that will need rewriting in 8 weeks."
- **Reconcile metrics that disagree before reporting them:** the example's "29% monthly App Store churn" against a "38% 12-month retention" claim ("Both can be true": annual cohort retention versus gross monthly churn), and an implied ~$4 per subscriber per month against a $30 list price (annual plan mix or promotional pricing). Name which one is reported externally.
- **Lead referral with whoever is already raising a hand:** five inbound ambassadors become the pilot cohort before any full program.
- **Run the pricing audit before testing changes.**
- **Open decisions ranked by impact,** each with a "Blocks:" line naming what it holds up.
- **A RACI table** (Domain | Responsible | Accountable | Consulted | Informed) over strategy, brand voice, implementation, lifecycle, SEO, store copy, founder social, events, ambassadors, B2B sales, pricing, investor narrative and future hires.
- **An appendix in three tiers:** published to the team, founder-authored context, and working drafts, with no machine-local paths.

## What not to copy

- **The idea-bank counts.** The example's Section 12.1 is headed "88 mapped" and its summary says "88 ideas applicable to Acquisition", but the section has 120 rows (97 without skips); across 12.1 to 12.6 there are 145 rows covering the 139 ideas. Its "roughly 30% of the available tactical surface area" rests on those counts. Derive counts from your own tables.
- **Its kickoff fixes** (a "CAN-SPAM footer" on a draft flow, a subscription center that "Improves deliverability (lower complaint rates)"): the rules are in [email-practices](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/backend/email-practices/SKILL.md).
- **Its client claims** (the email tool's MCP letting a "non-technical team ship flows", category retention averages, revenue per venue): the example client's statements, not measurements.

## Prove it

A new plan built from these patterns ranks three bets, states what the budget rules out, fills the done, in-flight and stuck tables honestly, gates paid on named preconditions, tests onboarding one belief at a time, ships lifecycle flows by content stability, reconciles disagreeing metrics, ranks open decisions with what each blocks, and carries no number copied from the example.
