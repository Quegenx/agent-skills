# Headlines by awareness stage

Text-led static concepts, especially B2B feed ads, where the words carry the idea. These are compact treatments inside the angle and template workflow; they don't replace audience research, the [hook system](HOOKS.md) or [platform specs](PLATFORM-SPECS.md). From [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, ad-creative, awareness-headlines.md] (MIT), which re-expresses the maintainer's follow-up to the ivangfalco/ads-skills remix. The source's sample headlines are invented illustrations of structure, so they're left out here: fill every treatment from your verified corpus.

## Choose the reader's next step

| Awareness | What the reader needs | Useful treatments | CTA direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware or new to the category | Recognize a specific lived situation | Feeling, conversation | Learn about the situation or mechanism |
| Problem-aware | See a credible way out of a named friction | Pain + outcome, time-anchored friction | See the workflow or explanation |
| Solution-aware | Understand how this approach differs | State change, documented statistic | Compare the mechanism or watch a demo |
| Product-aware | Resolve an objection with specific proof | Statistic, pain + outcome | Inspect proof, try, or book the relevant next step |

Stage is a working hypothesis about this audience, not a diagnosis from the platform alone. If unknown, state the assumption and propose two distinct stage hypotheses. A formula alone can't prove an ad will convert.

## Six treatments

Each can serve several stages; choose by what the reader already knows.

| Treatment | Construction and guardrail |
|---|---|
| **Feeling** | Name a recognizable feeling tied to a specific work or life moment. Avoid vague empowerment or a forced positive/negative reveal. |
| **Conversation** | Two short lines from a recognizable situation. Use an actual permitted exchange, or label invented dialogue as a dramatization; never fabricate a testimonial attribution. |
| **State change** | Show the current and the proposed workflow with concrete artifacts. Say what changes; don't force "not X, Y" copy or unsupported competitor claims. |
| **Time-anchored friction** | Name an obsolete or repetitive process and its actual moment (month-end, Sunday night). Critique the process without shaming the buyer or inventing a trend. |
| **Documented statistic** | One supported number plus its population, period or meaningful qualifier. Keep its real precision; "rounded numbers are not evidence of fakery." |
| **Pain + outcome** | One named friction and a concrete next action or outcome, usually under ten words. Brevity is a drafting constraint, not permission to drop a material qualifier. |

Read each draft against [anti-slop-writing TELLS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/anti-ai-slop/anti-slop-writing/TELLS.md). Don't produce six near-identical sentences to fill the menu. Keep customer language where it is permitted and relevant; facts can ground a paraphrase without becoming a fabricated quote.

## Gather proof before copy

Build a small evidence sheet: source ID, verbatim phrase or product fact, audience, date, allowed usage, limitations. Aim for five useful customer phrases from more than one perspective; with fewer, report the gap and reduce diversity claims rather than inventing quotes. Scaled batches still follow the [grounded inputs](SKILL.md#grounded-inputs) rules.

Separate **language evidence** (a complaint: "the export takes forever") from **claim evidence** (a measured result across a defined pilot). A complaint supports the pain angle; it doesn't substantiate a numerical promise. Keep a pilot's sample size and period in readable copy next to the claim, or drop the statistic when a truthful version can't fit. Never generalize a pilot to "every customer" or into a guarantee [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, ad-creative, evals #19]. The claim tests are in [anti-slop-copy](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/anti-ai-slop/anti-slop-copy/SKILL.md#3-tests-every-headline-and-claim-must-pass).

## The headline as the visual

For the Headline Statement and Stat Callout templates ([STATIC-TEMPLATES.md](STATIC-TEMPLATES.md)) when the words carry the concept. A production hypothesis, not a guarantee that text-only beats other formats.

- One dominant sentence in a bold, legible sans-serif. As a starting point, the headline takes roughly 50 to 70% of the composition; adjust after testing at actual size.
- Intentional line breaks that keep phrases together; product or logo secondary unless recognizing the product is the job.
- A dark background with light text, or a strong brand color with contrasting text, from the brand's actual palette, checked for readability ([color-system](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/web-ui/color-system/SKILL.md)).
- Include the audience's specific moment, one defensible idea and enough qualification to read truthfully. Leave out competing stock imagery, tiny disclaimers, fake review UI, extra badges and an unsupported second claim.
- Preview at the **actual mobile feed display size**, not only on a desktop canvas: the whole headline and its material qualifiers must read without zooming. If not, shorten the claim or change the layout; never shrink the required qualification into a footnote.
- Export sizes, crops, safe areas and text limits come from [PLATFORM-SPECS.md](PLATFORM-SPECS.md). The 50 to 70% suggestion is not a platform rule or a text-coverage threshold.

## Deliver and learn

Per concept: audience and stage assumption, distinct angle, treatment + template, headline, supporting qualification, CTA, source IDs, and a visual spec with the mobile readability check. Mark evidence gaps; keep the output as concepts for review unless publishing is authorized.

Test stage and angle hypotheses before font tweaks. Compare the downstream qualified outcome against the control, holding offer and audience steady. Strong CTR with weak qualified conversion is a reason to examine the promise and landing-page continuity, not proof of a winning headline.

## Prove it

Given two customer comments and one product fact, the concepts report that only two phrases exist rather than inventing five, label any invented dialogue as dramatization, trace every number to the fact, state an awareness hypothesis, and include a mobile-size readability check; given a pilot of 8 teams with no control, no headline says every customer saves 67%, the sample and period stay readable next to the claim, and no copy shames buyers [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, ad-creative, evals #17, #18, #19].
