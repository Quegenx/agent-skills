## Types, examples and tips

| Type | Examples | Why they work | Implementation tips |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calculators | ROI, savings, cost comparison, salary, tax estimator, pricing estimator, compound interest, break-even | Personalized output, high perceived value, share-worthy results, clear problem to solution | Keep inputs simple; show calculations transparently; make results shareable; add "powered by" branding |
| Generators | Policy (privacy, terms), template, name or tagline, email subject line, resume builder, color palette, logo maker, contract | Tangible output, saves time, easily shared, repeat usage | Output immediately usable; allow customization; offer download or export; "Include email gating for premium outputs" |
| Analyzers and auditors | Website grader, SEO analyzer, email subject tester, headline analyzer, security checker, performance auditor, accessibility checker, code quality analyzer | Curiosity-driven, personalized insights, creates awareness of problems, natural lead to solution | "Score or grade for gamification"; benchmark against averages; actionable recommendations; follow up with improvement offers |
| Testers and validators | Meta tag preview, email rendering test, mobile-friendly test, speed test, DNS checker, SSL certificate checker, redirect checker, broken link finder | Immediate utility, bookmark-worthy, repeat usage, professional necessity | "Fast results are essential"; show pass or fail clearly; provide fix instructions; integrate with your product where relevant |
| Libraries and resources | Icons, templates, code snippets, example gallery, industry directory, resource list, swipe files, font pairing | High SEO value, ongoing traffic, establishes authority, linkable asset | Searchable and filterable; easy copying and downloading; update regularly; accept community submissions |
| Interactive educational | Tutorials, code playgrounds, visual explainers, quizzes and assessments, simulators, comparison tools, decision trees, configurators | Engages deeply, demonstrates expertise, shareable, memory-creating | Hands-on; immediate feedback; lead to deeper resources; capture engaged users |

A meta tag preview or share-card checker overlaps with this repo's own share-preview rules in [seo](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/seo/SKILL.md); a site grader or auditor maps to the checks in [site-audit](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/site-audit/SKILL.md).

## Concepts by industry

- **SaaS product:** product ROI calculator, competitor comparison tool, readiness assessment quiz, template library for the use case, feature configurator.
- **Agency and services:** industry benchmark tool, project scoping calculator, portfolio review tool, cost estimator, proposal generator.
- **E-commerce:** product finder quiz, comparison tool, size or fit calculator, savings calculator, gift finder.
- **Developer tools:** code snippet library, testing or preview tool, documentation generator, interactive tutorials, API playground.
- **Finance:** financial calculators, investment comparison, budget planner, tax estimator, loan calculator.

## Case library

"Use these sourced examples to study product design and distribution. They are not transferable conversion benchmarks: a tool's existence or later acquisition does not establish its visitor-to-lead rate, CAC, or incremental revenue." The cases were not re-verified for this repo; the links are the source's.

- **Unsplash (Crew):** [the founder describes](https://unsplash.com/blog/hello-unsplash-inc/) launching a Tumblr blog with Dropbox links: 20,000 people signed up on day one, and a few months later it had 80,000 members and over one million downloads. Selecting ten featured photos every ten days took about an hour; the post gives no build time. [Unsplash later announced its acquisition by Getty Images](https://unsplash.com/blog/unsplash-getty/). Lesson: a useful byproduct can become a resource library; validate demand before building a custom platform.

| Tool | Company | What the source establishes | Design lesson to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Website Grader](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/website-grader) | HubSpot | A free website assessment tool | An analyzer can demonstrate expertise before a sales conversation; its inputs can help identify fit |
| [SaaS business calculators](https://changelog.baremetrics.com/saas-business-calculators-25uoes) | Baremetrics | A free calculator suite including startup runway | Solve a calculation adjacent to your paid product |
| [Headline Analyzer](https://coschedule.com/headline-analyzer) | CoSchedule | A free headline analysis entry point | Repeatable feedback can introduce a broader product |
| [Salary Calculator](https://buffer.com/resources/transparent-salaries/) | Buffer | A public calculator based on Buffer's salary formula | A shareable resource can communicate company values; reach is not qualified demand |
| [Logo Maker](https://www.shopify.com/tools/logo-maker) | Shopify | A free logo creation tool (Hatchful) | Solve a task prospective merchants face while starting a business |
| [Keyword Explorer](https://moz.com/products/pro/pricing) | Moz | Limited Keyword Explorer access through the free Community account | A limited free surface of a paid product can work as its demonstration |

## Using the cases

The evidence rule, verbatim: "The sources above do not establish comparable trial-conversion or signup rates. Do not quote the former unsourced rates as benchmarks. If you add a quantitative case, attach its primary source, reporting date, cohort, numerator and denominator, funnel stage, and attribution limits. Distinguish visitors, leads, trials, paying customers, and incremental outcomes."

- **Pattern-match:** borrow a mechanism (grading, calculating, generating) and treat its effect on your funnel as a hypothesis.
- **Forecast explicitly:** "Use your own baseline or label low/base/high conversion assumptions. Do not substitute another company's traffic or lifetime revenue for your expected outcome."
- **Justify the build:** compare expected qualified-customer contribution against build, distribution and maintenance costs; "Track tool use → qualified lead → customer by cohort, then test incremental contribution where feasible."

Asked what results free tools actually get, the source's eval expects named sourced cases without invented conversion rates, documented outcomes kept apart from assumptions, the four pitfalls, and tracking leads and conversion over vanity metrics [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, free-tools evals].

## Prove it

Every case quoted in a plan links its primary source and states only what that source shows; any number carries its date, cohort, numerator, denominator and funnel stage; and the forecast uses your own baseline or labelled low, base and high assumptions.
