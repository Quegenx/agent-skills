# Story angles

What earns press, copied from coreyhaines31/marketingskills `public-relations` (MIT), which distils it from Corey Haines's *Founding Marketing*, ch. 5. Chase press for the compound effect (authority, journalist relationships, a citable record), not the traffic bump: "The bump fades in a day; the backlinks, the relationships, and the citable record don't." If the pitch is "we built a thing," there's no story; if it is "here's a shift happening and we're proof of it," there is.

## The three angles

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] Every pitchable story bends toward one of three shapes. Pick the one that fits the moment; don't force all three.

| Angle | When to use | Exemplar (the book's) | How to run it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Founding Story** | You're early, have a sharp personal reason you exist, and will build in public and share real revenue | Pieter Levels' "12 startups in 12 months": shipping in the open, posting MRR screenshots, the challenge itself as the story | Share the arc (why you started, what you've learned, where the numbers are now), not the feature list. "Revenue transparency is the hook most founders are too scared to use." |
| **David vs Goliath** | There's a giant in your category and you do one thing dramatically better | Superhuman vs Gmail: "we're small" reframed as "the fast, obsessive alternative to the default everyone tolerates" | Name the Goliath, name the one thing you beat them at, let the size gap do the emotional work. "Don't hide that you're small, lead with it." |
| **Have an Enemy** | There's a systemic wrong in your space you can credibly stand against, and you'll take a real position | HEY vs Apple's App Store (2020): the fight was against App Store rules founders saw as broken, not Apple the company | Define the broken system precisely, stake a clear position, and be willing to be quoted on it. "A half-hearted enemy reads as a marketing stunt." |

**Guardrail:** the enemy is a system or a norm, never a named competitor. "'Company X is bad' is a smear; 'this way of doing things is broken' is a movement."

The app-growth guide's angle list (Numbers, Problem solved, Unique data, Category creation, David vs Goliath, Timely, Human story, tested by "one sentence that makes someone say 'interesting'") [eronred, ../../mobile/app-growth/LAUNCH-AND-MARKETING.md] is a second, app-launch list.

## Data stories

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] The most reliably pitchable moment is "proprietary numbers no one else has, packaged as an industry benchmark". Journalists can build a whole piece around a stat; you get the citation.

- **Exemplars:** Stripe's annual data reports; Intercom's benchmark reports. Each turns internal data into an annual, citable event.
- **How to run it:** "find the one number in your data that surprises, wrap it in methodology, and package it as a standalone one-pager (not your homepage)." Pitch it with the data story template in [PITCHING.md](PITCHING.md#pitch-templates).
- **Milestones:** "a milestone alone ('we hit $1M ARR') isn't a story, milestone *with narrative* is." Attach the number to a shift, a lesson, or a David vs Goliath frame.

## Relationships before you need them

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] Cold-pitching a journalist the day you have news is the hard way. Climb the ladder months earlier, for a handful of journalists on your beat:

1. **Follow their beat.** Read their last 5 articles; know what they cover and what they're tired of covering.
2. **Add value with no ask.** Reply to their work with a useful data point or correction, share their pieces, answer a question they post.
3. **Be a reliable source.** When they need a quote fast, be the person who "responds in 20 minutes with a clean, quotable line", even when there's nothing in it for you.

## The flywheel

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] One core asset feeds every channel: create one exceptional thing and reformat it five ways.

```
Core asset (data report / strong POV / founding story)
    │
    ├─→ Thread (X / LinkedIn) - the hook, teased publicly
    ├─→ Article (your blog) - the full argument, the destination
    ├─→ Guest post (someone else's audience) - borrow reach
    ├─→ Podcast appearance - the spoken, citable version
    └─→ Video - the durable, AI-crawled version
```

- Publish the article as the owned home base, tease it as a thread, pitch the guest post and podcast off the same idea, cut the video from the recording.
- Every format strengthens the next pitch: a journalist who finds your thread, article and podcast on a topic "sees a voice in the space, not an opportunist" ([NEWSJACKING.md](NEWSJACKING.md#the-public-trail)).
- "Weak assets stall it immediately."

## Prove it

- The story fits one named angle (or is a data story), and the product appears only as evidence.
- An Enemy story names a system or norm, never a competitor.
- A data story has its surprising number, a methodology and a standalone one-pager URL.
- A milestone pitch attaches the number to a shift, a lesson or a David vs Goliath frame.
