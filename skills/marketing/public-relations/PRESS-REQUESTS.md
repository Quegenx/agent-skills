## Daily triage

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] Treat it like email triage: a fast pass, a deep answer on the rare match.

1. **Filter (5 minutes).** Drop everything where you have no direct experience or data, from outlets your ICP doesn't read, or with a deadline you can't meet. Keep only requests you can answer completely, named and on the record. The source's estimate: "50 daily requests, 2-4 worth answering."
2. **Deep response (15 minutes per request).** Read the request 3 times (what's the actual angle?), look up the journalist's recent coverage, write a custom answer, and send within their deadline ("early > late").
3. **Log** date, platform, journalist and outlet, topic, response sent, outcome (no response, passed, quoted, linked). "After 30 responses, you'll see which topics/platforms convert."

## Response template

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] Under 200 words; journalists scan 50+ replies for one quote.

```
Hi [name],

Quick response to your request about [topic].

[Specific credential - 1 sentence. "Built X for 5 years" / "Led marketing at Y" / "Have analyzed N companies in space"]

The most important thing about [topic]: [your actual point in 2 sentences].

[A specific example, story, or data point - this is what gets quoted.]

[If applicable: a contrarian or surprising angle that differentiates from typical answers.]

Happy to expand on any of this, share data, or be quoted directly.

Feel free to use this attribution:
[Your name], [your title], [your company]

Contact for follow-up: [email + phone]
```

The structure: a one-sentence intro, a one-sentence credential, a two-sentence answer, a specific example (the quotable part), an optional differentiator, a clear offer, a pre-written attribution ("saves them 30 seconds"), and contact details.

## What gets picked

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] The source presents these as patterns from quoted responses; it gives no data for them.

- **Quotable specificity:** a concrete result from something you did, not "Companies should focus on customer experience."
- **A concrete credential:** what you actually ran and where, not "As a marketing expert...". "Specificity beats title-stacking."
- **A story over advice:** what happened to you and what changed, not "It's important to track the right metrics." "Stories make articles. Advice makes filler."
- **Formatted for their workflow:** a pre-written attribution, several quotable lines to pick from, a link to a high-res headshot (don't attach), a one-line company description.
- **Speed:** the source claims "Most quotes come from responses sent in the first 6 hours. After 24 hours, your chances drop sharply", and advises treating deadlines "as if they're 24h earlier than stated."

## What kills a response

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations]

- Pitching your product when they asked for expert commentary.
- Generic advice any expert could give.
- Several "experts" from one company answering the same request ("looks coordinated, often is").
- Hiring a PR firm to spam responses.
- "Demanding a link back": most journalists can't promise links.
- Missing the deadline by a day or more.
- A long bio before the actual answer.
- Asking to "see the article before publication": "you don't get to do that".
- Asking what other experts said.

## Is it worth the hours

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] The source's 90-day estimates: 5 hours a week of custom answers gives 3 to 10 quoted placements; 1 hour a week of templated answers gives 0 to 2; outsourcing to a PR firm gives "Lots of submissions, few quotes". A tier-1 quote brings a backlink, a sales-collateral asset, AI-citation surface and legitimacy; a tier-3 quote, often a nofollow backlink.

**Decision rule:** "if you can sustain 5 hr/week of quality responses for 90 days, this is worth it. If you can only do 1 hr/week, skip it and invest in proactive pitching instead" ([PITCHING.md](PITCHING.md)).

## Setup checklist

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations]

- [ ] The press page exists and is current ([SKILL.md](SKILL.md#the-rules), rule 3).
- [ ] A one-line credential is written and rehearsed.
- [ ] A high-res headshot sits at a public URL.
- [ ] 3 to 5 specific stories or data points are ready.
- [ ] 2 to 3 platforms chosen.
- [ ] A daily 20-minute triage window is blocked.
- [ ] Responses are logged in a tracker.

"Without these, you're spamming and wasting their time and yours."

## Prove it

- Every answer sent is under 200 words, named and on the record, has a specific example and a pre-written attribution, and went out before the stated deadline.
- No answer pitches the product, asks for a link or a preview, or comes from a second person at the same company.
- The log has every response and outcome, and after 90 days the hours spent are compared with the decision rule.
