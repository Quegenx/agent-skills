## The media list

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] The goal: 20 to 40 journalists who actually cover your beat. "Not 500 names from a database."

For each candidate:

- [ ] Read their **last 5 articles**: are they covering your beat right now?
- [ ] Note their **publication**: does it reach your ICP?
- [ ] Check their **bio** on the outlet site: what topics do they own?
- [ ] Check **X and LinkedIn** for what they're posting about this week.
- [ ] Note their **email** (usually on the outlet author page, Muck Rack, or the company About page).
- [ ] Check **Muck Rack** if available: it shows recent topics and pitch preferences.

| Where to find candidates | How |
|---|---|
| Reverse lookup from coverage you want | Find 5 articles about competitors or your category, note the bylines |
| Topic search on Muck Rack | The free tier shows journalists by topic |
| X lists | "[your niche] reporters" lists already exist |
| LinkedIn search | "Journalist" plus "[your category]", filtered by recent activity |
| Newsletter author pages | Beehiiv, Substack, ConvertKit creators are pitchable |
| Podcast hosts | Listen to an episode before pitching ([PODCAST-PREP.md](PODCAST-PREP.md)) |

Don't waste time on: mass media databases (Cision, Meltwater) at an early stage ("overkill and expensive"); journalists who haven't posted in 6+ months; generic "editor-in-chief" addresses; journalists whose bio says "no PR pitches" ("respect it").

## Fit scoring

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] Score each journalist 1 to 10 on four dimensions, multiply by the weight, sum and rank; focus on the top 20.

| Dimension | What it measures | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Beat match** | Do they cover your category specifically? | 3x |
| **Reach** | Outlet audience plus their byline traction | 2x |
| **Engagement** | Do they respond to pitches publicly or on X? | 2x |
| **Recency** | Have they written about a related topic in the last 30 days? | 1x |

- **Tier 1 (8 to 10):** a personal pitch with an original angle, custom each time.
- **Tier 2 (5 to 7):** a standard pitch, lightly customized.
- **Tier 3 (below 5):** skip, or pitch only when the story is exceptional.

The source's numbers don't add up: weights total 8x, so a weighted sum runs to 80, while the tiers read on a 1 to 10 scale and its profile template says "X/40". It gives no conversion between them.

## Pitch templates

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] Six structures; pick the one that matches the story ([STORY-ANGLES.md](STORY-ANGLES.md)).

**1. Data story**

```
Subject: [Specific stat] - [implication]

Hi [name],

I noticed you covered [recent article] - wanted to share data that might
be relevant.

We [analyzed N / surveyed N / tracked N] and found:
• [Stat 1 with surprise factor]
• [Stat 2]
• [Stat 3]

The most interesting pattern: [one-sentence insight].

Full data + methodology here: [link to one-pager, not your homepage]

Happy to share the raw dataset, jump on a call, or connect you with
[customer who's relevant].

[your name + 1-line credential]
```

**2. Exclusive launch or milestone**

```
Subject: Exclusive: [specific milestone] at [company]

Hi [name],

I have an exclusive on [milestone] that I think fits your [beat] coverage.

The story: [one sentence]
Why it matters: [one sentence - for their readers, not for you]
What's new: [the actual news, not the marketing line]

Embargo until [day, time, timezone] - would love to give you first
window. Press kit + assets: [link]

Free to talk [two specific time options].

[your name]
```

**3. Op-ed or contributed piece**

```
Subject: Op-ed pitch: [provocative thesis]

Hi [name],

I read your piece on [recent article] - sharp take on [specific point].

I'd like to pitch a 700-word op-ed: "[Thesis as a headline]"

Core argument:
• [Point 1]
• [Point 2]
• [Point 3 - the surprising one]

Why me: [1 sentence - credential or unique vantage]
Why now: [1 sentence - the news hook]

Can have a draft to you by [date]. Happy to adapt to your house style.

[your name]
```

**4. Customer story**

```
Subject: Customer story for [their beat] - [specific outcome]

Hi [name],

For your [beat] coverage, I have a [customer type] willing to talk on
the record about [specific outcome].

The hook: [customer] [did something specific] and [measurable result].

The interesting part: [the surprising or counterintuitive detail].

Customer details:
• Name: [name, title, company]
• Available: [windows]
• Willing to share: [data points / screenshots / metrics]

Happy to coordinate the intro.

[your name]
```

**5. Trend piece or connector**

```
Subject: Trend forming in [space] - three signals

Hi [name],

Three things in [space] this month that I think connect:

1. [Signal 1 with link]
2. [Signal 2 with link]
3. [Signal 3 - yours, briefly]

The pattern: [one sentence].

This might be early for a piece, but if you're tracking the space I
wanted to flag it. Happy to share data we've collected or connect you
with others seeing the same.

[your name]
```

**6. Newsjack response** ([NEWSJACKING.md](NEWSJACKING.md))

```
Subject: Re: [their article headline] - quick data point

Hi [name],

Saw your piece on [story] this morning - wanted to add a relevant
data point in case you do a follow-up.

[One-sentence stat or insight].

Source: [our data / our customers / our analysis]
Methodology: [one sentence]

Quotable: "[a sentence you'd be comfortable seeing in print]"

If useful for a follow-up, I'm around all day at this number: [phone].

[your name]
```

## Subject lines

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] Journalists open pitches on the subject line alone.

- **Under 50 characters:** "mobile preview cuts off". The app-growth guide instead gives "[your angle in 8 words]" after the app name [eronred].
- **Lead with the specific:** a concrete number or fact beats "New data on [topic]".
- **Promise a story, not a product:** "Why [trend]" beats "[Company] launches [thing]".
- **Prefixes that signal value:** "Exclusive:", "Data:", "Op-ed pitch:", "Re: [their article]".
- **Patterns that work:** "[Specific stat] - [implication]"; "Exclusive: [milestone]"; "Re: [their headline]"; "[Provocative thesis]".
- **Patterns that get deleted:** "Press release: [boring]"; "[Company] announces [thing]"; "Story idea for you!"; "Following up on my previous email"; any subject with "innovative," "disruptive," "revolutionary".
- Test: would *you* open this in a 200-email inbox?

## Length, structure and voice

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations]

- **Length:** "150 words max for the pitch. If you can't say it in 150 words, you don't know what your story is yet." The app-growth guide says "5 sentences max" [eronred]; the two agree in spirit.
- **Structure:** one-line context (why them: their recent article or beat); the story in one sentence; why it matters to their readers, not to you; proof (data, customer, quote, link); the ask (interview, embargo, quote, link).
- **Voice:** sound like a person, not a press release; reference their actual recent work; "Don't use emoji unless they do"; "Don't open with 'I hope this finds you well', burn it."
- **Banned words:** the source bans "Revolutionary, disruptive, game-changing, paradigm shift, leverage, synergy, robust, seamless, holistic, world-class, best-in-class, next-generation, cutting-edge, AI-powered (unless that's the actual differentiation), at-the-end-of-the-day." Most are already on the ban and cap lists in [anti-slop-writing TELLS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/anti-ai-slop/anti-slop-writing/TELLS.md#3-vocabulary); in a pitch, treat every word on the source's list as banned.

## Embargoes and exclusives

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations]

- **Embargo** ("you can write this story, but don't publish until [time]"): offer it only to journalists you've worked with or who have strong reputations for honoring embargoes; state day, time and timezone; "If they break embargo, your relationship with them is over."
- **Exclusive** ("only you get this story"): first-tier outlets only, since exclusives to tier 2 or 3 outlets "waste the lever"; be honest about scope ("exclusive to [outlet] in the US" is fine); have a parallel plan for what you publish or pitch the day after it runs.
- The app-growth guide adds for app launches: contact 1 to 2 weeks before, publish on launch day, one journalist per tier, never the same exclusive to two competing outlets [eronred]. A Product Hunt launch can be timed to the embargo lift ([product-launch PRODUCT-HUNT.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/product-launch/PRODUCT-HUNT.md#day-time-and-scheduling)).

## Follow-up

The two guides differ; choose one cadence per campaign:

- [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] **Day 0** initial pitch; **day 3** one follow-up if you have new information ("Just talked to [customer] who can join us"); **day 7** a final check-in with a fresh hook ("This came out today, still relevant?"); **after day 7** let it go and re-pitch only with something new. Never "Bumping this up" or "Did you see my email?", never silent follow-ups with no new value, never the same pitch reformatted.
- [eronred, ../../mobile/app-growth/LAUNCH-AND-MARKETING.md] "follow up once after 5 business days, then move on."

Both cap follow-ups.

## Pitch killers

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations]

- Wrong name or wrong outlet (a mail-merge failure).
- Pitching topics they explicitly don't cover.
- "Press release attached as PDF" (paste the key bits).
- A long signature with logos and disclaimers.
- CC'ing 5 other journalists on the same email.
- "Per my last email" energy.
- Asking them to sign an NDA before talking.
- Pitching a story you can't actually tell (no customer willing to talk, no data ready to share).

## Finding contacts

- **Use the address the journalist or outlet published** (author page, Muck Rack, About page) or a verification tool such as Hunter.io or RocketReach. The source also suggests to "just guess and bounce-check" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations], and the app-growth guide gives the pattern "first@publication.com" [eronred]; don't send to guessed addresses, because hard bounces damage sender reputation ([email-practices DELIVERABILITY.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/backend/email-practices/DELIVERABILITY.md#bounces-and-complaints)).
- **A media list of named journalists with emails is personal data** under the GDPR, which defines it as "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person" (Art. 4(1), https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/). Record where each address came from, honor "no pitches" and removal requests, and follow [email-practices COMPLIANCE.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/backend/email-practices/COMPLIANCE.md) for the law that applies; the source does not say whether one-to-one press pitches fall under CAN-SPAM.
- **Researching recent work:** read a handful of public author pages and profiles within each site's terms; the source's "persistent session, no rate limits" browser approach is not carried over. Driving a browser is [browser-automation SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/web-automation/browser-automation/SKILL.md).

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations]

```
JOURNALIST PROFILE - [name]
Outlet: [name]
Beat: [topics from last 5 articles]
Recent angle: [pattern you noticed]
Recent X activity: [what they're posting]
Score: [weighted total out of 80; the source's template says X/40]
Best pitch angle: [from template library]
Email: [confirmed, and where it was published]
```

Keep the list in `.agents/media-list.md` (or a CSV) and update it monthly: "journalists move jobs constantly."

```markdown
## Tier 1 (top 20)
| Name | Outlet | Beat | Last contact | Last coverage | Email | Address source | Score |
|------|--------|------|--------------|---------------|-------|----------------|-------|
```

Track each pitch: date sent, subject line, journalist, outlet, response (open, reply, pass, coverage), what they said. "After 30 pitches, you'll see which subject patterns and which angles work for you specifically."

## Outlets

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, public-relations] The source ships a long outlet list; it is left out here because "This list ages fast" and some entries were not checked against the outlets' own sites. Its rules for any list:

- "Don't pitch a publication. Pitch a journalist at that publication."
- Tier signals quality, not effort: a small outlet may be perfect for a niche. Don't overlook vertical trade press ("smaller audience, much higher intent"), or local business journals and regional tech news when you have a regional angle.
- "a journalist's email beats a tip form every time".
- Verify the outlet still exists and the journalist is still there before pitching.
- **Not press:** Product Hunt, BetaList and Indie Hackers are directories ([product-launch PRODUCT-HUNT.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/product-launch/PRODUCT-HUNT.md), [directory submissions](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/seo/DIRECTORY-SUBMISSIONS.md)); press release wires are "overpriced for early-stage; journalists ignore them. Skip until you have IR / SEC requirements"; paid "As featured in" badge mills are "Worthless and damaging"; guest-post link networks are link spam under Google's spam policies (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies; links in [seo SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/seo/SKILL.md)).

The app-growth guide's outlet tiers for apps are in [app-growth LAUNCH-AND-MARKETING.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/app-growth/LAUNCH-AND-MARKETING.md#press-and-pr) [eronred].

## Prove it

- The list holds 20 to 40 journalists, each with their last 5 articles read, a weighted score and tier, and the published source of their address; no one whose bio says "no PR pitches" is on it.
- Each pitch uses one template, is under 150 words (or 5 sentences), has a subject line under 50 characters with no deleted pattern, opens with why this journalist, and makes one clear ask.
- Embargoes go only to journalists with a record of honoring them and state day, time and timezone; an exclusive goes to one first-tier outlet.
- Follow-ups follow one chosen cadence, each carries new information, and none says "bumping this up".
- No pitch went to a guessed address or carried a PDF attachment, an NDA ask or a CC list.
