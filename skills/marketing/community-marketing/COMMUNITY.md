## Ask first

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, community-marketing]

1. **The product or brand:** what problem it solves, who uses it.
2. **The platform(s) in play:** Discord, Slack, Circle, Reddit, Facebook Groups, a forum.
3. **The stage:** pre-launch, 0 to 100 members, 100 to 1k, scaling, established.
4. **The primary goal:** retention, activation, word of mouth, support deflection, product feedback, revenue.
5. **The ideal member:** role, motivation, what they hope to get from joining.

Also: do you have existing users to seed from, and how much time a week can go into community management? Work with what's available; state the assumptions you make.

## Principles

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, community-marketing]

- **Build around a shared identity, not just a product:** who members *are* or aspire to be (the source's examples: Indie Hackers for bootstrapped founders, r/homelab for tinkerers who self-host, the Figma community for designers who care about craft). Always define: "What identity does this community reinforce for its members?"
- **Value flows to members first:** exclusive knowledge or early access; peer connections they can't get elsewhere; recognition and status in a group they respect; direct influence on the roadmap; career opportunities, visibility, credibility.
- **Design for the flywheel from day one:**

```
Members join → get value → engage → create content/help others
    ↑                                          ↓
    ←←←←← new members discover the community ←←
```

"Every decision should ask: *Does this accelerate the loop or slow it down?*"

## Launching from zero

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, community-marketing]

1. **Recruit 20 to 50 founding members manually:** DM your most engaged users, beta testers or fans. "Don't open publicly until there is baseline activity."
2. **Set the culture explicitly:** guidelines that describe the *vibe*, not just the rules.
3. **Seed conversations before launch:** 5 to 10 posts that model the behavior you want (questions, wins, resources).
4. **Do things that don't scale:** reply to every post, welcome every new member by name, host a weekly call. "You are buying social proof."
5. **Define the core loop:** the action you want members to take weekly; make it easy and reward it publicly.

## Growing an existing community

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, community-marketing]

1. **Audit where members drop off:** joining but not posting, or posting once and disappearing.
2. **Create a new-member journey:** a pinned welcome post, an #introduce-yourself channel, a DM or email from a community manager, a clear "start here" path.
3. **Surface member wins publicly:** projects, testimonials, milestones.
4. **Run recurring rituals:** weekly threads ("What are you working on?"), monthly AMAs, seasonal challenges.
5. **Invest in power users:** recognition, early access, moderator roles, direct product input. The source's "1% of members generate 90% of value" has no cited source.

## Ambassadors

- **The playbook** [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, community-marketing]:
  1. **Find candidates** who already recommend you unprompted: reviews, social mentions, community posts.
  2. **Ask personally,** 1:1, saying why you chose them; not a generic form.
  3. **Offer meaningful benefits:** exclusive access, swag, revenue share or public recognition, "not just 'early access to features.'"
  4. **Give them tools:** referral links, shareable assets, key talking points, a private channel.
  5. **Measure** referral traffic, signups and engagement driven by advocates, and double down on what works.
- **They disclose what they get.** The source leaves this out. FTC: "#ambassador" or "partner" alone "is ambiguous and confusing", while "#XYZ_Ambassador" or "#XYZ_Partner" "will likely be more understandable"; "Gifted" alone is ambiguous, and "Gifted by XYZ" is enough for a free product. The brand shares responsibility ("the ultimate responsibility ... rests with the influencer and the brand ... not the platform"), so add your own disclosure instruction even when a platform offers a tool, and monitor the people you have a deal with during the deal and "a reasonable time, such as a few months" after it. [https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking] The full disclosure rules are [creator-marketing DISCLOSURE.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/creator-marketing/DISCLOSURE.md); referral rewards are [referral-programs SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/referral-programs/SKILL.md).
- The book's benchmark for an ambassador program at scale is Notion: "300+ ambassadors", "1M+ template downloads", "25% of new users come from community referrals". The source itself says to use these "as the 'what great looks like' north star ... not as day-one targets".

## Community-led support

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, community-marketing]

1. **Build a searchable knowledge base** from the top community questions.
2. **Recognize members who help:** "Community Expert" badges, leaderboards, shoutouts.
3. **Close the loop with product:** when community feedback drives a change, announce it publicly and credit the members who raised it ([product-launch LAUNCH-PLAN.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/product-launch/LAUNCH-PLAN.md#ongoing-launches)).
4. **Monitor sentiment weekly** for patterns in complaints or confusion before they become churn signals ([churn-prevention SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/churn-prevention/SKILL.md)).

On a free Slack plan, history is visible for 90 days and content older than a year is deleted: see the Slack row in [Platforms](#platforms).

## Models

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, community-marketing] Pick the model that matches the primary goal; most communities lean on one and borrow from others. The source calls these "the 5 community models" but lists four, with the blend as the fifth.

| Model | Best when | Goal | The book's case |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Support-driven** | Support volume is high, questions repeat, peers answer faster than your team | Support deflection | GreenPal's Facebook group |
| **Product-development** | Users are technical or opinionated and their feedback shapes the roadmap | Feedback and build-in-public momentum | Ydata's Slack, driving GitHub stars and feedback |
| **Education and enablement** | The product has a learning curve and retention hinges on members getting good at it | Churn reduction | LiveAgent |
| **Founder-led** | You're early, the founder's voice is the brand, personal presence builds trust fastest | Direct relationships and word of mouth | Bento (Discord), Postaga |

"The fifth 'model' in practice is the blend: a community usually starts **Founder-Led**, then specializes toward Support-Driven, Product-Development, or Education as it scales."

## The role shift

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, community-marketing] "Running a 1,000-member community like a 50-member one (or vice versa) is the most common failure."

| Phase | Members | Role | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | 0 to 100 | **Community Architect** | Lay the foundation and build relationships: do things that don't scale, know members by name, set the culture |
| Systems | 100 to 1,000 | **Community Manager** | Systems and governance: rituals, moderation, onboarding paths, clear norms so activity survives without you in every thread |
| Scale | 1,000+ | **Community Enabler** | Enable others: ambassador programs and sub-communities that members and leaders run |

"Architect the *room*, manage the *systems*, enable the *people*."

## Platforms

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, community-marketing]

| Platform | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Discord | Developer, gaming and creator communities; real-time chat | High noise, hard to search, onboarding friction |
| Slack | B2B and professional communities; familiar to SaaS buyers | Feels like work. Free plan: "90-day access" to message and file history; "Messages and files on the Free plan will only be stored for one year. Any content in your workspace more than a year old will be permanently deleted"; huddles and external collaboration are "One-to-one only" ([Slack](https://slack.com/help/articles/7050776459923)) |
| Circle | Creator or course-based communities; clean UX | Less organic discovery; you must drive traffic |
| Reddit | High-volume public communities; SEO benefit | You don't own it; moderation is hard |
| Facebook Groups | Consumer brands; older demographics | Declining organic reach; algorithm dependent |
| Forum (Discourse) | Long-form technical communities; SEO-rich | Slower velocity; higher effort to post |

In subreddits you don't run, "contribute before promoting" [eronred, ../../mobile/app-growth/LAUNCH-AND-MARKETING.md]; social posting is [social-media SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/social-media/SKILL.md).

## Health metrics

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, community-marketing] Read weekly:

- **DAU/MAU:** stickiness. The source calls above 20% healthy "for most communities", with no cited source.
- **New-member post rate:** the share of new members who post within 7 days of joining.
- **Thread reply rate:** the share of posts with at least one reply.
- **Lurker ratio:** members who joined but haven't posted in 30+ days.
- **Non-staff content:** the share of posts not written by the company team.

**Warning signs:** most posts are from the company team; questions go unanswered for more than 24 hours; the same 5 people account for 80%+ of engagement; new members stop posting after their intro.

If the community emits events into product analytics, these can be computed there ([posthog SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/monitoring/posthog/SKILL.md)).

## Outputs

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, community-marketing] Produce one of: a **community strategy doc** (platform, identity, core loop, 90-day launch plan); a **channel architecture** (channels or categories with purpose and posting guidelines); a **new-member journey** (pinned post, DM template, first-week prompts); a **ritual calendar** (weekly and monthly events and threads); an **ambassador program brief** (criteria, benefits, outreach template, disclosure wording, tracking plan); a **health audit** (current metrics, diagnosis, the top 3 priorities). "Generic advice ('be consistent,' 'provide value') is not useful. Give the user something they can act on today."

## Prove it

- The strategy names the identity the community reinforces, the model, the stage and its role, and the weekly core loop.
- A new community had 20 to 50 hand-recruited founding members and 5 to 10 seeded posts before opening.
- The ambassador brief tells every ambassador to disclose with the brand's name in the tag, and says how and for how long they are monitored.
- A support-led community isn't on a free Slack plan that deletes history older than a year.
- The weekly health read reports all five metrics and checks every warning sign.
