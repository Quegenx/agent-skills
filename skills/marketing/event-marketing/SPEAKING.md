# Speaking

Get booked, design a talk that lands, and harvest the recording, copied from coreyhaines31/marketingskills `events` (MIT), `references/speaking.md`. Its outline-then-storyboard method is "distilled from Knowatoa's `talk-storyboard` and `presentation-outline` skills" (https://github.com/Knowatoa/ai-visibility-skills, MIT, credited). "Stage time confers borrowed authority no booth can buy, and the recording compounds for years."

## Getting booked

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] Organizers optimize for their audience's experience, not your reach.

- **Pitch the audience's takeaway, not your company.** A CFP that reads like the attendee's problem gets accepted; a product story gets filtered. The product appears inside the talk only as evidence.
- **Title formula:** a specific outcome, a specific audience, and a tension or number. The source's example: "How we cut CAC 40% by killing our best channel" beats "Rethinking Growth."
- **The abstract carries three things:** the problem as the audience feels it; 2 to 3 concrete things they'll walk away knowing; why *you* (numbers, scars). Keep it under 150 words: "organizers skim hundreds."
- **Ladder:** local meetups, then niche conference tracks, then main stages. Recordings of small talks are the portfolio for bigger ones; podcast appearances feed the same ladder ([public-relations PODCAST-PREP.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/public-relations/PODCAST-PREP.md)).
- **Off-cycle:** many events fill panels and replacement slots late, so a short note to organizers with a tight topic and proof you can speak "lands surprisingly often."

## Designing the talk

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] "A talk is a journey you take the room on, not a document you read at them." Two passes.

**Pass 1, the outline.** Before slides, write down:

1. **Who it's for:** one person, their situation, what they believe walking in.
2. **The Monday takeaway:** what they can do or decide on a specific next day; "a talk without one is content, not a talk".
3. **What you get:** your win (pipeline, credibility, hiring), so the close can carry it without a swerve.
4. **The blocks:** each with a point and a proof (a story or a number). "No proof, no block."

**Pass 2, storyboard the feeling.** A beat is a change in energy or emotion, not a slide heading; a 20-minute talk is usually 5 to 8 beats. For each beat name:

- **Energy:** in directable words: quiet lean-in, rising unease, laugh-release, peak, still.
- **Feel:** in the audience's own words: "that's me", "wait, we're the problem", "I can try this Monday".
- **The hit:** the one thing the beat must land, in one sentence.

Then check the journey: does the sequence of feelings *earn* the Monday takeaway, or is it only stated at the end? Cut or merge a block that produces no change in feeling ("two blocks that feel the same are one beat"); if the close needs a feeling that never appeared, the outline isn't done. "One well-placed contrarian take stands out most on stages that have converged on a consensus."

## The recording

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] "The room holds 200 people for 25 minutes; the recording works for years."

- **Say important things in liftable form:** your company name next to your category ("we build X, the Y for Z"), numbers spoken aloud rather than shown on a slide, and the quotable line "on a beat the room will feel". The source holds that transcripts get "crawled and cited by AI assistants"; that is its claim ([ai-search-visibility SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/ai-search-visibility/SKILL.md)).
- **"Slides are not the asset."** Anything only visual (the key number, the framework name) doesn't exist for the transcript, the podcast version, or an attendee retelling it: speak it.
- **Get the recording:** confirm before accepting the slot that you'll receive it and may republish. If the event doesn't record, record your own re-delivery within a week.

## Around the talk

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events]

- **Before:** post the "why this topic now" angle; invite specific people to the session; plan to meet the other speakers ("speaker-to-speaker is the strongest networking lane at any event").
- **During:** end with one low-friction pointer, a memorable URL to the slides plus a related asset, which doubles as a lead magnet ([lead-magnets SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/lead-magnets/SKILL.md)). Note every Q&A question: it is content research.
- **After:** publish the recording and a written version ([content-strategy SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/content-strategy/SKILL.md)), clip the peak beats ([marketing-video SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/videos/marketing-video/SKILL.md)), and follow up within 24 to 48 hours with everyone who asked a question or approached you: "the warmest leads an event produces".
- **Roll the talk forward:** the same core talk, sharpened by each Q&A, can run a season. "Retire it when the Q&A stops surprising you."

## Prove it

- The CFP title has an outcome, an audience and a tension or number; the abstract is under 150 words with the problem, 2 to 3 takeaways and proof.
- The outline names one person, a Monday takeaway, your win, and blocks that each have a proof.
- The storyboard has 5 to 8 beats for 20 minutes, each with energy, feel and hit, and the feelings lead to the takeaway.
- The key number and the company-plus-category line are spoken, not only on a slide.
- Recording and republishing rights were confirmed before accepting, and every questioner got a follow-up within 48 hours.
