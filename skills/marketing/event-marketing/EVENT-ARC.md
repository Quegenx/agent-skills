## Before

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] Where pipeline is actually made.

- **Build the target list:** who's attending that matches your ICP (attendee lists, speaker lists, "who's going" posts, past-year attendees). Researching a speaker list is [web-research EVENTS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/web-research/EVENTS.md).
- **Book meetings before you arrive:** "A meeting booked two weeks out is worth ten hopeful hallway collisions." The ask is specific, low-friction and time-boxed ("15 min at the coffee bar Tuesday"). The outreach email follows [cold-outreach SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/cold-outreach/SKILL.md) and the law in [SPONSORSHIP.md](SPONSORSHIP.md#lawful-follow-up).
- **Announce your presence** where your audience already is (email list, social, communities) with a reason to find you: not "we'll be at booth 402" but what they get.

## During

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events]

- **Optimize for qualified conversations, not contacts.** "One real conversation with an ICP buyer beats fifty badge scans."
- **Capture context:** after each conversation, record what they said, what they care about and the agreed next step. "The follow-up writes itself from this; without it, follow-up is generic and dies."
- **Create content while there:** the event is "a recording studio you already paid for" ([The content arc](#the-content-arc)).

## After

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] Where pipeline is won or lost.

- **The 24 to 48 hour window:** follow up while the conversation is warm, referencing what was actually discussed. The source's claim that "Every day of delay roughly halves response rates" is, in its own words, "directional, not a law".
- **Tier it:** hot gets a personal note and a concrete next step, warm a relevant asset tied to their stated problem, scan-only one light touch or nothing ("don't burn your domain on people who don't remember you"); the tiers in full, and where a scan-only touch is lawful, are in [SPONSORSHIP.md](SPONSORSHIP.md#follow-up).
- **Route to systems:** the CRM with event source tags ([revops SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/revops/SKILL.md)); the not-nows to nurture email ([email-marketing](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/email-marketing/SKILL.md)).

## Hosting formats

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events]

| Format | Best for | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Webinar** | Lead gen and education at scale | Low to mid | The flagship repeatable format: [WEBINAR-FUNNEL.md](WEBINAR-FUNNEL.md) |
| **Workshop** | Product-qualified leads, activation | Mid | Hands-on beats presentation for conversion; smaller and deeper than a webinar |
| **Dinner or small gathering** | Exec relationships, ABM accounts | Mid | 8 to 14 seats, no pitch, a curated guest mix; the source calls it "the highest meetings-per-dollar format in B2B" |
| **Meetup series** | Local community, recurring presence | Mid | Consistency beats production value; hand hosting to members over time ([community-marketing COMMUNITY.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/community-marketing/COMMUNITY.md)) |
| **Virtual summit** | List building via partner audiences | High | Multi-speaker means built-in distribution; every speaker promotes ([partnerships PARTNER-MARKETING.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/partnerships/PARTNER-MARKETING.md)) |
| **User conference** | Retention, expansion, category authority | Very high | Not before you have a community "that would attend without being begged" |

Two rules outrank the format:

- **"The topic is the targeting."** "State of [category] [year]" attracts your ICP; "All about [your product]" attracts existing customers only. Pick topics your buyer would attend even if they'd never buy.
- **"Recurring beats one-off."** A monthly webinar or quarterly dinner compounds audiences, promotion muscle and a content library; a single big event evaporates.

## Attending without a booth

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] The zero-budget motion, "and often the best ROI in the building":

1. **Target list first:** 15 to 30 named people, from the attendee and speaker lists and social chatter.
2. **Pre-book** 1 to 3 weeks ahead; the ask is 15 minutes, anchored to a time and place.
3. **The side-event play:** a dinner or breakfast near the conference for 8 to 12 target accounts. You get host status without sponsor pricing, and the source says it "often out-generates a booth at a tenth of the cost" ([SPONSORSHIP.md](SPONSORSHIP.md#the-side-event)).
4. **Work sessions strategically:** go where your targets speak, ask a real question, follow up on it.
5. The same 24 to 48 hour follow-up as every other role.

## The content arc

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events]

- **Record everything you're allowed to record.** "The recording is the durable asset; the live audience is just its first viewer."
- **Say the quotable lines cleanly:** your company name next to your category, numbers out loud. The source holds that published transcripts "get crawled and cited by AI assistants"; that is its claim, not a measured mechanism ([ai-search-visibility SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/ai-search-visibility/SKILL.md)).
- **Slice the recording:** clips ([marketing-video SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/videos/marketing-video/SKILL.md)), a recap post per session ([content-strategy SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/content-strategy/SKILL.md)), pull-quotes for social ([social-media SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/social-media/SKILL.md)), proof points for sales ([sales-enablement SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/sales-enablement/SKILL.md)).
- **Collect social proof:** testimonials captured at the event are "the most natural you'll ever get".

## Measurement

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events]

| Tier | Examples | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Vanity** | Registrations, badge scans, foot traffic, impressions | "Track, never optimize for, never report as success" |
| **Real** | Qualified conversations, meetings booked, opportunities created, pipeline influenced | The actual scoreboard |
| **Decisive** | Cost per qualified meeting, cost per opportunity, closed-won influenced | What decides whether you do it again |

- Compare cost per qualified meeting with your other channels (ads, outbound): the go or no-go math is in [SPONSORSHIP.md](SPONSORSHIP.md#should-we-sponsor).
- Events are multi-touch: use source tags plus self-reported attribution ("heard us at X") and influence windows, and "never claim last-click credit for a deal the event merely touched" ([marketing-attribution SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/marketing-attribution/SKILL.md); a "how did you hear about us" survey is [posthog SURVEYS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/monitoring/posthog/SURVEYS.md)).
- Judge a recurring program on a 2 to 3 event trend: "the first run of anything underperforms its steady state."

## Common mistakes

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events]

- **Sponsoring for "brand awareness" with no conversation target:** "If nobody owns a meetings number, the booth is décor."
- **The follow-up gap:** leads captured, then a first generic touch two weeks later.
- **Optimizing show-up for a topic nobody wants:** reminders can't save weak demand; fix topic and promise first.
- **One-off thinking:** "Budget for the third instance before running the first."
- **Doing the event, skipping the recording.**
- **Counting badge scans as leads:** "A scan is a person who walked slowly." Qualify before it enters the pipeline.
- **Writing off events after one bad conference:** each event is its own ecosystem.
- **Chasing the biggest conferences:** size correlates with noise and audience dilution, not ROI.

## Prove it

- The target list names people, and meetings were booked before the event with a time and place.
- Every conversation in the CRM has a context note and an event source tag.
- Follow-ups went out within 48 hours, matched to their tier, each referencing what was discussed.
- The report puts vanity metrics in the vanity row and leads with qualified meetings, opportunities and cost per qualified meeting compared with another channel; no deal is claimed on last click.
- The recording exists and was sliced into at least one recap and one set of clips or quotes.
