# The webinar funnel

Registration, show-up, live-to-close and nurture, copied from coreyhaines31/marketingskills `events` (MIT), `references/webinar-funnel.md` (its idea credited to alirezarezvani/claude-skills, MIT; "content authored from scratch"). Each stage's rate is a separate lever with separate fixes. Consent for reminders and honest urgency are added from the FCC and FTC.

## Benchmarks

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] The source's "typical B2B ranges", which it calls "directional benchmarks, not targets; your own trend line is the real baseline". It cites no data.

| Stage | Metric | Typical range |
|---|---|---|
| Registration page | Visitor to registrant | 30 to 50% (warm traffic), 10 to 25% (cold) |
| Show-up | Registrant to attendee | 35 to 45% live; lower for cold or ads traffic |
| Hold | Attendee stays past minute 40 | 50 to 70% |
| Convert | Attendee to next step (trial, demo, offer) | 5 to 15% of attendees for a soft CTA; 1 to 5% direct purchase |

## Stage 0: topic and offer

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] Decided before anything else; "The topic does the targeting and most of the selling."

- **A problem-aware topic, not a product topic:** "How [ICP] does X without Y" out-registers "Intro to [Product]".
- **Name the transformation in the title:** a specific outcome and audience, optionally a number or timeframe. "The title *is* the ad": test titles like ad headlines.
- **Decide the offer before writing the content:** the next step for an attendee who loved it (trial, demo, audit, purchase). "A webinar with no decided offer becomes a lecture with an awkward ending."
- "One topic, one promise, one offer. Stack more and every rate drops."

## Stage 1: registration

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events]

**The registration page** is a landing page ([cro SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/cro/SKILL.md), [anti-slop-copy SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/anti-ai-slop/anti-slop-copy/SKILL.md)), with webinar rules:

- Headline is the promise from the title; the subhead says who it's for and what they'll be able to do.
- 3 to 5 "you'll learn" bullets written as outcomes, not agenda items.
- One tight speaker-credibility block.
- Date and time with timezone handling; "can't make it? register anyway for the replay" (replay registrants are real leads).
- A short form: name and email, plus at most one qualifying field.

**Promotion,** starting 2 weeks out, not 6:

- **Email list:** 3 sends: announcement, a value-add reminder with a preview insight, a day-of last call.
- **Social:** founder or host personal posts outperform brand posts; share the "why this topic now" angle ([social-media SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/social-media/SKILL.md)).
- **Partners:** a co-hosted webinar "doubles reach for free", but co-hosted registrant lists need explicit consent for both parties: the form says the registration goes to both companies ([partnerships PARTNER-MARKETING.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/partnerships/PARTNER-MARKETING.md#the-agreement)). When one email promotes both, CAN-SPAM lets them "designate one of the marketers as the 'sender'" ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)).
- **Paid:** only after the topic is proven organically; retargeting warm traffic works, and "cold-to-webinar ads are an expensive way to buy no-shows" ([paid-ads SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/paid-ads/SKILL.md)).
- **Speakers' audiences:** for panels and summits, every speaker promotes; give them pre-written posts and custom links.

## Stage 2: show-up

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] "Registrants are cheap; attendance is the funnel's leakiest joint."

- **Calendar add at registration:** "the single highest-leverage fix".
- **Reminder cadence:** confirmation immediately, with calendar links; a value reminder at T-1 day (tease a specific insight, not "don't forget!"); T-1 hour; **T-5 minutes with the join link**, which the source says "moves attendance more than the rest combined".
- **SMS reminders where consented.** In the US, an autodialed text that is telemarketing needs prior express written consent (47 CFR 64.1200(a)(2), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200); for an informational text, "callers can obtain prior express consent ... either orally or in writing" (FCC, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-393514A1.pdf). Read together, a plain reminder for an event the person registered for looks informational, and one that also promotes an offer looks like telemarketing (this file's reading of the two rules, not an FCC ruling). Current SMS rules and registration are [sms-marketing COMPLIANCE.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/sms-marketing/COMPLIANCE.md).
- **Close the gap between registration and the event:** someone who registered 6 weeks out has forgotten you; add a mid-window touch (a related asset, a poll that shapes the content).
- **Pre-engagement:** ask a question at registration ("what's your biggest challenge with X?") for content input, segmentation and a micro-commitment.
- **Time slot:** mid-week, late morning or early afternoon in the audience's dominant timezone, avoiding Mondays and Fridays; test against your own data.

## Stage 3: live to close

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] Sell without being salesy:

1. **Open (0 to 5 minutes):** restate the promise, preview the payoff, and say the offer is coming: "at the end I'll show how we do this, first, the practice you can use regardless". "Naming the pitch upfront *removes* the salesy feeling; the ambush is what people hate."
2. **Content (5 to 35 minutes):** teach the real thing, as 3 teachable points, each with a proof (story, number, live example); use questions and polls to keep hold up.
3. **The transition (1 minute, scripted word for word):** "everything I showed you can be done manually, here's what it looks like when [product] does it for you." The product enters as the implementation of the content, not a topic change.
4. **Offer (5 to 8 minutes):** one offer: what they get, what it costs or what the next step is, and a real reason for now (an expiring bonus, a cohort start, limited seats). "Never fake scarcity." The FTC lists baseless countdown timers, false low-stock and false high-demand messages as dark patterns ([FTC, Bringing Dark Patterns to Light](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf)); offer, deadline and scarcity rules are [offer-design FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/offer-design/FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md).
5. **Q&A (10+ minutes):** "questions are objections in disguise". Seed 2 to 3 starter questions, answer the objection behind each, and restate the offer and link once mid-Q&A and once at the close.

**Hold mechanics:** deliver at about minute 35 on a promise announced in minute 1; a pattern break about every 7 minutes (poll, story, screen change); never front-load housekeeping.

## Stage 4: after the webinar

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] "Half the revenue is here." Segment by behavior, then sequence ([email-marketing](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/email-marketing/SKILL.md)):

| Segment | Play |
|---|---|
| **Attended, engaged** (stayed for the offer, asked questions) | A personal follow-up within 24h referencing their question; a direct next step |
| **Attended, left early** | The replay with a timestamp to what they missed; a softer CTA |
| **No-show** | "Sorry we missed you" plus the replay with a deadline: a 2 to 3 email replay sequence |
| **Replay registrants** | The no-show sequence without the apology |

- **Replay:** time-limited (72 hours to 1 week) for offers and launches; evergreen for education-led capture, where it becomes a lead magnet ([lead-magnets SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/lead-magnets/SKILL.md)).
- **Offer close:** if the offer had a deadline, run a real close sequence (deadline reminder, objection email, final hours). "All urgency claims must be true": the deadline ends when it says.
- **Recycle:** transcript to a recap post ([content-strategy SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/content-strategy/SKILL.md)), clips ([marketing-video SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/videos/marketing-video/SKILL.md)), quotable stats.

## Diagnostics

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events]

- **Low registration:** a topic, title or promise problem (or traffic quality). Fix the webinar's own offer before adding promotion.
- **Low show-up** (under 30%): the reminder system or the registration-to-event gap; check the calendar-add rate first.
- **Low hold:** front-loaded content or a promise mismatch; find the drop-off timestamp.
- **High hold, low conversion:** a transition or offer problem: "the audience liked the class but wasn't shown a reason to act."
- Cost per qualified attendee and per opportunity, compared with other channels, decide the program's future.

## Prove it

- The title states a problem-aware outcome for a named audience, and the offer was written before the content.
- The registration form asks for name, email and at most one more field; a co-hosted form says both companies receive the registration.
- The reminder sequence includes the calendar add and a T-5 minute join link; SMS reminders go only to people with the consent their content needs.
- The live script has the upfront offer mention, 3 points with proofs, a word-for-word transition and one offer whose reason for now is true.
- Each post-webinar segment has its own sequence, and every deadline in it is real.
- A low rate is traced to its stage before any fix.
