# Sponsoring and exhibiting

Evaluate, negotiate, work the floor and follow up, copied from coreyhaines31/marketingskills `events` (MIT), `references/sponsorship-roi.md`. "Treat every sponsorship as a paid-acquisition channel with a cost-per-qualified-meeting, and make it beat your alternatives or don't buy it." The consent conditions for following up scans come from the FTC, the CASL statute and the email skill.

## Should we sponsor

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] Run this before reading the prospectus pricing:

1. **ICP overlap in absolute numbers.** Ask organizers for the attendee breakdown by role and company type; check last year's attendee and speaker lists and social chatter. "A 5,000-person event with 200 ICP attendees is a 200-person event for you."
2. **Do the meetings math backwards.** Expected qualified conversations are a small fraction of ICP attendees (the source's directional figure: a well-worked booth might turn 10 to 20% of relevant walk-bys into real conversations, "varies wildly by event"). Then:

   **cost per qualified meeting = total cost (sponsorship + travel + staff time + booth build) ÷ expected qualified meetings**

   Compare it with what a meeting costs from outbound or ads. "If the event is 3x your outbound cost with no strategic upside, pass."
3. **Strategic multipliers** that justify a premium: your exact buyers concentrated nowhere else; a category-defining event where absence is conspicuous (late stage); access you can't buy elsewhere (execs who ignore cold outreach).
4. **The counterfactual:** what would the same budget produce in your best channel? "Sponsorship must beat that, not zero."

**Prospectus red flags:** attendee counts without composition; "impressions" as the headline metric; leads defined as badge scans; last year's sponsor logos heavy on companies that didn't return.

## Negotiation

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] "Sponsorship pricing is soft, especially inside 8 weeks." Negotiate in this order of value:

1. **A speaking or panel slot:** worth more than a bigger booth ([SPEAKING.md](SPEAKING.md)).
2. **Side-event rights:** space or permission to host a dinner, breakfast or workshop for a curated list.
3. **What attendee data you really get:** full lists are increasingly rare for privacy reasons, so negotiate for opt-in scans, a question on the registration page, or sponsored-session registrant lists, and "Get what's actually deliverable in writing."
4. **Placement and timing:** near traffic (coffee, entrances, the main stage exit); demo-day timing if there is one.
5. **Price,** last, after the package is right: "Unsold inventory close to the date discounts heavily."

## The side event

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] Often better than the booth: skip or downgrade the booth and host a curated dinner or breakfast next to the conference.

- 8 to 14 seats: hand-picked ICP attendees plus "a couple of magnetic guests" (a respected practitioner draws acceptances).
- Personal invitations 2 to 4 weeks out; the source's line is "join 10 [role]s for dinner during [event]". Each invitation is one-to-one and lawful ([Lawful follow-up](#lawful-follow-up)).
- No pitch: "The host halo and the conversations are the product"; the follow-up carries the commercial weight.
- It costs a fraction of a mid-tier sponsorship, and works when you can't sponsor at all: "you don't need the event's permission for your own dinner across the street."

## Working the booth

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events]

- **Staff it with people who can qualify and demo:** "Two energetic people beat five tired ones"; write a shift schedule.
- **A 30-second qualifying question beats a pitch:** "what does your team use for X today?" Have a graceful fast exit for non-ICP traffic.
- **Capture context, not just scans:** after every real conversation, 15 seconds of notes (what they said, what they care about, the next step), the same hour, by voice memo or CRM app. "A bare badge scan is a name with amnesia."
- **One message on the booth** (the category problem, not your feature list), demo stations for depth, and a book-a-meeting QR code that goes to a calendar, not a form.
- **Pre-book meetings:** the booth is a venue for booked meetings, not just a net for walk-bys.

## Follow-up

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events] A 24 to 48 hour SLA, tiered:

- **Hot** (real conversation, next step agreed): a personal email referencing the conversation, with a calendar link, the same or next day.
- **Warm** (conversation, no commitment): a personal note plus one asset matched to what they said.
- **Scan-only:** one light "we were both at [event]" touch, or nothing. "Never dump scans into a sales sequence": it "burns domain reputation and brand on people who don't remember you" ([email-practices DELIVERABILITY.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/backend/email-practices/DELIVERABILITY.md#reputation-and-thresholds)).
- Whoever worked the booth writes or reviews the follow-up.
- The not-nows go to nurture with event source tags; routing and scoring are [revops SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/revops/SKILL.md).

## Lawful follow-up

The source's scan-only touch assumes the US or Canada. Where the recipient is decides:

- **US:** CAN-SPAM is opt-out, covers every commercial message, and "makes no exception for business-to-business email": accurate headers, a physical postal address and a working opt-out in every follow-up. [https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business]
- **Canada:** CASL s.10(9)(c) implies consent when the person "has disclosed, to the person who sends the message ... the electronic address ... without indicating a wish not to receive" messages, and "the message is relevant to the person's business, role, functions or duties"; s.10(9)(b) covers an address the person conspicuously published. A card handed to you, or a scan the person agreed to, fits only for a message about their role. [https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-2.html]
- **EU and UK:** this repo's email rule is explicit, recorded consent for marketing, following the strictest applicable law ([email-practices COMPLIANCE.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/backend/email-practices/COMPLIANCE.md#cold-and-unsolicited-email), which also holds the CASL 10(9) and CAN-SPAM business-to-business rules). Without that consent, a scan-only contact there gets nothing.

The repo rule and the source agree on the hot and warm tiers (a real conversation and a personal reply); they differ only on the scan-only touch, which is lawful in the US and Canada under the conditions above.

## Measuring it

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, events]

- Tag every touched contact with the event source and measure **qualified conversations, meetings, opportunities, pipeline, closed-won influenced**, on an influence window matching your sales cycle (the source: "90 days is common for B2B; long cycles need longer windows", with no data given).
- Report **cost per qualified meeting and cost per opportunity** against your other channels: "this is the renewal decision for next year".
- Self-reported attribution ("met you at [event]") catches what source tags miss ([marketing-attribution SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/marketing-attribution/SKILL.md)); badge-scan counts and booth traffic are activity, never results.
- Judge a first-time event at a discount: "A promising-but-unprofitable first year is a redesign signal, not necessarily a no."

## Prove it

- The decision memo states ICP attendees in absolute numbers, the cost per qualified meeting with every cost included, the same figure for outbound or ads, and the counterfactual channel.
- The signed package lists what attendee data is deliverable, in writing, and the negotiation asked for a speaking slot and side-event rights before price.
- Every booth conversation has a same-hour note; the QR code opens a calendar.
- Every follow-up went out within 48 hours by tier; no scan entered a sales sequence; each message carries a postal address and opt-out, Canadian scan-only touches relate to the person's role, and EU or UK contacts without consent got nothing.
- The renewal report uses qualified meetings and cost per opportunity, not scans.
