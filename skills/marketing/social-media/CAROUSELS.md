# Carousels

Five slide-by-slide narrative architectures for Instagram carousels and LinkedIn document posts. Each is a different reason to keep swiping; picking the right one matters more than polishing slides. Text is from coreyhaines31/marketingskills `social` (`references/carousel-frameworks.md`, MIT), with platform limits checked against Instagram's and LinkedIn's docs. "A carousel is not a blog post chopped into squares": each slide delivers one idea and makes the next swipe irresistible. Treat the frameworks as starting structures and validate against your own analytics. Making the slide images is in [marketing-images SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/marketing-images/SKILL.md).

## Picking a framework

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, social]`

| Your content is... | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A list of resources, tools or tips | **A: Value-Stack** | Completeness is the promise; the count is the hook |
| A personal result with a system behind it | **B: Problem-Proof** | Proof opens and closes the loop; the system is the meat |
| Several named techniques on one theme | **C: Hack List** | Each hack re-earns the swipe independently |
| A strong opinion about a common practice | **D: Rant Callout** | Conviction is the content; structure keeps it fair |
| A product or workflow you can show | **E: Demo Walkthrough** | Seeing the steps is more persuasive than describing them |

Two rules across frameworks:

- **Slide 1 is the thumbnail.** It competes in the feed alone, before anyone knows a carousel follows. Write it as a standalone scroll-stopper.
- **One visual template per carousel.** Same layout, type scale and palette on every interior slide; consistency lets the content change while the frame stays still.

## A: Value-Stack

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, social]` 4 to 14 slides. The "everything you need" carousel: the cover makes a completeness claim (an exact count and an exact deliverable) and every swipe pays it down.

| Slide | Job | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 1, cover | State the exact count and exact deliverable | "[N] [resource type] for [role/outcome]" |
| 2 to N−1, value | One item or category per slide, 3 to 6 concrete sub-items each; same template, zero filler | "[Category/item]: [sub-items]" |
| N, close | Convert the swipe-through into an action | "[Action] for [the payoff]": comment a keyword, follow, link in bio |

A number is a checkable promise: "27 free tools" outperforms "the best free tools" because the reader can verify you delivered. Example: "12 ChatGPT prompts for SaaS onboarding emails", one prompt per slide with the use case, then "Comment PROMPTS and I'll send the full doc." **Failure mode:** filler slides to hit a bigger number; "a tight 8 beats a padded 14."

## B: Problem-Proof

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, social]` 6 to 10 slides. "I did X, here's the system": the hook is a result stated as fact, and the final slide shows the receipt.

| Slide | Job | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 1, hook | A specific personal claim with a number; a result, not a tip | "[I/we did X]. [Specific result, with number]." |
| 2, reframe | Name what's actually going wrong so the reader recognizes themselves | "The real problem: [named issue]" |
| 3 to 4, mechanism | The real system: named tools, named steps | "The system: [named tools/steps]" |
| 5 to N−1, detail | The literal prompt, template or step-by-step; the save-worthy part | "[The literal prompt or process detail]" |
| N, proof | The actual output: a screenshot, not a description | "[Screenshot of output]: the receipt" |

Slide 1 makes a claim, the last slide proves it, everything between explains how. Example: "We cut churn 22% with one onboarding email", then the real problem (activation, not price), the sequence structure, the literal email copy, the retention chart. **Failure mode:** vague mechanism slides; if the middle doesn't name tools and steps, the proof reads as luck. The numbers on the proof slide are yours and real.

## C: Hack List

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, social]` 6 to 10 slides. A contrarian cover implies most people do this wrong; each numbered hack re-earns the swipe.

| Slide | Job | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 1, contrarian hook | A stat or claim implying most people fail at this | "[Stat implying most people fail at X]" |
| 2, the problem | Why the common approach fails, ideally with an analogy | "The problem: [why it fails]" |
| 3 to N−1, hacks | One named technique per slide; a bad-vs-good contrast makes each land | "Hack #[n]: [named technique]" |
| N, synthesis and close | One line tying the hacks together, then a save, share or follow CTA | "[Thesis tying hacks together]" + [CTA] |

Name every technique: "The 3-second rule" is shareable; "keep it short" is not. Example: "90% of trial emails never get opened", then why (they read like receipts), "Hack #1: The founder-from line", "Hack #2: The one-question subject", then the thesis and "Save this for your next sequence." **Failure mode:** hacks that restate each other; merge them and go shorter.

## D: Rant Callout

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, social]` 4 to 8 slides. Personality-led and polarizing; the structure keeps the heat fair so it reads as conviction.

| Slide | Job | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 1, provocative claim | An unpopular opinion or direct accusation about a common practice | "Unpopular opinion: [common practice]" |
| 2 to N−2, escalate | Sensory, specific detail; each slide raises the stakes | "[Escalating, specific complaint]" |
| N−1, fairness pivot | "Don't get me wrong…": what you're not attacking | "I use [X] every day. My problem is [Y]." |
| N, close | A firm, signed sign-off | "[Firm sign-off]: signed [name/persona]" |

"The fairness pivot is what makes it work": it draws a precise line and pre-empts the top hostile comment. Example: "Your AI-generated LinkedIn posts are costing you customers", then the specifics (same em-dash cadence, same "game-changer" vocabulary, zero lived detail), "Don't get me wrong. I use AI daily. My problem is publishing the first draft.", "Write like you talk. [Name]". **Failure modes:** skipping the pivot, or ranting about something your own product visibly does.

## E: Demo Walkthrough

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, social]` 5 to 11 slides. Show the finished result first, then the steps.

| Slide | Job | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 1, product and outcome | Brand or product plus the finished result | "[Product] + [outcome], finished result" |
| 2, problem | The specific frustration this replaces | "[The specific pain this replaces]" |
| 3, overview | The numbered step list before the detail | "[N] steps to [outcome] in [timeframe]" |
| 4 to N−1, steps | One real UI screenshot per step with a short caption; mockups read as vaporware | "Step [n]: [screenshot] + [caption]" |
| N, result and positioning | The full-size output plus one brand-philosophy line | "[Final output] + [positioning line]" |

Overview before detail: a reader who saw "4 steps, 10 minutes" on slide 3 finishes. Example: "From CSV to live dashboard in 4 steps", the pain (weekly manual reporting), the overview, one screenshot per step, the dashboard and "Built for marketers, not analysts." **Failure mode:** screenshots with too much UI; crop to the action, zoom the control, annotate with one arrow, readable at thumbnail size.

## Platform notes

- **Instagram:** 1080×1350 (4:5) slides `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, social]`. The app allows up to 20 slides (source); through the API "Carousels can have up to 10 total images, vidoes, or a mix" (https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/media), so a carousel published by a scheduler or agent has at most 10. The caption carries the searchable text: restate the hook and add context. Slide 1 doubles as the grid thumbnail.
- **LinkedIn document posts:** upload a PDF (LinkedIn also accepts DOC, DOCX, PPT and PPTX, up to 100 MB and 300 pages, Help a564109). Square or 4:5. LinkedIn shows an "N pages" badge, which suits Value-Stack's exact count. B2B tolerates denser text per slide, but one idea per slide still holds. The post text above the document is a second hook; write it as its own post `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, social]`.
- **TikTok photo mode:** the same frameworks work at a faster pace; trim toward each framework's minimum `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, social]`.
- **Threads:** carousels take 2 to 20 items ([PLATFORM-LIMITS.md](PLATFORM-LIMITS.md#threads)).

## Production checklist

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, social]`

- [ ] Slide 1 works as a standalone feed post
- [ ] One idea per slide; if a slide needs two sentences of setup, split it
- [ ] Same visual template on every interior slide
- [ ] Any count promised on the cover is exactly delivered (no filler, no rounding)
- [ ] Claims and numbers are real and yours; no invented stats or borrowed screenshots
- [ ] Text is legible at thumbnail size (minimum ~28pt at 1080px wide)
- [ ] Final slide has one CTA, not three
- [ ] Caption or post text written as its own hook, not a repeat of slide 1
- [ ] Slide count fits the publishing path (at most 10 through Instagram's API)

## Measuring what worked

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, social]` Judge carousels on **saves and completion**, not likes. Where per-slide analytics exist (Instagram professional insights, LinkedIn document analytics, third-party tools), the drop-off position shows which slide broke the chain. When a carousel underperforms the fix is usually slide 1 or the framework choice, not the middle slides. Track performance per framework and double down on the two that fit your audience.

## Prove it

The framework is picked before any slide is written and matches the content type (a list of 9 named tactics is a Hack List or Value-Stack, with the choice justified); slide 1 stands alone; every interior slide uses one template; the cover's count equals the slides delivered; text is at least ~28pt at 1080px; there is one CTA; the slide count fits Instagram's API cap of 10 when scheduled; and the result is read on saves and completion.
