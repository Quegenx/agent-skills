# Viral loops

"Virality can be engineered through product design, not just bought with reward programs. But don't force it - decide whether virality fits your product before building anything." From [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, referrals, references/viral-mechanisms.md] unless tagged. Growth loops as a planning model are in [marketing-loops](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/marketing-loops/SKILL.md).

## The viral potential spectrum

**Natural viral potential (build for it):**

- **Collaboration tools:** value grows when you invite others (docs, whiteboards, project management).
- **Communication tools:** you can't use them alone (email, scheduling, messaging).
- **User-facing outputs:** every use produces something others see (design, video, forms, links).

**Limited viral potential (don't force it):**

- **Backend and infrastructure:** invisible to end users; plumbing no one talks about.
- **Competitive-advantage tools:** "users *hide* that they use them (their edge)".
- **Internal-only tools:** never leave the org.

"If you're on the limited end, invest in referral programs, content, and partnerships instead of embedding viral loops that won't fire." For a collaborative product, the source's eval expects product-embedded virality first, with a reward program as the incentive-driven fallback [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, referrals evals]. app-growth sorts mobile apps the same way (strong: network effects, high LTV, show-off content; weak: solo utilities, low-ARPU free apps, embarrassing apps) in [app-growth LAUNCH-AND-MARKETING.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/app-growth/LAUNCH-AND-MARKETING.md#referral-programs).

## The seven viral mechanisms

Most are non-incentive: the loop is built into the product, not paid for.

1. **"Powered By" badges:** a small attributed badge on user-facing output ("Powered by [Product]"); every page, form or widget a customer ships becomes an ad. Often free-tier only (the paid tier removes it).
2. **Exposure loops:** normal use shows the product to non-users. Calendly and SavvyCal: every meeting invite shows the tool to the recipient. Superhuman's "Sent via Superhuman" signature worked "as a status signal, not just attribution": "Exposure loops are strongest when using the product confers status."
3. **Social sharing:** natively shareable output with a branded hook, for example #MadeWithGlide; one-tap "share to X/LinkedIn" on any milestone, result or artifact.
4. **Embeds:** users embed their content elsewhere and the embed carries your brand and a link back (Notion, Figma, Loom).
5. **Watermarks and mandatory badges:** harder to remove, baked into the output (OpusClips' watermark on generated clips, "Made in Webflow" on free-plan sites). "Free tier carries the mark; paid tier removes it. The free users become the distribution."
6. **Referral programs:** explicit incentives for referring; "The one *incentive-driven* mechanism on this list - use it when the product itself doesn't naturally spread" ([PROGRAM-DESIGN.md](PROGRAM-DESIGN.md)).
7. **Product-driven word of mouth:** people tell others unprompted because the product is good, novel or useful. "Not a mechanism you bolt on - it's earned through the product experience. Engineering the other six makes this easier to trigger."

## Referral best practices

- **Lead with the larger number:** frame the reward with whichever number looks bigger. On a $25 product, say "$10 off", not "40% off"; on a $500 product, "20% off" rather than "$100 off" "if the percentage frames better". "Rule of thumb: under ~$100, lead with the dollar amount; over ~$100, test the percentage." The claim must match the real offer ([offer-design FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/offer-design/FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md)). The source's "larger number" label conflicts with its literal examples ($10 rather than 40, then 20 rather than 100); keep them as attributed framing choices to test, not a consistent numerical rule. [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, referrals, references/viral-mechanisms.md]
- **Reward timing:** ask and reward at the aha moment or a milestone (first success, upgrade, streak). "Motivation to share peaks right after value is experienced, not at signup."
- **Double-sided rewards:** both referrer and referred get value; "Higher conversion than single-sided, and gives the referrer a generous, non-selfish reason to share".
- **Friction:** "Every extra step kills share rate." One-click sharing with a pre-generated link and no form; pre-written messages so the user just hits send; in-product placement at the trigger moment, "not buried in settings".

## Prove it

The product's place on the spectrum is written down with the reason; a natural-end product ships at least one built-in mechanism (badge, exposure loop, share, embed or watermark) before a paid program; a removable badge is removable only on the paid tier; and the share action takes one tap from the moment it appears.
