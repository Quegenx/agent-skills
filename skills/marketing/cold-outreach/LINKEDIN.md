# LinkedIn outreach

LinkedIn is the second channel in most B2B outreach, and people who ignore cold email often accept a relevant connection request; it also carries the tightest limits and the highest account risk [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, cold-email] references/linkedin-outreach.md. The source says its figures "come mostly from LinkedIn automation vendors' and agencies' own datasets (2025-26). Use them as ranges." LinkedIn's own limits were not fetched. Data-protection rules for LinkedIn contacts are in [prospecting LIST-COMPLIANCE.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/prospecting/LIST-COMPLIANCE.md#linkedin).

## Fix the profile first

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, cold-email] Every prospect who gets a request looks at the sender's profile:

- **Headline:** who you help and how.
- **About:** opens with the problem you solve, then proof.
- **Featured:** one case study, teardown or resource a prospect would click.
- **Recent activity:** posts or thoughtful comments from the last few weeks.
- A real photo and a banner that reinforces what you do.

## Volume

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, cold-email] Vendor ranges: about 100 connection requests a week per account (15 to 25 a day; new or quiet accounts start at 20 to 30 a week); roughly 50 to 100 messages a day to connections; InMail limited by your credits. A low acceptance rate says your requests are unwanted: keep it above about 25 to 30% "by sending fewer, better-targeted requests". Withdraw requests still pending after 2 to 3 weeks.

## The sequence

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, cold-email]

1. **Day 1:** view their profile, follow them, react to or comment on a recent post (a real comment, not "Great post!").
2. **Day 2 to 3:** connection request; a note is optional.
3. **After acceptance:** a short first message tied to why you connected. No pitch.
4. **3 to 5 days later:** a message with value: an insight, a resource, an example.
5. **5 to 7 days later:** a light ask, or a voice note.

Stop after three messages without a reply. Run it alongside email and stop both the moment either gets a reply ([SEQUENCES.md](SEQUENCES.md#rules-that-hold-any-cadence-together)).

## Copy

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, cold-email]

- **Connection note:** under about 200 characters, the reason you're connecting and nothing else. Use one when you have a specific, credible reason; one 2025 agency study the source cites found notes cut acceptance slightly (about 25% vs 28%) but raised replies (8% vs 5%). Write these yourself or edit hard.
  > Saw your post on moving pricing tests off the homepage. We've run a few of those for SaaS teams; would be good to connect.
- **First message after acceptance:** 150 to 200 characters, the reason you connected, an easy question.
  > Thanks for connecting. Curious how you're deciding what to test first on the new pricing page. Is that sitting with you or with product?
- **Voice notes** (mobile app only): under 45 seconds, their name and the specific reason, never read from a script. Public data is thin; test them.
- **InMail:** for senior people you can't reach otherwise; the same rules as cold email.

The voice and AI-tell rules in [COPY.md](COPY.md) apply even more strongly here: "LinkedIn messages sit next to the sender's face and name."

## The User Agreement on automation

LinkedIn's User Agreement section 8.2 says members agree not to "Use bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services, add or download contacts, send or redirect messages ... or otherwise drive inauthentic engagement", and also bans scraping tools and attempts to "bypass or circumvent any access controls or use limits" (https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement). The source's line "Every third-party LinkedIn automation tool violates LinkedIn's User Agreement" matches it. Its history of LinkedIn acting against vendors (company pages removed for Apollo and Seamless in 2025 and HeyReach in March 2026, lawsuits against scrapers) is news the source reports; it was not verified.

| Option | Where it stands | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Manual (Sales Navigator, phone, a daily routine) | Within the User Agreement | Tier 1 accounts, founders, anyone whose profile is a business asset |
| Assisted (tools that queue tasks for a person to do, CRM reminders) | The person does every action | Most teams |
| Automated (tools that act through your account or session) | Against section 8.2; the account can be restricted and the vendor can disappear | The source: "High-volume Tier 3 programs, on profiles the business can afford to lose"; this skill doesn't recommend it because of section 8.2 |

The source goes on to give tips for running automation under detection (randomized timing, dedicated residential IPs per account); those circumvent use limits and are out of bounds ([SENDING.md](SENDING.md#out-of-bounds)). Use only real profiles owned by the people they represent, never rented or bought accounts, and export your own data regularly.

## Measure

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, cold-email] Vendor ranges for 2025 to 2026: acceptance 25 to 30% on average, 30%+ for active, followed profiles; replies to messages 7 to 10%. Track positive replies and meetings as for email. "A high acceptance rate with no replies means the targeting is fine and the messages aren't."

## Prove it

The sender's profile states who they help with a featured proof item; every request and message was sent by the person whose profile it is, with no tool acting through the session; notes are under about 200 characters and first messages under about 200; nobody gets a fourth unanswered message; pending requests older than three weeks are withdrawn; and a LinkedIn reply pauses that person's email sequence.
