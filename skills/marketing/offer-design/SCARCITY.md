# Scarcity and urgency

The reason to buy now, honestly. Scarcity limits quantity; urgency limits time. From [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers] `references/scarcity-urgency.md` (MIT, Copyright (c) 2025 Corey Haines). The source's bar is "only ship real scarcity"; the FTC's list of fake patterns (false low stock, false high demand, baseless countdown timers, false limited-time messages, false activity messages) and the 16 CFR 233.5 rule on advance sales and "limited" offers are defined in [FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md](FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md#scarcity-urgency-and-dark-patterns). Under FTC Act Section 5 those patterns are deceptive advertising, not only bad marketing.

## Honest formats

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

| Format | Sample line | Works for |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | "We take 2 fractional CMO engagements per quarter. Next slot opens September 15." | Services, agencies, coaching, anything labor-bound |
| Cohort | "Cohort 7 starts October 1. Doors close September 28. Next cohort: January." | Courses, programs, group coaching |
| Founding member | "Founding pricing - $497/mo for the first 50 members. After we hit 50, the price moves to $797/mo." | SaaS, communities, memberships |
| Inventory | "We're only producing 100 of the print edition. Sold out, no reprints." | Physical products, limited digital runs, bespoke services |
| Seasonal | "Tax-prep service for Q1 2026 closes January 15. After that, we focus on existing clients only until April." | Tax, retail Q4, events, enrollment |
| Bonus expiry | "Order by Friday and the 1:1 onboarding session is included. After Friday, the offer is the same but onboarding is +$497." | Anything with a launch window |
| Price-increase scheduling | "On November 1, the program moves from $1,997 to $2,497. Anyone enrolled before November 1 keeps the $1,997 price for life." | Courses, SaaS, communities |

- **Enforce it.** The source: "actually raise the price when you hit 50. Otherwise it was a lie"; "actually remove the bonus when the date hits". 16 CFR 233.5 makes this a rule: no advance sale without a good-faith expected increase, and no "limited" offer that isn't limited.
- **A bonus that expires is still a "free" offer** under 16 CFR 251.1 ([BONUSES.md](BONUSES.md)).

## Fake patterns to remove

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

- **Countdown timers that reset.** If you use a countdown, it ends on a real date and the discount or bonus verifiably ends then.
- **"Only 3 spots left"** on a product with no capacity limit, or a number that stays at 3 for weeks.
- **Manufactured FOMO:** "127 people are looking at this page right now". The source says such counts can be honest when they reflect actual traffic; the FTC names only false ones. The source's Booking.com example is unverified and not repeated here.
- **An always-on "expires at midnight" bonus.**
- **Repeated "last chance" emails.** Use "last chance" once.
- **Not repeated here:** the source's claim that "real scarcity converts ~the same as fake scarcity at the moment of purchase" has no source.

## When scarcity isn't needed

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers] Cancel-anytime subscriptions; low-priced impulse products ($5 to 30); premium or luxury brands (explicit scarcity reads as low-status); high-trust audiences who already know they'll buy. Forced scarcity is worse than none.

## The diagnostic

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

1. Is there a real constraint (capacity, cohort, inventory, season, batch)?
2. What is the honest version, in plain English?
3. Can the buyer verify it (can they see the count of founding members)?
4. Will you actually enforce it (close the door, raise the price, remove the bonus)?
5. Is it next to the buy button, not buried?

Scarcity is the close, not the offer: fix dream outcome, likelihood, time delay and effort first ([OFFER-ANATOMY.md](OFFER-ANATOMY.md#the-value-equation)). "Strong offer + real scarcity = the buyer decides now. Weak offer + scarcity = the buyer decides not to buy, faster."

## Prove it

Every deadline, count and limit on the page traces to a real constraint you can show; the countdown's end date is fixed and survives a reload in a fresh browser; on the date, the price rose or the bonus came off; "last chance" went out once; and the scarcity line sits next to the buy button.
