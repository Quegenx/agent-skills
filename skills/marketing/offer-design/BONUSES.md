# Bonuses

How to add bonuses that raise perceived value without devaluing the core. From [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers] `references/bonus-stacking.md` and `references/offer-anatomy.md` (MIT, Copyright (c) 2025 Corey Haines). In US law a bonus is a "free" item: no mark-up on the core to pay for it, terms beside the offer and not behind an asterisk, no more than 6 months of the same offer in 12, and a "$X value" only where substantial sales happen at that price. Those rules are defined in [FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md](FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md#free-offers-and-bonuses).

## What bonuses do

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers] Three jobs at once: raise perceived value of the total offer; lower perceived risk ("I still got X"); and close specific buying objections, the underrated one. Strong stacks read the buyer's hesitations and close them in order; weak ones throw four generic "extras" at the buyer.

## One bonus per objection

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

| Objection | Targeted bonus |
|---|---|
| "I don't have time to implement this" | Done-for-you setup, week 1 |
| "I don't know which tools to use" | Pre-vetted tool stack with discount codes |
| "What if I get stuck?" | 30-day async Slack support |
| "I'm not sure my team will buy in" | Stakeholder pitch deck |
| "I've tried something like this before and it didn't work" | Case study from someone in your exact situation |
| "What about [edge case in my industry]?" | Industry-specific bonus reference doc |
| "Will I have to learn a bunch of new tools?" | Pre-built templates for the tools we recommend |
| "What if I don't finish it?" | 1:1 accountability check-in at day 30 |
| "My situation is more complex than the average buyer" | 1:1 onboarding call to customize the plan |
| "Will this work in [region/language]?" | Localized version or addendum |

**Find the real objections:** read every refund-request email and sales-call transcript from the last 6 months; read your own sales page aloud and write down every doubt; ask 3 recent buyers "What almost made you not buy?". The answers cluster around 3 to 6 objections; build a bonus for each. Interview method is in [customer-research SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/customer-research/SKILL.md).

## The stack

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers] Most strong offers stack 3 to 5 bonuses; more reads as padding, fewer leaves objections open.

| # | Type | Purpose | Typical form |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Speed bonus | Removes the time-delay objection | Templates, swipes, accelerators |
| 2 | Trust bonus | Removes the likelihood-of-failure objection | Case study, methodology doc, examples library |
| 3 | Stuck bonus | Removes "what if I get stuck" | Office hours, Slack, on-demand support |
| 4 | Decision bonus | Removes "I have to choose between X and Y" | Tool stack with discount codes, pre-vetted recommendations |
| 5 (optional) | Bigger-than-you-asked bonus | Adds dream-outcome surface area | Adjacent deliverable, related framework, partner offer |

## Stated values

- **The source's rules** [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]: a stated value the buyer can verify against a comparable product ("$497 value - that's what the standalone template pack costs" beats "$5,000 value"); things you'd actually sell separately; each bonus with a specific named outcome; and a total under 2x the core price (its heuristic).
- **The FTC rule is stricter** (16 CFR 233.2): a comparable or retail value claim holds only if a reasonable number of principal outlets sell comparable goods at that price. If you can't point to that price, drop the dollar amount. A proof asset (case studies) carries no dollar value, and a savings estimate ("saves ~$1,200/yr" in discount codes) is not a sale price, so label it as savings.
- **Not copied from the source:** its worked example's "Weekly 1:1s for 12 weeks (~$12K value)" and "Pre-vetted execution-partner intros (priceless)" have no comparable and break its own rule ([FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md](FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md#what-the-sources-got-wrong)).

## When bonuses backfire

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

- **Inflated values:** "$50,000 in bonuses included today only!" on a $497 product.
- **Bonuses that devalue the core:** "bonus sales page edits for free for life" on a $5K sales page implies the core is incomplete. Bonuses add to a complete core; they don't patch an incomplete one.
- **A bonus stack instead of a core:** converts at the sale, then produces angry refund requests.
- **Stacked too high:** 5+ high-value bonuses reads as a course-bro funnel.
- **Same as everyone else:** "Private community access!" is table stakes, not a differentiator.

## Timing

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers] Day 1: speed bonuses (templates, swipes, toolkit). Weeks 2 to 4: support bonuses (Slack, office hours, check-ins). Completion: identity bonuses (certificate, alumni access). A buyer who gets every bonus up front is more likely to abandon; some at completion pulls them to finish.

## The audit

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers] For each bonus:

1. What specific buying objection does this close? If none, it's filler.
2. What is its defensible stated value? If you can't point to a comparable price, drop the dollar amount.
3. Does the buyer get it on day 1, or at a meaningful point in their journey?
4. Is it specific to my buyer, or could any competitor offer it?
5. "Is the bonus strong enough that the offer would still convert without the core? If yes, the core is weak."

## Prove it

Each bonus names the objection it closes; there are 3 to 5; every dollar value names a comparable price that substantial sales happen at, or there is no dollar value; the core price did not rise when the bonus was added; the bonus terms and any expiry sit next to the offer with no asterisk; and the same "free bonus" has not run more than 6 of the last 12 months.
