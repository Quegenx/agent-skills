## The value equation

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers] The frame is credited to Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*; "the underlying idea (multiply benefits, divide costs) is much older".

```
              Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
  Value  =  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
              Time Delay     ×   Effort & Sacrifice
```

The buyer compares Value to Price. **"Price is the comparison, not the value."** Most "lower the price" requests are really "raise the numerator or lower the denominator" requests.

| Lever | What it means | How to raise value |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome ↑ | What the customer actually wants | Connect to the bigger goal behind the surface ask. Specify and name it. |
| Perceived likelihood ↑ | Do they believe they'll get it | Proof (case studies, named customers, data), guarantees, methodology specificity |
| Time delay ↓ | How long until result | Faster onboarding, faster first win, faster end-to-end timeline |
| Effort & sacrifice ↓ | What it costs them besides money | Done-for-you, simpler process, fewer decisions, lower learning curve |

- **Dream outcome is one or two levels above the surface ask.** The source's table: "I want a website" → "I want more qualified leads I can close"; "I want to learn copywriting" → "I want to write copy clients pay me $5K+ per project for"; "I want a Notion template" → "I want to feel in control of my work for the first time in years"; "I want to lower CAC" → "I want a marketing engine I can step away from for two weeks without things breaking". Name it specifically, connect it to the bigger goal, show the day after in concrete terms. The common mistake is pitching the surface ask.
- **Perceived likelihood is "the single most underweighted lever".** Raise it with named proof, a named method, a guarantee ([GUARANTEES.md](GUARANTEES.md)), people *like them* who got results, and by pre-empting the failure path. The common mistake: "Stacking more features... instead of stacking more proof. Features address dream outcome. Proof addresses likelihood." This agrees with [anti-slop-copy SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/anti-ai-slop/anti-slop-copy/SKILL.md): outcomes over features, the feature as the proof.
- **Time delay erodes likelihood too.** Engineer the smallest early win into the first 7 days, replace a 60-minute kickoff with a short async intake, give the templates on day 1, compress the timeline only as far as you can credibly deliver, and give an explicit "first thing to do today". Promising a faster timeline than you deliver damages every later buyer's belief.
- **Effort and sacrifice is everything but money:** time, learning curve, decisions, willpower, social risk, opportunity cost. Done-for-you over do-it-yourself, fewer decisions (bundle, default, recommend), templates and defaults, async over live, less willpower. The common mistake is overestimating how much the buyer enjoys the work; the exception is identity buyers (fitness, learning, mastery), for whom the process is part of the outcome.

## Scoring the levers

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers] Score each lever 1 to 10 honestly; **the lowest is the binding constraint.**

- **Dream outcome:** can the buyer picture, in concrete terms, the day after this works? Is it named specifically enough to repeat to a friend?
- **Perceived likelihood:** do they have "at least three named, comparable proof points"? Is the method specific enough to repeat? Have you answered "but my situation is different"?
- **Time delay:** what is the first concrete win and how soon? The end-to-end timeline? Any delay surfaces (onboarding lag, asset drip, week-1 friction)?
- **Effort and sacrifice:** how much work, how many decisions, how steep the learning curve, how much synchronous time, how much emotional, social or opportunity-cost risk?
- **You know it's working** when the buyer can say the dream outcome back in their own words and doesn't ask "but does this actually work?". If they ask either, it's a value equation problem, not a copy problem.

## The six components

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

| # | Component | Question it answers | Where it fails | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Core deliverable | What do they get? | Too vague, or features instead of outcome | below |
| 2 | Bonus stack | What else makes the core feel undervalued? | No bonuses, or inflated or fake ones | [BONUSES.md](BONUSES.md) |
| 3 | Guarantee | What happens if it doesn't work? | None, wrong type, or over-promising | [GUARANTEES.md](GUARANTEES.md) |
| 4 | Scarcity / urgency | Why now, not later? | None, fake, or manipulative | [SCARCITY.md](SCARCITY.md) |
| 5 | Name | What is this thing called? | Generic, internal jargon, or none | below |
| 6 | Price + payment structure | What do they pay and how? | One number with no payment flexibility | below |

"Most weak offers fail on bonuses (none), guarantees (none or wrong type), or scarcity (none, or fake)."

## Core deliverable and scope

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

- **Define it as an outcome.** Weak: "6 modules, 24 lessons, weekly calls, private community." Strong: "A working customer-acquisition system that brings 5 qualified leads per week within 60 days - built with you, not handed to you." The features still matter; the frame is the outcome.
- **Write the scope down:** what's in, what's out, what's optional. The source's template:

```
Includes:
- 90-day program with weekly live calls (recorded)
- Private Slack with daily founder access
- 12 fill-in-the-blank templates
- 1 90-minute strategy session with a senior strategist

Doesn't include:
- 1:1 calls outside the strategy session
- Implementation of the work (you/your team does this; we coach)
- Tools and software (you provide; we recommend specific stacks)
```

- **Match depth to awareness:** sophisticated buyers want the method and scope; buyers new to the category want the outcome and proof.

## Name

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers] A named offer is repeatable, distinct, and can charge more than the same delivery sold as a service.

- **Patterns that work:** outcome-named ("The 30-Day Activation Sprint"), methodology-named ("The VAULT Framework"), identity-named ("Founder Marketing OS"), compression-named ("5-Day Cohort").
- **Patterns that don't:** generic descriptors ("Marketing Coaching Program"), internal jargon ("Tier 2 Standard"), course-bro ("The Money-Making Machine"), pun overload ("GrowthGoGetter").
- **The test:** can a buyer text a friend "I just signed up for *the [name]*. It's $X and you get [one-line outcome]"? If not, rename.

## Price and payment structure

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

- **Buyers compare the price to** the dream outcome, the next-best alternative, the cost of doing nothing, and other items in your own catalog. Move the perception without moving the number by showing the cost of doing nothing, anchoring against a higher-priced alternative, or sequencing higher-priced catalog items first.
- **Payment structure is its own lever:**

| Structure | When it works | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Pay in full | High-trust buyers, lower price points | Highest perceived commitment, smallest buyer pool |
| Pay in 2-4 installments | Mid-range price, hesitant buyers | More buyers, payment defaults |
| Monthly subscription | SaaS, ongoing services | Annuity revenue, churn risk |
| Pay-after-results | High-confidence delivery, sophisticated buyers | Cash flow lag, fewer disputes |
| Down payment + balance on delivery | Services with milestone-based delivery | Balance risk on backend |
| Free trial → paid | Low-friction SaaS, info products | Conversion drop-off |

- **Show the total on an installment plan.** "$6K today" vs "$2K × 3 monthly" converts differently, and the FTC lists showing only the per-payment price as a dark pattern: show the number of payments and the total ([FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md](FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md#installments-and-per-period-prices)).
- **A free trial that converts or a plan that renews** carries ROSCA's disclosure and consent duties ([FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md](FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md#trials-auto-renewal-and-cancellation)).
- **For self-serve SaaS,** tier structure and the value metric do more work than bonuses and guarantees: start in [pricing-strategy SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/pricing-strategy/SKILL.md).

## The diagnostic loop

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers] When "my offer isn't converting":

1. **Identify the business type** (service, course, coaching, info product, SaaS, agency, B2B); the playbook is type-specific ([OFFER-FORMATS.md](OFFER-FORMATS.md)).
2. **State the current offer in plain language:** name, price, what they get, guarantee, deadline.
3. **Run the value equation:** score each lever 1 to 10; the lowest is the binding constraint.
4. **Audit the anatomy:** which of the six components is missing or weak?
5. **Pick one lever to fix this iteration;** don't rebuild everything.
6. **Draft the changed component:** a new bonus, guarantee, scarcity, name or payment plan.
7. **Project the lift honestly** (below) and confirm it with a test.

## Projecting the lift

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

- **Multiply lifts, don't add them:** +20% then +30% = `1.20 × 1.30 = 1.56`, so 2% becomes 3.12%, a 1.12 percentage-point increase.
- **The source's planning range** is 10 to 40% relative lift from one component change; it says two iterations rarely reach 2 to 3x. That range is the source's heuristic, not a measured effect: treat projections as scenarios, not proven combined effects, and confirm the combined offer with an A/B test ([ab-testing SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/ab-testing/SKILL.md)).

## Prove it

The offer can be written in one plain paragraph (name, price, what's in and out, guarantee, deadline); each of the four levers has a score and the change being made targets the lowest; all six components are present or deliberately absent; a buyer can repeat the name and outcome in one text message; any installment price shows the count and total; and a stated lift is computed by multiplication and labelled as a scenario until a test confirms it.
