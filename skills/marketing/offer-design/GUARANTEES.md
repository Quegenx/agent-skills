# Guarantees

Which guarantee to offer, how to word it, and how to stress-test it. A guarantee raises perceived likelihood ("they'll only offer this if they're confident") and lowers effort and sacrifice (less emotional risk); the wrong one hurts more than none. From [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers] `references/guarantee-design.md` (MIT, Copyright (c) 2025 Corey Haines). What a guarantee may legally claim (full refund for "money back", conditions next to the claim, "lifetime" names the life, perform what you promise) is defined once in [FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md](FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md#guarantees).

## The eight types

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

| Type | What it promises | When it works | When it backfires |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Unconditional money-back | "Refund anytime within X days, no questions" | Low-priced info, high-trust audience | High-priced or high-touch; refund risk eats margin |
| 2. Conditional money-back | "Refund if you complete X and still don't see Y" | Courses, programs requiring effort | Sophisticated buyers; harder to honor publicly |
| 3. Better-than-money-back | "If it doesn't work, full refund + $X" | Confident delivery, ample margin | If you fail; the few failures explode publicly |
| 4. Service-level / SLA | "If we don't deliver X by Y, your money back" | Productized services, agency work | Vague SLAs you can't measure |
| 5. Performance-based | "Pay only when X happens" (rev share, results-based) | Sophisticated B2B, high-confidence delivery | Long cycles, hard-to-attribute outcomes |
| 6. Anti-guarantee | "No refunds. Make sure you want it." | Premium audiences, mature buyers | Confused or first-time buyers; reads cold |
| 7. Outcome-or-extension | "If you don't get X by Y, we continue free" | Coaching, services with extendable time | Open-ended cost; choose with care |
| 8. Comparison guarantee | "Beat [competitor]'s result or refund" | When you can credibly compare | When you can't measure the competitor cleanly |

- **Types 1 to 3 must refund the full purchase price** to be called money-back (16 CFR 239.3(a)). An anti-guarantee sold to EU consumers meets the 14-day withdrawal right, which these skills have not checked ([FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md](FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md)).

## Picking one

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

1. **The buyer's biggest perceived risk:** "What if it doesn't work?" → money-back (1, 2, 3); "What if you don't deliver on time?" → SLA (4); "What if I pay and get no results?" → performance-based (5) or outcome-or-extension (7); "Is this real or scam?" → comparison or specificity (8).
2. **Your refund tolerance:** absorb refunds at scale → unconditional (1); need to qualify refunders → conditional (2); confident enough to add a bonus → better-than-money-back (3); can't afford refunds → anti-guarantee (6), or none plus strong proof.
3. **Buyer sophistication:** premium or mature → an anti-guarantee can read as confidence; first-time in the category → a strong refund guarantee; sophisticated B2B → SLA or performance-based.
4. **How measurable the outcome is:** clean → performance, comparison or outcome-or-extension; fuzzy → money-back with a conditional gate (you completed the work).

## By business type

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

| Business | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| Course / cohort | "Complete all six modules within 60 days, submit the final exercise, and if you haven't [specific outcome] we refund in full." | "100% money-back guarantee." No conditions = refund magnet. |
| Coaching / consulting | A mid-engagement off-ramp: after the first two sessions, end it and refund the unused balance. Call this a pro-rated refund, not money-back (16 CFR 239.3(a)). | "Satisfaction guaranteed." Means nothing. |
| Productized service / retainer | "First month is a paid pilot. At the end, if you don't see [specific milestone], you don't pay for month 2 and we end on good terms." | "We'll work until you're happy." Open-ended cost. |
| High-ticket community or mastermind | "No refunds. The application process is rigorous because the value is real. If you're not sure, don't apply yet." | A generic 30-day refund. Reads cheap. |
| Low-ticket info product | "30-day no-questions refund." | No guarantee. |
| SaaS | A free trial, or annual prepay with money back in the first 30 days. | "Cancel anytime" alone: standard terms, not a guarantee. |
| Direct response | Double-your-money-back or comparable risk inversion. | A vanilla 30-day refund. |

- **The source's coaching line** ("we end it and refund the unused balance") sat in the money-back family; the FTC guide reserves that label for a full purchase-price refund ([FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md](FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md#what-the-sources-got-wrong)).
- **On the app stores,** refunds belong to the store; don't promise an in-app refund ([onboarding-and-upgrades UPGRADE-PROMPTS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/onboarding-and-upgrades/UPGRADE-PROMPTS.md#store-rules-for-in-app-upgrades)).

## Wording

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

- **Specific terms:** "If, after completing the first 4 weeks of the program, you can't point to one specific business outcome you've achieved, email us and we'll refund 100%."
- **Confident tone:** "We know this works. If it doesn't for you, we don't want your money."
- **Acknowledge the awkwardness:** "Guarantees feel slimy. Here's ours anyway: if you do the work in modules 1-3 and don't see meaningful traction, we refund."
- **Patterns that don't work:** "100% satisfaction guaranteed!" (generic, and with no stated conditions it fails 16 CFR 239.3(b)); "Lifetime guarantee" (meaningless without conditions, and 239.4 requires naming the life); multiple stacked guarantees; legalese.
- **Put it next to the buy button.** The source: "Move it above the buy button." The FTC guide requires the conditions to be "noticed and understood", so the conditions travel with it. The copy side is in [anti-slop-copy PAGES.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/anti-ai-slop/anti-slop-copy/PAGES.md) ("the risk reducer that's true").

## Common mistakes

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers]

- **Promising more than you deliver:** "Double your revenue or your money back + $1,000." Stress test: **what happens if 10% of buyers invoke the guarantee?**
- **Too many conditions:** "Two conditions max. Three only if they're closely related" (completed the course AND submitted the final project AND emailed a question). A long list reads as "they made it impossible to get a refund".
- **Hiding it in fine print.**
- **Never re-reading it:** re-read the text every six months; an offer change can undermine it.
- **A guarantee instead of proof:** "Strong proof + weak guarantee > strong guarantee + weak proof." Build proof first.

## No guarantee

[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, offers] An anti-guarantee works when buyer sophistication is high, an application or qualification step precedes the sale, the price is premium to luxury, the brand is established, and proof is overwhelming. The wrong audience reads it as cold or scammy.

The audit: (1) what is the buyer's actual risk, in concrete terms; (2) which of the eight types reverses that risk; (3) what is your honest refund tolerance (refund rate × refund cost, sustainable?); (4) does the type match the audience's sophistication? The source's claim that "adding any guarantee is almost always a lift" is its opinion with no study behind it.

## Prove it

The chosen type is named and traced to the buyer's biggest risk; a "money back" guarantee refunds the full price; its conditions (two at most) sit beside the claim, above the buy button, not in a footnote; "lifetime" says whose lifetime; the 10%-invoke stress test fits the margin; and proof on the page outweighs the guarantee.
