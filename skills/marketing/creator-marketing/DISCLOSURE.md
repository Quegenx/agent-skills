# Disclosure and honest endorsements

The US rules for paid, gifted and affiliated content, as the FTC states them, and what Meta, TikTok, YouTube and Telegram require on top. Sources: the FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255 (revised 88 FR 48102, July 26 2023, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255); the Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465 (89 FR 68077, Aug 22 2024, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465); the FTC's "Endorsement Guides: What People Are Asking" (https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides, "FAQ" below), "Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers" (https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers, "Disclosures 101") and "Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule: Q&A" (https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers, "Reviews Rule Q&A"); and the platform pages linked in each section. Where coreyhaines31/marketingskills `influencer-marketing` or lightreel.ai says something different, the official form is given with what the source said. Outside the US, "stricter regimes in the UK/EU, category rules for health/finance/alcohol" apply; "When the campaign is regulated or cross-border, route to legal" `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]`.

## Who is liable

Liability runs three ways (16 CFR 255.1):

- **The advertiser:** "An advertiser may be liable for a deceptive endorsement even when the endorser is not liable." (255.1(d))
- **The endorser:** endorsers "may also be liable for failing to disclose" a material connection. (255.1(e))
- **Intermediaries:** agencies, PR firms, review brokers and "other similar intermediaries may be liable". (255.1(f))

The source said "FTC actions target advertisers"; the rule above makes the brand, the creator and the middleman each liable.

The advertiser's compliance program, from 255.1(d): advertisers should "(1) Provide guidance to their endorsers ...; (2) Monitor their endorsers' compliance; and (3) Take action sufficient to remedy non-compliance and prevent future non-compliance. While not a safe harbor, good faith and effective guidance, monitoring, and remedial action should reduce ... an advertiser's odds of facing a Commission enforcement action." In practice: the disclosure duty in the brief and the agreement, a check of each live post, and a fix request (or takedown) when one is missing `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]`.

## What counts as a material connection

Disclose a connection when it "is not reasonably expected by the audience" (255.5(a)). Material connections include "free or discounted products (including products unrelated to the endorsed product) ... regardless of whether the advertiser requires an endorsement in return", and "early access to a product or the possibility of being paid, of winning a prize". Disclosures 101: "Disclose when you have any financial, employment, personal, or family relationship with a brand... Financial relationships aren't limited to money."

So gifting is not a loophole: a gifted post still needs disclosure `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]`, and so does an ambassador's early access, a commission link, or a family or employee tie.

## Words that work

From Disclosures 101 and the FAQ:

| Clear enough | Not enough on its own |
|---|---|
| "advertisement", "ad", "sponsored" ("So are terms like 'advertisement,' 'ad,' and 'sponsored.'") | "sp", "spon", "collab" ("Don't use vague or confusing terms like 'sp,' 'spon,' or 'collab,'") |
| "Thanks to Acme brand for the free product" ("often enough if placed in a way that is hard to miss") | Stand-alone "thanks" or "ambassador" |
| "#XYZ_Ambassador", "#XYZ_Partner" ("will likely be more understandable"); "AcmePartner" or "Acme Ambassador" on space-limited platforms | "#client", "#advisor", "#consultant" ("Probably not") |
| "I'm a paid consultant to the marketers of XYZ", "XYZ_Consultant" | "Gifted" alone (ambiguous) |
| "Gifted by XYZ" ("should be sufficient when all you've received is a free product") | "contains paid content" when "the video mentions multiple products or services without identifying which content is paid" |

The source listed "thanks to [brand]" among terms the FTC finds insufficient; the FTC calls "Thanks to Acme brand for the free product" often enough, and only a stand-alone "thanks" vague. The source's "Presented by" label for newsletters is not a term the FTC names; "Sponsored" is.

"The disclosure should be in the same language as the endorsement itself." (Disclosures 101)

## Where and how to disclose

- **Same channel as the claim** (16 CFR 255.0(f)): "if the representation is made through visual means, the disclosure should be made in at least the communication's visual portion; if ... audible means ... at least the communication's audible portion; and if ... both ... the disclosure should be made in the communication's visual and audible portions. A disclosure presented simultaneously in both ... is more likely to be clear and conspicuous." The source said the disclosure must be "spoken aloud in video/audio, not just in the description"; the rule requires audio when the claim is spoken, and both channels are only "more likely" to be clear.
- **In the video, not only the description:** "If making an endorsement in a video, the disclosure should be in the video and not just in the description" (Disclosures 101). The Reviews Rule Q&A adds that "#Ad ... may be too easy to miss in a video post".
- **Stand out:** "by its size, contrast, location, the length of time it appears ... should stand out"; audible disclosures need "volume, speed, and cadence sufficient"; on social media "the disclosure should be unavoidable"; and it must not be "contradicted or mitigated by ... anything else in the communication" (255.0(f)). When an endorsement targets a specific audience, such as older adults, "'ordinary consumers' includes members of that group."
- **Stories and pictures:** "superimpose the disclosure over the picture and make sure viewers have enough time to notice and read it." (Disclosures 101)
- **Live streams:** "the disclosure should be repeated periodically so viewers who only see part of the stream will get the disclosure." (Disclosures 101)
- **Places that are missed:** "ABOUT ME or profile page, at the end of posts or videos, or anywhere that requires a person to click MORE"; and "Don't mix your disclosure into a group of hashtags or links." (Disclosures 101) A brand affiliation in a dedicated account's bio is therefore a profile page: each post still needs its own disclosure.
- **One convention across a group:** on a brand trip or a multi-creator campaign, give every participant the same plain wording ("Hosted by…", "Paid partnership with…", "Brand ambassador"); brand visibility in the shot is not disclosure `[lightreel]`. A lightreel FAQ block suggests branded hashtags "instead of formal #ad tags" and says co-branded drops need no disclosure; that contradicts Disclosures 101 above and lightreel's own post body, so don't use it.

## Platform tools

Use the platform's tool in addition to your own words: "the ultimate responsibility for clearly and conspicuously disclosing a material connection rests with the influencer and the brand - not the platform. ... always best to add your own disclosure even if a platform offers its own disclosure tool." (FAQ) "Don't assume that a platform's disclosure tool is good enough, but consider using it in addition to your own, good disclosure." (Disclosures 101) The Reviews Rule Q&A says a built-in tool is "Not necessarily" clear and conspicuous. The platforms also require their tool:

- **Meta** (Branded Content Policies, https://www.facebook.com/business/help/221149188908254): "Branded content may only be posted with the use of the branded content tool, and creators must use the branded content tool to tag the featured third party product, brand, or business partner with their prior permission". Accounts must pass eligibility. Government officials and candidates "may not use branded content" (a US exception exists for partners authorized for political ads). For dating services the business partner "must receive written permission from Facebook"; for real-money gambling both the partner and the creator need it; pharmacies and drug and alcohol treatment centers also need LegitScript certification. "Creators cannot accept anything of value to post content that does not feature themselves or that they were not involved in creating."
- **Meta partnership ads** (https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/instagram/116947042301556, https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1732548120741800): the brand can boost a creator's branded content only when the creator turned on "Allow brand partner to boost" and tagged it with the paid partnership label, or shared a partnership ad code. The ad header holds "up to 2 identities" (advertiser and partner, partner only, or Dynamic identity); "the first identity ... has to be the Instagram account that authored the post"; "Regardless of which identity is selected in the header, ranking will use signals from both accounts". Boosted content "can no longer be edited", and partnership ads appear in the Meta Ad Library. The ad-code API is `POST /{ig-media-id}/partnership_ad_code` with `instagram_basic`, `instagram_branded_content_creator` and `business_management` (https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/marketing-api/ad-creative/partnership-ads/ad-codes.md). The label comes from the creator's branded-content tagging, not from whitelisting.
- **TikTok** (https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-to-turn-on-the-commercial-content-disclosure-setting-in-tiktok, https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-the-content-disclosure-setting-for-creators): the path is Upload > Show more > Disclose commercial content > Your brand / Branded content > Brand partnership tag; "The authorization period is 60 days by default". Labels read "Commercial content", or "Paid partnership" for third-party branded content. The setting also exists for LIVE and after posting (More options > Ad settings). Disclosure "will not affect the way TikTok recommends content"; undisclosed branded content "may be restricted or removed" and "may not be eligible for distribution in the For You feed". No TikTok page found says Spark Ads authorization adds the label. The Branded Content Policy published 4 Aug 2026, in effect 31 Aug 2026 (https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/bc-policy/en), says "You are responsible for ensuring that any Branded Content you post complies with all applicable laws ... including ... disclosures and disclaimers that are in addition to Branded Content disclosures", and restricted-industry brands "may only partner with content creators through the TikTok One platform".
- **TikTok and Instagram APIs:** an agent that posts sponsored or AI-generated content sets TikTok's `brand_content_toggle` and `is_aigc` and Instagram's `is_ai_generated`; what each flag means is in [social-media PLATFORM-LIMITS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/social-media/PLATFORM-LIMITS.md#tiktok).
- **YouTube** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/154235): "you have to let YouTube know by selecting the paid promotion button", which "adds a disclosure label that appears at the beginning of your video". Age and location limits for branded content are set in YouTube Studio on a computer, only after selecting Yes under Paid promotion. "You and the partners you work with are also responsible for ... all applicable legal requirements."
- **Telegram Ads** (https://promote.telegram.org/guidelines): "The maximum length of the ad text is 160 characters including spaces"; the policy bans "Exaggerated comparisons and absolute claims ... 'best company'" and "Clickbait". For posts bought directly from a channel admin the source says "Paid posts are ads" and applies the disclosure rules of your market and the channel's audience `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` ([TELEGRAM-CHANNELS.md](TELEGRAM-CHANNELS.md)).

## Honest endorsements

- **Real users:** when the ad says the endorser uses the product, "the endorser must have been a bona fide user of it at the time", and the advertiser may keep running it "only so long as it has good reason to believe that the endorser remains a bona fide user" (255.1(c)). Creators in a UGC or creator-ad program use the product before they make content ([UGC-PROGRAMS.md](UGC-PROGRAMS.md#playbook-first)).
- **Views still held:** the advertiser needs "good reason to believe that the endorser continues to subscribe to the views presented", secured "at reasonable intervals" (255.1(b)). This bears on 3 to 6 month usage-rights windows.
- **Old posts after a product change:** no duty to delete a historic endorsement "as long as the date of that post is clear and conspicuous", but if "the advertiser shares the contractor's original endorsement after the reformulation", it must confirm the endorser still holds the view (255.1 Example 1). Check before whitelisting old creator content after a change.
- **Results claims:** an influencer who claims "This lotion cures eczema" beyond their own experience is liable, and the advertiser is liable "regardless" if it lacks substantiation (255.1 Example 5). Give creators only claims you can back; they can't fake results or imply they are customers if they aren't `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]`.
- **Faces:** an endorsement shown with "the image or likeness of a person other than the actual endorser is deceptive if it misrepresents a material attribute" (255.1(g)). This covers stock faces on testimonials and UGC slideshows.
- **AI avatars:** the Reviews Rule Q&A says there is "no blanket prohibition" on AI avatars, but testimonials must not be fake or false, avatars can be deceptive under 255.1(g) and 255.2(c), and a celebrity avatar without permission violates the rule. `[lightreel]` likewise identifies synthetic spokespeople where relevant; AI UGC formats are in [UGC-FORMATS.md](UGC-FORMATS.md#ai-ugc).

## Fake engagement and fake accounts

- **Bought indicators are a rule violation:** it is a violation to "Purchase or procure fake indicators of social media influence that they knew or should have known to be fake" (16 CFR 465.8). Indicators include "followers, ... views, ... likes, saves, shares, reposts, and comments" (465.1(j)); fake means made by bots, by accounts not tied to a real person, or by accounts that "otherwise do not reflect a real individual's or entity's activities, opinions" (465.1(h)). The FTC limits this provision to indicators the buyer knew or should have known were fake and that misrepresent influence or importance for a commercial purpose ([FTC final-rule announcement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). The old summary omitted that commercial-misrepresentation qualification. The repo still prohibits fake engagement in all cases; the fake-follower check in [PROGRAMS.md](PROGRAMS.md#finding-and-vetting-partners) helps detect it.
- **Paid comments posing as bystanders** are undisclosed endorsements `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` and, when bought, can count as fake indicators under 465.8 (comments that "do not reflect a real individual's ... opinions"). Comment from the program or brand account, or from clearly affiliated team accounts ([UGC-PROGRAMS.md](UGC-PROGRAMS.md#the-conversion-ladder)).
- **Account networks:** TikTok allows multiple accounts "but not to deceive others or break the rules", and deceptive account behavior can mean bans; TikTok "strictly prohibit[s] automation tools, scripts, or other tricks designed to bypass our systems" (Integrity and Authenticity, https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity). Do not run accounts with slight handle variations to fake independent discovery, do not simulate word of mouth, and do not script or fake account activity to avoid looking automated. A lightreel FAQ block describes apps doing the first two; it is recorded here only as a do-not.

## Claims in creator content

`[lightreel]`, from its posts on supplement, skincare and before/after videos:

- **Supplements:** use "support", "help" or "formulated to" verbs; claims printed on packaging shown in a video are still claims; keep caption claims aligned with what the creator says; give a disclaimer frame people can read; disclose redundantly (platform label, caption, and early on-screen text). The FDA disclaimer wording it quotes was not checked against 21 CFR 101.93, so take the wording from the regulation.
- **Before/after proof for efficacy claims:** the proof layers (matching angles and light, dates, split-screen, disclosed enhancement, no morphs) are in [UGC-FORMATS.md](UGC-FORMATS.md#before-and-after). Lightreel's own FAQ block suggests switching from dim before-light to bright after-light, which contradicts its post body; the body is the version kept.
- AI imagery that overpromises becomes the joke.

## Does disclosure hurt performance

The source said "the FTC has never found '#ad' to tank a genuinely good integration"; no FTC source says that. The closest official finding is TikTok's: an internal study of "nearly 2 million TikTok videos with and without proper branded content disclosure" found "no performance difference (2023 TikTok Marketing Science: Tested in ID and PK)" (TikTok content disclosure help, above).

## Prove it

Every live sponsored, gifted, affiliate or ambassador post shows a disclosure from the "clear enough" column in the post itself, in the same language and channel as the claim (on screen and spoken when the claim is spoken), outside the hashtag block and above any "more" fold; the platform tool (Meta branded content, TikTok Disclose commercial content, YouTube Paid promotion) is on as well; live streams repeat it; every agreement carries the disclosure duty and someone checked each post after it went live; no program pays for comments or followers or runs accounts that hide the brand; and a creator's results claim has substantiation on file before it runs.
