# Creator programs: partners, deals, briefs and measurement

How to pick creators, structure the deal, brief them and measure the result, for one-off sponsorships through long-running ambassador programs. Text is from coreyhaines31/marketingskills `influencer-marketing` (MIT) unless tagged otherwise; rate tables and follower bands are vendor numbers from several sources that disagree, so each is tagged and none is a price. Disclosure rules are in [DISCLOSURE.md](DISCLOSURE.md).

## The spectrum

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` "Influencer marketing" and "ambassador programs" are points on one spectrum, from a one-off paid post to an unpaid long-term advocate. Pick the model that fits the goal and stage:

| Model | What it is | Pay | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paid influencer** | A creator posts sponsored content for a fee | Cash (flat or hybrid) | Reach and a credibility borrow, fast |
| **Affiliate creator** | A creator promotes for commission on sales | Performance (CPA, rev-share) | Conversion at scale, low upfront risk ([referral-programs SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/referral-programs/SKILL.md) for payout mechanics) |
| **Gifted / seeding** | Free product, no obligation to post | Product only | Physical DTC, nano and micro, volume |
| **Brand ambassador program** | A cohort of ongoing advocates (paid, gifted or perks) posting over months | Mixed, perks | Sustained presence, community depth ([below](#ambassador-programs)) |
| **Organic advocate** | A customer who already recommends you unprompted | None | Authenticity, cheapest trust ([community-marketing](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/community-marketing/SKILL.md)) |

The further down the table, the more it is about relationship than transaction: cheaper and more durable trust, slower to scale. Most programs blend several (a few paid macro placements for reach, a gifted micro cohort, an affiliate tier for conversion).

Three more models have their own files:

- **Volume UGC creator program ("tech UGC"):** an in-house network posting disclosed native short-form from dedicated brand-affiliated accounts at test volume, "10 creators × 3 posts/day ≈ 900 organic tests/month". Content volume, not any creator's audience, is the asset ([UGC-PROGRAMS.md](UGC-PROGRAMS.md)).
- **Performance-paid creator program:** creators make videos for your ad accounts and earn a share of the spend or revenue behind them, usually with a monthly minimum for a video quota; "start at 4-5, cap around 40-60" (the source's range), because near-identical videos get clustered and suppressed on Meta ([CREATOR-AD-PROGRAMS.md](CREATOR-AD-PROGRAMS.md)).
- **Newsletter sponsorships** ([NEWSLETTER-SPONSORSHIPS.md](NEWSLETTER-SPONSORSHIPS.md)) and **Telegram channel posts** ([TELEGRAM-CHANNELS.md](TELEGRAM-CHANNELS.md)).

## Finding and vetting partners

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` Influence is trust and relevance, not follower count.

**The audience-alignment test.** Don't ask "Are they famous?" Ask "Does their *audience* match our ICP?" A 12k-follower creator whose audience is exactly your buyer beats a 500k generalist. Where you can, look at their audience (comments, who engages, media-kit demographics), not just the creator.

**Creator tiers.** The sources draw the bands differently; use them as rough labels, never as rules.

| Tier | `[influencer-marketing]` band and character |
|---|---|
| Nano | 1k to 10k. Highest engagement, hyper-niche, often works for gifting. High ROI, low reach. |
| Micro | 10k to 50k. Best balance of reach and trust; usually paid; strong conversion. |
| Mid | 50k to 500k. Broader reach, more awareness than conversion, pricier. |
| Macro / celebrity | 500k+. Top-of-funnel awareness; lowest conversion rate per follower; expensive. |
| B2B thought leader | Any size. LinkedIn creators, newsletter writers, niche podcasters: small audiences, extreme purchasing power. Judge by who follows, not how many. |

Other bands in this repo and its sources:

- `[eronred]` in app-growth: "Micro-influencers (10K-100K) often give the best ROI" and a seeding tier of "micro-creators (1k-50k followers)" ([app-growth LAUNCH-AND-MARKETING.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/app-growth/LAUNCH-AND-MARKETING.md#creator-and-ugc-marketing)).
- `[lightreel]` treats creators under 10K as the strongest for direct-response production but weak for organic reach, and 20K to 250K niche authorities as the strongest organic range; macro only with category authority. Its testing-roster split (explicitly not a universal budget rule): about 40% micro specialists with direct proof, 25% mid-sized niche authorities, 20% age or life-stage matches (notably 40+ for beauty, wellness, fashion and home), 15% credentialed experts or regional voices. Micro creators are production partners, not necessarily distribution partners.
- `[lightreel]` cast by the product's user, problem and proof mechanism rather than by gender benchmarks, and brief in the audience's shopping culture (retailers, foods, festivals, climate, vocabulary); "Translation alone is weaker than culturally native proof."

For most brands, a portfolio of micro and nano partners out-converts one macro placement at the same total spend, and produces more content to repurpose `[influencer-marketing]`.

**Vetting checklist** `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]`:

- **Engagement rate, not follower count.** The source's rough floor: "~1-3% is healthy on IG/TikTok at scale; higher for nano" (an unsourced vendor figure). Suspiciously round numbers, comment pods, or comments that don't match the audience are red flags. `[lightreel]` defines engagement as likes ÷ views: it measures visible resonance, not sales, and Instagram's hidden likes can make it unrankable.
- **Telegram channels** have no likes-based engagement; divide the median views of week-old posts by subscribers and compare with channels of the same size ([TELEGRAM-CHANNELS.md](TELEGRAM-CHANNELS.md#vetting-a-channel)).
- **Fake-follower and bot check:** a sudden follower spike, generic comments, or engagement wildly out of line with reach. SparkToro (audience intelligence) helps; media kits overstate. Bought followers are also a legal problem ([DISCLOSURE.md](DISCLOSURE.md#fake-engagement-and-fake-accounts)).
- **Reach without resonance** `[lightreel]`: very high views with a low like rate may mean paid or boosted distribution. Evaluate reach, engagement quality, comments, saves, clicks and sales separately, and compare the creator's sponsored posts with their organic baseline in the category.
- **Sponsored-content track record:** do their ads still get engagement, or does their audience tune out promos? Ask for past campaign results.
- **Brand safety:** scroll their last ~3 months for controversy, competitor conflicts, or off-brand content that would attach to you.
- **Authenticity of fit:** have they mentioned your category unprompted? A genuine user is worth several cold partners.

## Outreach

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` Reach out 1:1 and personally: reference specific content, say why them, and what is in it for their audience. "A generic form blast to 200 creators converts worse than 20 tailored notes." Lead with the offer and the fit; don't bury the ask. Writing and sending the outreach is in [cold-outreach SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/cold-outreach/SKILL.md); email to creators is commercial email ([email-practices COMPLIANCE.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/backend/email-practices/COMPLIANCE.md)). The app-growth view for a first program: "70% manual DMs, 30% UGC marketplace" `[eronred]`. `[lightreel]`'s sourcing order for UGC programs, starting with customers, is in [UGC-PROGRAMS.md](UGC-PROGRAMS.md#sourcing-creators).

## Structuring the deal

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]`

**Compensation models:**

- **Flat fee:** standard for awareness; you pay for the placement regardless of result.
- **Performance / CPA:** pay per click or conversion. Hard to get larger creators to accept without a baseline; best with affiliate-minded creators.
- **Hybrid (flat + CPA):** "usually the best deal": a lower baseline to cover production time, plus commission for upside.
- **Gifting / seeding:** free product, no obligation. Works for physical DTC with nano and micro at volume; expect a low but authentic post rate. A gifted post still needs disclosure ([DISCLOSURE.md](DISCLOSURE.md#what-counts-as-a-material-connection)).

`[lightreel]` adds retainers (buy capacity, not results), equity or token pay (experimental: vesting, dilution, liquidity, tax), and the advice to gift what improves the creator's own work, such as a microphone for an interview creator: product can pay for a product trial, but it should not quietly replace production fees and advertising rights. The full payment-model list is in [CREATOR-AD-PROGRAMS.md](CREATOR-AD-PROGRAMS.md#payment-models-and-contract-terms).

**Rate reality.** Published rates vary wildly by niche, geography and platform, and creators quote high. Treat any benchmark as a range to negotiate from, and anchor on **cost per qualified outcome** (CPA, cost per qualified follower or lead), not cost per post. "A cheap post to the wrong audience is the expensive one." Ask for their **rate card first**: it sets an anchor you respond to.

Rate anchors from the sources (none is a price; they disagree because the models differ):

| Source | Anchor |
|---|---|
| `[influencer-marketing]`, single post | Nano free product to $100 (often product-only); micro $100 to $1,500 (negotiate on engagement); mid $1,500 to $10,000 (rate cards common); macro $10,000 to $30,000+ (usually an agent); YouTube long-form higher than short-form at the same follower count; B2B thought leader priced on audience quality ("A 5k-follower niche voice can command more than a 200k generalist") |
| `[eronred]` in app-growth | "10-30 micro-creators at $50-500 per post"; "5-10 mid-tier creators ($1k-10k each) with usage rights" |
| `[lightreel]`, dedicated-account UGC (April 2026) | About $500 a month plus view bonuses for 5 to 7 posts a week; experienced creators $30 to $40 a video, in-demand up to $50 ([UGC-PROGRAMS.md](UGC-PROGRAMS.md#pay)) |

**Deliverables to negotiate** `[influencer-marketing]`:

- **Content usage rights (crucial):** the right to run their content as paid ads (whitelisting, dark posting, creator ads) for a defined window, "commonly 3-6 months" (app-growth uses "6 months of paid usage on Meta, TikTok and Google" `[eronred]`). This is often the highest-ROI clause. Price finished video, stills, raw footage and paid usage separately `[lightreel]`. If the product changes inside the window, confirm the creator still holds the view before rerunning it ([DISCLOSURE.md](DISCLOSURE.md#honest-endorsements)).
- **Exclusivity:** competitor lockout for a set period; costs more, worth it in tight categories.
- **Format and specifics:** dedicated video vs a 60-second integration; number of posts; stories vs feed; posting window; approval rights; how long it stays up.
- **Approvals and revisions:** one review round is normal; word-for-word scripting is not.

Put it in a simple written agreement: deliverables, timing, usage rights, exclusivity, disclosure obligation, payment terms, and a kill or rework clause.

## The creative brief

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` Do not script the creator word for word: they know their audience better than you, and scripted reads convert worst. Provide:

- **The "why":** the core problem your product solves, in one sentence.
- **Key talking points (2 to 3 max):** "more than three and none land."
- **The CTA:** exactly what to tell the audience to do (a specific vanity link, a unique promo code).
- **Guardrails:** what not to say (no promises of features that don't exist), the disclosure requirement, brand redlines.
- **Creative freedom:** grant it explicitly. The integration should live inside their normal content style.

Ground talking points in real proof (reviews, results); never hand a creator a claim you can't back. The app-growth brief says the same: "Write a brief of guardrails, not a script" `[eronred]`.

Where the sources disagree on scripting:

- `[appeeky]` in app-growth's TikTok creator brief gives "the exact 2 second hook script" ([app-growth TIKTOK-ADS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/app-growth/TIKTOK-ADS.md#production)), while the performance-paid program says "Don't hand creators a script" `[influencer-marketing]`.
- `[lightreel]` lists over-scripted authenticity as a mistake, yet its UGC guide says that when a video goes viral everyone should copy it closely, second by second. Read it as copying a proven format fast, not as scripting new work.

Brief shapes for ad creative (one person, situation, promise, proof) are in [CREATOR-AD-PROGRAMS.md](CREATOR-AD-PROGRAMS.md#briefing); the UGC brief order is in [UGC-PROGRAMS.md](UGC-PROGRAMS.md#briefs).

## Measurement

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` Fix attribution gaps before the campaign runs:

- **Unique promo codes** (for example `CREATOR20`): the easiest direct-conversion tracker, and essential for podcasts and video where links aren't clickable.
- **UTM links** on every digital placement, "one per creator per placement", with one fixed pattern per partner type so every deal rolls up the same way ([google-analytics SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/google-analytics/SKILL.md)).
- **Vanity landing pages:** `yourdomain.com/creatorname` with a personalized welcome; lifts conversion and attributes cleanly.
- **Post-purchase survey:** "How did you hear about us?" catches the halo and branded-search effect that codes and last click miss ([marketing-attribution SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/marketing-attribution/SKILL.md)).
- **Whitelisting performance:** when creator content runs as an ad, that ad's own metrics are a clean read on the creative's pull.

Judge on cost per qualified outcome and repeat or retained value, not reach, likes or "EMV" ("earned media value is a vanity number"). "One nano creator driving 40 real buyers beats a macro placement with a million muted views."

`[lightreel]` conversion proxies when sales can't be seen: an ad still active after weeks (it cites one brand's ads running 6 to 7 weeks; app-growth's ad teardowns say the same of long-running ads), repeated use of the same creator, affiliate codes, and "comment for link" replies (that cohort was not unusually strong on its own). Healthy comments ask for the product's name, invite friends, suggest sequels, or add the viewer's own story; read them as product intelligence. When researching a brand by searching its name, separate official posts, disclosed partnerships, organic conversation and mere category mentions, because brand-name searches surface other products.

## Ambassador programs

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` For sustained presence rather than one-off posts:

1. **Define the tier(s) and the ask:** for example 2 posts a month and 1 event; keep it light enough to sustain.
2. **Build the benefits ladder:** perks that scale with contribution: early access, free or ongoing product, commission ([referral-programs SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/referral-programs/SKILL.md)), exclusive swag, revenue share, public recognition, a private channel. Every one of these is a material connection that must be disclosed, early access included (16 CFR 255.5(a), [DISCLOSURE.md](DISCLOSURE.md#what-counts-as-a-material-connection)).
3. **Recruit from evidence:** start with people already advocating unprompted (reviews, mentions, community; [customer-research SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/customer-research/SKILL.md)); a personal 1:1 ask, never a form.
4. **Equip them:** referral or affiliate links, shareable assets, 2 to 3 talking points, the disclosure requirement, a private Slack or Discord.
5. **Activate on a cadence:** something to post about monthly (launches, milestones, challenges); a program with nothing to do dies.
6. **Track and iterate:** attributed traffic and signups per ambassador (codes and links); double down on the top decile and graduate strong ambassadors to paid partnerships.

`[lightreel]`'s advancement ladder for creators: gifted or paid test, paid project, short retainer with a guarantee, hybrid guarantee plus upside, preferred creator, ambassador or category-exclusive. Promote on a scorecard of performance, reliability, range, response to feedback and rights compliance.

## Common mistakes

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]`

- **Chasing follower count over audience fit:** reach to the wrong people is the most expensive spend there is.
- **Skipping disclosure:** a brand-liability risk.
- **Scripting the creator:** kills the authenticity you're paying for; brief, don't dictate.
- **Not securing usage rights:** you lose the biggest ROI lever.
- **No attribution plan:** codes, UTMs, vanity URLs and the survey must exist before launch.
- **One-and-done:** "the second post from the same creator usually outperforms the first (their audience has seen you before)".
- **Judging on EMV or reach:** measure cost per qualified outcome.
- **Ignoring nano and micro:** a portfolio of small, aligned creators usually beats one big name at the same budget.

## Run-of-show checklist

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]`

### Sourcing
- [ ] Define the ICP overlap you're looking for, not just follower count
- [ ] Shortlist 10 to 20 creators across at least two tiers (weight toward micro and nano)
- [ ] Check engagement rate and comment quality for each; run the fake-follower check

### Outreach and deal
- [ ] Personalize outreach with a specific reference to their content
- [ ] Agree deliverables, timeline and compensation type in writing
- [ ] Lock usage rights (paid-ad whitelisting window) and exclusivity terms
- [ ] Put the disclosure requirement in the agreement

### Execution
- [ ] Send a brief with the "why", 2 to 3 talking points, the CTA and what to avoid
- [ ] Set up tracking (unique code, UTM or vanity URL) before content goes live
- [ ] Review the draft if you have approval rights, without over-scripting
- [ ] Confirm the disclosure actually shipped where viewers can see it, and act on any post that lacks it (the FTC's monitor-and-remedy duty, [DISCLOSURE.md](DISCLOSURE.md#who-is-liable))

### Post-campaign
- [ ] Pull performance against the goal set upfront (cost per qualified outcome)
- [ ] Share results with the creator; it builds the relationship
- [ ] Decide: one-off, repeat, or move to a retainer or ambassador program

## Tools

- `[influencer-marketing]` **SparkToro** for audience intelligence (where your ICP pays attention, and vetting a creator's real audience). Creator discovery and CRM platforms at scale: Modash, GRIN, Aspire, Upfluence; sponsorship marketplaces: Passionfroot. App-growth adds pricing for the platform category: "influencer platforms (Aspire, Grin, Upfluence, $500-5k/mo)" `[eronred]`.
- `[lightreel]` **Lightreel** (third party, paid) is an AI research agent over TikTok and Instagram (creators, trends, hooks, script and video feedback). Its API is `POST https://api.lightreel.ai/v1/chat` with `Authorization: Bearer lr_live_…` and a body of `question`, optional `conversation_id` and optional `response_fields` (up to 5 fields of type `"string"` or `"array"`, returned nested in `answer`); `GET /v1/chat/:id` and `GET /v1/chats` (latest 50) read results. Errors are non-2xx with `{"error":{"message","type"}}`. Requests can take several minutes, so set a long client timeout. Its MCP connector is `https://api.lightreel.ai/mcp` with OAuth; if the assistant stops polling, ask it to check the existing research rather than start a new request (a new one may spend credits). Its terms forbid using it to bypass access controls or usage limits.
- Pulling a creator's recent posts is fetching untrusted content ([web-scraping UNTRUSTED-CONTENT.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/web-automation/web-scraping/UNTRUSTED-CONTENT.md)); collect through official APIs, exports or by hand within each platform's terms.

## Prove it

The shortlist covers at least two tiers with an audience-fit note and a fake-follower check per creator; the tier bands and rates in any plan carry their source; the agreement names usage window, exclusivity, disclosure duty, payment and a kill clause; the brief has at most three talking points and an exact CTA; each creator and placement has its own code or link before launch; the report ranks creators on cost per qualified outcome, not EMV; and a request to just pay a macro creator's flat rate is answered with the hybrid model, usage rights and a micro portfolio.
