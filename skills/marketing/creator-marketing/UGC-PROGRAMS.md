# Volume UGC creator programs

A volume UGC program ("tech UGC") is an in-house network of creators posting disclosed native short-form from dedicated brand-affiliated accounts, where "content volume, not any creator's audience, is the asset". The system is from coreyhaines31/marketingskills `influencer-marketing` (`references/ugc-creator-program.md`, MIT), which distils Julia Pintar / Playkit's public playbook ("How Playkit Drove 12M App Downloads With Tech UGC", 2026) and rewrites it after Rachel Karten's public FTC critique; sourcing, screening, pay and operations detail is from lightreel.ai's UGC playbook (April 2026) and its program guide, paraphrased and tagged `[lightreel]`. Paid-ads programs where creators earn on spend are in [CREATOR-AD-PROGRAMS.md](CREATOR-AD-PROGRAMS.md); hooks and formats are in [UGC-HOOKS.md](UGC-HOOKS.md) and [UGC-FORMATS.md](UGC-FORMATS.md).

## The model

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` "10 creators posting 3×/day ≈ 900 organic tests in a 30-day campaign - against ~30 for a brand account posting daily." Creators don't need existing audiences: discovery-based feeds distribute on content. The source program's economics claim, "~$3.87 CPM vs ~$20 for Meta ads and ~$119 for micro-influencer placements", is labelled by the source itself as "vendor-supplied internal numbers", directional and not a benchmark.

`[lightreel]` runs the same idea as a five-stage loop: recruit for a specific gap, brief a customer problem, produce modular variations, distribute and measure, and retain the creators who repeatedly contribute winners.

## Compliance first

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` The viral version of this playbook drew a public FTC callout. Run it as a disclosed creator program; the testing engine works just as well when the accounts say what they are.

- **Paid creator posts are ads.** Every post needs a clear disclosure in the post plus the platform's paid-partnership label, including posts from fresh accounts designed not to look like a brand. "Doesn't look like an ad" is the exact pattern disclosure rules exist for. The bio affiliation does not replace the per-post disclosure (Disclosures 101 counts the profile page among missed placements, [DISCLOSURE.md](DISCLOSURE.md#where-and-how-to-disclose)).
- **No paid comments posing as bystanders.** The original tactic, paying creators bonuses to comment from personal accounts on videos that hit 50k views, is non-compliant as described; bought comments that don't reflect the commenter's real opinion can also count as fake indicators under 16 CFR 465.8 ([DISCLOSURE.md](DISCLOSURE.md#fake-engagement-and-fake-accounts)).
- **Honest beliefs only.** Creators actually use the product and can't fake results or imply an unpaid-customer experience they didn't have (16 CFR 255.1(c)).
- **Platform policy.** Coordinated fresh-account networks brush against TikTok and Instagram inauthentic-behavior and spam policies; disclosure labels and brand-affiliated bios reduce that risk. Meta adds: "Creators cannot accept anything of value to post content that does not feature themselves or that they were not involved in creating." A persona account run by a paid creator is still an endorsement and needs per-post disclosure.

## Playbook first

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` You cannot tell creators to "make it authentic and fun." Before recruiting:

- **Creators use the product first:** complete onboarding, test every core feature, write down the screens where the value becomes obvious.
- **Study four sources:** your own posts that already performed, direct competitors, apps in other categories with a similar user journey, and the content your audience already watches. A language app can borrow a streak format from Duolingo, a progress reveal from Strava, a study setup from Quizlet.
- **Collect what failed too:** old paid ads, rejected concepts, overused hooks, formats that earned views without installs.
- **Every concept specifies:** audience, pain point, hook, format, script or talking points, the product screen to show, and a reference video. Knowing what must be made tells you who to hire.

## The four formats

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]`

| Format | Share | What it is | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Talking video** | ~70% | Creator talks to the camera like they're on FaceTime with a friend: open with a specific problem, the product enters where it fits the story, end with the result | The conversion workhorse |
| **Wall-of-text** | | Simple B-roll and a longer on-screen thought | Goes most viral, converts least; top of funnel and account warm-up |
| **Slideshow** | | Lists, screenshots, before/after sequences; the first slide creates curiosity | Cheap volume; often automatable |
| **Hook-and-demo** | | Short hook, feature, action on screen, result | Aging format (audiences have caught on); needs a creative twist |

Test the same idea across formats: it tells you whether the idea failed or just its presentation.

## Sourcing creators

`[lightreel]` (one author's playbook; numbers are its claims):

- **Order of sources:** customers and organic advocates first; then manual recruits who fill documented audience or format gaps; then a vetted marketplace (casting by portfolio, reviews and similarity to a reference video); gifting to test affinity; referrals from retained creators; broad affiliate recruitment only once coaching and measurement exist. App-growth's split for a first program is "70% manual DMs, 30% UGC marketplace" `[eronred]`.
- **Recruit against a gap:** one creator for developer workflows, one for parents, one for salespeople, one for another geography, rather than ten identical selfie testimonials.
- **Customer to creator:** brands notice tagged, already-native product use and reach out; creators put niche and contact in their bio and apply through ambassador pages.
- **Job posts on UGC job boards:** lightreel cites about $150 a month for its own Sideshift posting while competing listings paid $200 to $300 a week; a listing needs a bright visual logo, a strong description, and cumulative monthly pay with bonuses emphasized. Its screen: a well-lit profile picture, a TikTok or Instagram account, iPhone-quality video (and, for Instagram-only applicants, a college in the bio and 500+ followers).
- **Best TikTok recruits:** 2,000 to 10,000 followers, 1 to 5 videos with 100,000+ views, face on camera, not already doing heavy UGC. Avoid creators already working for many AI startups or big programs, and program "leads" (paid 4 to 5 times the normal rate to run a page).
- **Outreach:** lightreel's DM opener is short ("[paid collab] Love your [content]. Would love to collab with you. We pay well, are low time commitment, and this wouldn't be on your main page. Lmk if you're interested!") and says the account sending it matters more than the wording; its cold-email subject is "[$xxxx/month PAID collab]". Email outreach is commercial email ([email-practices COMPLIANCE.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/backend/email-practices/COMPLIANCE.md), [cold-outreach SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/cold-outreach/SKILL.md)); send DMs by hand within each platform's messaging limits, and never pose as someone you are not.
- **Gifts:** give what improves the creator's own work (a microphone for an interview creator); product can pay for a product trial, but should not quietly replace production fees and advertising rights.

## Hiring by trial

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]`

- What matters: can they talk to a phone camera naturally, follow direction, make a script sound like their own words, and match the persona in the playbook (a study app, a fertility app and a budgeting app need different creator profiles).
- **Run a paid week-long trial** with real concepts from the playbook. Score hook, delivery, framing and editing; give written feedback; ask for a revision. "The revision shows whether you can work with them", which matters more over a long partnership.
- Pay structure: a stable base plus performance bonuses (the source program's reference point: "~$500/week per working creator").

## Pay

`[lightreel]` (April 2026, one author's figures):

- **Standard rate:** about $500 a month plus view bonuses for 5 to 7 posts a week; experienced creators $30 to $40 a video, in-demand creators up to $50. This differs from app-growth's per-post pricing ("10-30 micro-creators at $50-500 per post" `[eronred]`) because it pays a dedicated-account program monthly.
- **View bonuses:** thresholds ($100 per 100K views, $250 per 250K, or $100 per 250K) or CPM ($1 per 1,000 views; its worked example: videos of 4,000, 2,000 and 67,000 views earn $73). Pure CPM with no base attracts low-quality creators and burns them out.
- **Pay weekly or bi-weekly** (PayPal, wire, or an automated payout service).
- Payment models and contract terms for performance pay are in [CREATOR-AD-PROGRAMS.md](CREATOR-AD-PROGRAMS.md#payment-models-and-contract-terms).

## Accounts

- `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` Each creator runs dedicated per-brand TikTok or Instagram accounts "with the brand affiliation in the bio and disclosure on the posts"; "the algorithm benefits of a fresh, niche-trained account don't depend on hiding who runs it."
- `[lightreel]` Name the account with the creator's real name or an abbreviation and use a normal photo of the person, not a logo; the brand in the bio is optional in its playbook and improves conversion (and the bio still doesn't replace per-post disclosure).
- **Before posting,** the creator uses the account as a real viewer in the niche for a few days (watching, liking, commenting genuinely, following a handful of relevant accounts) so the feed learns the audience. Both sources frame their warm-up checklists partly as not looking automated; that framing is not adopted. Do not script, automate or fake activity to get past platform detection: TikTok "strictly prohibit[s] automation tools, scripts, or other tricks designed to bypass our systems". App-growth's line for Spark Ads accounts: "Age a new account with 3 to 5 organic niche videos over 3 to 7 days" `[appeeky]` ([app-growth TIKTOK-ADS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/app-growth/TIKTOK-ADS.md#accounts-and-posting)).

## Briefs

`[lightreel]`:

- **Brief order** (from a Wispr Flow example): a recognizable situation, a specific friction, the product used on screen, the visible output, the benefit in the customer's language, a direct CTA.
- **A reusable container:** a stable format with a swappable hero item and audience ("one fall staple styled three ways").
- **Different openings, shared body:** ask for genuinely different opening hypotheses on one body (for example context switching, ticket-writing time, flow state), not synonyms, and get clean footage you can recombine. App-growth's 6-ad matrix has "A and A′ differ only in the first 2 seconds" `[appeeky]`; lightreel's point is that the two openings must be different reasons to keep watching.

## Cadence and review

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]`

- **3 posts a day per creator, ~2 hours apart,** captions and hashtags per the playbook.
- **Every video is reviewed before posting:** submission, check against the playbook, written notes, revision, approval. Track brief, submission, feedback, approval and results in one system.
- Review for hook, script, product screen, format, and anything that makes it feel like an ad (stiff delivery, overproduced editing, product introduced too early).
- **Vague feedback, vague revisions.** "Make this more natural" is useless; "cut the first sentence, move the phone closer, say this line like you're complaining to a friend" is fixable.
- `[lightreel]` Don't commission more content than you can launch, label, evaluate and feed back ("inventory debt").

## The conversion ladder

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` One video doesn't do the whole job:

1. **Name the product in the hook** without stopping to explain it.
2. **Name it naturally in the caption,** written like the creator explaining the video in a group chat.
3. **Engage the comments, compliantly.** Reply from the program account, have the founder or brand engage openly, or use clearly affiliated team accounts. Do not pay for comments posing as organic bystanders. Mine comments as research.
4. **Make reply videos** to product questions: the asker has watched, opened comments and chosen to learn more. "Highest-intent surface in the system."

A strong visual hook helps, but if the conversation doesn't connect back to the product, you've earned views that move no one closer to installing.

## Iterate daily, judge in weeks

`[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]`

- Review yesterday's videos every day: repeat, change or stop.
- **Judge by product questions, saves, shares and install data, not views.** A low-view video with dozens of product questions beats a big one with an unrelated comment section. App-growth calls save and share rate "the best leading indicator" `[eronred]`.
- When something shows promise, remake it at once: new hook on the same format, same hook with another creator, same idea in another format. **Change one major variable at a time.**
- Reuse the exact language commenters use for their problem; turn repeated questions into reply videos.
- You're looking for a format that performs more than once.
- **Give it four weeks minimum.** By week four you should see hooks and formats working across several creators, repeated product questions, and concepts driving saves, shares and installs more than once.

## Operations and retention

- `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, influencer-marketing]` Three requirements: creator pay, **one person who owns the program**, and a system for briefs, review and tracking. Ownership is "a full-time role at ~10 creators".
- `[lightreel]` **Comms by size:** a WhatsApp or iMessage group up to about 10 creators, an Instagram group, Discord for larger groups, Slack for professional or agency creators.
- `[lightreel]` **Retention:** share performance data (hooks, hold rates, clicks, conversions, revenue), coaching, weekly calls, community, equipment, unplanned bonuses. Report back which opening held attention, which proof drove clicks, which audience converted, and which versions never got enough spend to judge.
- `[lightreel]` Keep a searchable library of hooks, bodies, demonstrations, creators, audiences and outcomes, with the rights each one carries.
- `[lightreel]` **Mistakes:** mass acceptance without differentiation; performance pay without transparency; one-off briefs with no retention path; over-scripted authenticity; product-only pay paired with ad rights; views as the only metric; scaling output before operations. App-growth's "always-on UGC network (Insense, Billo, Fiverr), 20+ ads per month" `[eronred]` works only with the tagging, testing and feedback capacity this list asks for.

## Clipping campaigns

`[lightreel]` (verified campaign cards observed in one week):

- **Economics:** payouts clustered at $0.50 to $1.00 per 1,000 views; read the hidden terms (minimum views, per-post caps such as $100, $500, $750 or $2,000, finite pools, approval risk, measurement windows such as 14 days, an agency fee quoted at 25% of budget).
- **Payout flow:** join, download approved footage, publish, submit the URL, the platform checks brief, geography, originality and suspicious traffic, qualified views accrue, balance after approval and ID checks.
- **The brief includes:** prohibited framings, disclosure expectations, approved claims, source rights, and how to handle virality for the wrong reason. Pay for installs, trials or purchases where tracking allows, not raw views only, and test with a small paid cohort before opening to a marketplace. Per-view pay is a material connection, so clips carry the disclosure (16 CFR 255.5(a), [DISCLOSURE.md](DISCLOSURE.md)).

## Prove it

The playbook exists before the first hire, with each concept naming audience, pain, hook, format, product screen and reference video; every creator used the product and passed a paid trial with a revision; every account names the brand affiliation and every post carries its own disclosure and the platform label; nobody is paid to comment as a bystander and no account activity is scripted or automated; each video passed review against the playbook; results are read on product questions, saves, shares and installs over at least four weeks; and one named person owns the program.
