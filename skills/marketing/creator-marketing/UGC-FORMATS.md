# UGC formats

How creator and UGC videos are filmed, edited and structured by format. Everything here is `[lightreel]`: lightreel.ai's UGC playbook and its October 2026 weekly reports (talking-head, before-and-after, day-in-the-life, founder-led, meme-edit, AI-generated, clipping, brand-collab, music, consumer-app and category reports), paraphrased. They are one-week observational samples measured on likes and views; trend specifics (named sounds, creators, brand cases) are left out because they expire weekly. Where a report's FAQ block contradicts its body, the body is used. The program's four production formats (talking video, wall-of-text, slideshow, hook-and-demo) are in [UGC-PROGRAMS.md](UGC-PROGRAMS.md#the-four-formats); organic video structures and specs are in [social-media SHORT-VIDEO.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/social-media/SHORT-VIDEO.md); paid TikTok formats are in [app-growth TIKTOK-ADS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/app-growth/TIKTOK-ADS.md#formats).

## Retention edits

A hand movement or physical action in the first seconds; overlaid screenshots; bottom captions; every silence cut; varied intonation and eye contact; a trending reference; a Snapchat-style text overlay. App-growth's paid rules add "Captions always on: 80%+ watch muted" and "native beats polished" `[appeeky]`.

## Talking head

- **Light and set:** natural light (car windows, window light, a lamp, a desk, sun). A car works as a studio: eye-level medium close-up, eye contact with the lens, a static thesis on top, one take, a small hand activity. The background should explain who is speaking; props prove the claim, preview the demo, or give the hands a job.
- **Delivery matches the stakes:** high energy is not required, emotional congruence is. Cut only when the argument changes.
- **Length bands observed in one week:** under 30 s for one idea; 40 to 75 s the most repeatable (a thesis plus 2 to 3 points); 90 s to 3 min for a story or proof sequence; 4 to 5 min only with dense narrative. The same post's FAQ block calls 15 to 30 s "a dead zone"; neither is a rule. App-growth's paid spec is "9:16 vertical, 15 to 30 s" `[appeeky]`: paid and organic contexts differ.
- **Pre-publish questions** (from its checklist): can a viewer repeat the thesis after five seconds? Does every cut introduce evidence or advance the argument? Is there a natural prop or activity if the shot stays static? Does the ending resolve the opening?

## Before and after

- **Transitions, best first:** a matched-frame hard cut on the strongest audio accent (same frame position, one variable changes, the after held at least as long as the before); a hand covering the camera (beauty, performance); a door reveal for rooms (7 to 9 s on one readable camera path, start at the threshold, end on a composed wide); a process montage when the labor adds value; a time-lapse only as process proof; a digital morph never for efficacy claims.
- **Time-lapse:** open with the mess visible, a locked wide angle, cut dead time, end on an unobstructed wide after the creator leaves frame, hold several seconds on the finished state.
- **On-screen text:** tension beats "before/after" labels (an objection like "Please don't paint it white!", skepticism plus a timeline like "4 weeks to use", despair to relief, elapsed time like "5 months btw."). Keep text off the area viewers must inspect.
- **Proof for efficacy claims:** matching angles and lighting, dates or session count, a final split-screen, unfiltered close-up movement, disclosure of styling or enhancement; never harsh before-light and flattering after-light ([DISCLOSURE.md](DISCLOSURE.md#claims-in-creator-content)).
- **The "after" by category:** beauty runs neutral, gesture, eye contact, head turn, final expression; hair needs motion (two of front, side, back, hand-through, chair spin); fitness matches pose, lens height, distance and light; rooms get one continuous after-pan with lamps on; furniture gets a texture close-up, a full wide and one functional detail; cleaning returns to the exact dirty angle, the empty room before the celebration.
- **Avoid:** a transition more dramatic than the result, a one-second after, changed lighting, a generic "wait for it", morphs for efficacy, process without payoff (label series installments), packaging as the opening shot.
- App-growth's paid version: "Before 3 to 5 s, after 3 to 5 s, fast cuts" `[appeeky]`.

## Day in the life

- "A container, not an idea." Framings that worked: occupation plus attitude plus destination; occupation plus a difficult event; life stage plus ordinary behavior; a role plus a protected pocket of time ("before the house wakes up"); routine plus an unresolved personal question; routine plus a visible imperfection; brand plus mission.
- The product arrives when the day creates a need for it, gets one narrative job, and is used before it is explained.
- **Length bands:** under 15 s for one joke or transformation; 40 to 60 s timestamped with 6 to 12 beats and one endpoint; 60 to 120 s narrated; 2 to 5 min only with live personality or conflict.

## Founder-led

Four lanes: access, authority, stakes, character; each post carries at least two. Story order is in [UGC-HOOKS.md](UGC-HOOKS.md#talking-head-openers).

## Meme edits

Formulas: a competitor roast ("Competitor is going to hate this", a frustrating behavior, one reaction asset, the alternative, one brand principle); relatable text, reaction meme, app reveal; fake-chat drama with the product in the final line; the product interface as the entertainment (a recognition game); a brand-owned recurring sitcom. Brainrot only with one stable anchor. Avoid a product reveal after an unrelated meme.

## AI UGC

- **Three layers:** real proof, then AI amplification, then disclosure and CTA.
- **Tests that worked:** one pack shot with three impossible motions while the label stays stable; one human performance dubbed into several markets; proof first, AI payoff second, back to real footage before the CTA; an entertainment-led brand world.
- **Avatar testimonials underperform** (smooth skin, mouth artifacts, no physical proof). Realism cues it reports from an Arcads tutorial: pores, oily skin, under-eye redness, focus hunting, exposure shifts.
- AI testimonials and avatars fall under the Endorsement Guides and the Reviews Rule ([DISCLOSURE.md](DISCLOSURE.md#honest-endorsements)); label AI-generated content with the platform's flag where one exists (`is_aigc` on TikTok's API, `is_ai_generated` on Instagram's).

## Clips

Start inside the conflict; put a precise money number before the explanation; borrow authority, then screen-record the tool, then one low-friction CTA; show the result before the workflow; one CTA only ("Comment 'X'", "Try it free", "Join the Discord"); move the confrontation to the opening rather than excerpting in order. Clipping-campaign economics are in [UGC-PROGRAMS.md](UGC-PROGRAMS.md#clipping-campaigns).

## Brand collabs

- Lead with the viewer's problem or the creative payoff, disclose plainly, show the product doing a real task, name a limitation, and put the CTA only after the proof.
- **Brand trips:** cast for chemistry, give each creator a different editorial territory, and publish the arc (invitation or outfit, packing and travel, room reveal, local activity, a mishap, creator crossover, the product-use moment, a reflective follow-up), with one disclosure convention across everyone ([DISCLOSURE.md](DISCLOSURE.md#where-and-how-to-disclose)).
- **Two-post experiences:** one post shows how the brand enabled the experience, a second proves it was meaningful, without repeating the code.
- **Co-branded products:** show sketches and prototypes before campaign imagery.

## Music releases

One song section in five contexts (raw performance, a comment reply, a process transformation, a scenario or POV, the release-day payoff). "Do not change the snippet before recognition has had time to form. Change the wrapper." A teaser states the emotional situation, plays the strongest lyric immediately, stops before resolution, and asks the release question or pre-save in the caption. Catalog seeding: detect fan movement, credit the creator, join natively, add an artist-only twist, follow up.

## Launch sequences

For product drops (makeup, K-beauty, fitness): a mood or mystery teaser, an information-rich reveal, the process, a creator demo, then proof. Do not make one video carry both desire and information. Launch planning is in [product-launch SKILL.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/product-launch/SKILL.md).

## App videos

- **Strongest format:** a human use case, fast screen proof, then the product name.
- **CTA matches activation:** invite a friend, create an event, log a meal, build a profile; end cards that remove friction ("Free barcode scanning", "No ads", "No account"); reward the activation itself (one app pays only for creating an event); put trust assets (an App Store feature, reviews, real data) where the viewer needs reassurance.
- **When the screen appears, two views:** app-growth's paid rule is to "show the app on screen 50%+ of the runtime" and keep the "app on screen for at least 50% of the video" `[appeeky]` `[eronred]`; lightreel's Instagram report saw successful app Reels hold the interface until the viewer understands the problem ("Show the experience, not the interface"), while its clipping report notes operators found stronger conversion when the app was named early. The repo rule stands for paid; test the later reveal for organic.

## Prove it

Each video names its format and follows that format's structure here; before/after footage keeps angle, lens height and light constant with the after held at least as long as the before; no efficacy claim uses a morph; AI-generated content is labelled and any avatar testimonial is truthful; an app video shows a person's use case and screen proof before the name and ends on a CTA that matches activation; and a format is kept only after it performs more than once.
