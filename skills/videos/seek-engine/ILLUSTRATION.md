# Illustration and characters

Drawn worlds, characters, game feel and pixel art, each kept a pure function of time. Copy `templates/pen.js` for the drawn line; the [drawing kit](#hand-drawn-line-art-ink-and-watercolour) splits base, nature and cast into three classic scripts.

## Characters in code

- **The rig's origin is the ground point under the body.** Callers pass the ground line; the rig never takes a per-call ground offset (that knob produced stilt legs). Rotate a standing character about the contact point that stays put (a heel, the base of the body), never the waist, or it sinks into the floor. Pose with a handful of named numbers (gait phase, lean, head dip, jaw, blink), defaults a neutral stance. Before a rig enters a film, pass a test sheet: standing, four walk phases, every special pose, flipped, and a 2x crop you would show full screen. Scale per shot: hero close-up ~45% of frame width, working shot 15-25%, world shot 5-9% (and then it needs company).
- **Lip sync from the voice, not a loop.** The voiceover skill's `mouth.py` writes one pose per frame (closed, soft, round, open) from the audio; map each to the rig's mouth (a line, a small oval, an "o", an open shape with a tongue) and read it in `seek`. Don't add head bob or blinking driven by the same table: the mouth carries the speech, the body acts on the beats.
- **Character sheet first.** Before any shot, draw the character once in its key poses (front, three-quarter, side), with its silhouette, palette and five or so expressions, and lock them: every later shot, and every style the character travels through, reads its proportions from that sheet. A character that drifts between shots reads as several characters.
- **A 2D rig, not redrawn frames.** Build the body from parts (torso, head, upper and lower limbs) each with a pivot, nested so a rotation carries its children. Pose = a set of joint angles, a pure function of `t`.
- **Inverse kinematics for contact.** Two-bone IK can pick knees that cross the body: give it a preferred knee direction, or use explicit knee offsets per leg (an inverted V) where side view reads cleaner. When a hand or foot must stay planted (on the ground, a rope, a handlebar), solve the two-bone chain in closed form from the target point (law of cosines for the middle joint, a fixed bend direction), so the limb follows the body without sliding. The same chain lets a tail, antenna or plant react to wind: drive the target, let IK place the joints.
- **Real motion capture on a drawn figure**: for a doodle or stick character that walks, dances, waves or jumps, retarget real motion capture instead of hand-tuning curves. Convert a BVH file once, offline, into a compact 2D clip (per-frame joint angles relative to the hips, plus the root's travel, scaled to the figure's height), then play it from `t` and choose only which clip, when and where; free sources include the CMU motion-capture database. The motion reads as human because it was. Check the clip's own licence.
- **Walks from joint curves.** A walk cycle is periodic joint-angle curves: hips and knees as phase-offset sines (legs half a cycle apart, the knee bending most as its leg passes under the body), the body bobbing twice per cycle at its lowest on each contact. Feet must not slide: derive the body's travel from the planted foot's stride, not a separate tween.
- **Acting reads in poses.** Hold a clear key pose, move to the next with anticipation and a small overshoot, and let secondary parts (hair, scarf, ears, tail) trail the main motion by 3-6 frames.
- **Turn a drawn character through its drawn views, never a 3D projection.** Front, three-quarter, side, back three-quarter, back: a turn steps through them over 0.12-0.25 s with a smear on the fast frames, the way a cartoon turns; all views face one way and a flip faces the other. Depth comes from overlap, scale and colour (farther is smaller, bluer, paler), never from rotating a flat drawing in perspective.
- **Mood tints the body.** An emotion is a face, a colour and a way of idling: pale for fear, flushed for anger, bluish for sadness, rosy for love, greenish for disgust, gold for pride, cross-faded with the face change; each emotion's idle motion locked to the film's beat.
- **Big enough to act.** In an acting shot the character is about 15-20% of the frame height at least (a medium shot), 30-50% in a close-up; a small character in a big landscape is for an establishing shot, never the whole film.
- **Contacts touch.** Feet sit on the ground, held things are drawn at the arm's tip in the arm's own space so they follow every pose, a throw leaves from the hand; check them in a full-resolution crop, where floating props show.
- **p5.brush quirks**, if you paint with it: colours mix like pigment (yellow over blue is green, a thin wash over a glow is grey), so draw light with an additive glow or a full-opacity wash on top; strokes drawn far from the origin under a zoomed camera collapse to a dot, so draw each shape around its own centre and keep long edges under a canvas-length; outline weight is in world units and grows with the zoom, so thin it for small shapes in a close-up; a NaN in a point list surfaces as an unrelated `OffscreenCanvas` error. Seed every separate element's randomness on its own key, or one moving thing re-boils everything drawn after it.
- **A mood never snaps.** A face that changes (calm to scared, scared to delighted) does it as a small event: the eyes squash shut, the body does a quick squash-and-stretch take, a mark pops by the head (a sweat drop, a spark, a heart, a "!"), then the new eyes open. Drive it from a mood timeline, `mood(t, [[t0, 'normal'], [t1, 'scared', 'sweat'],...])`, that returns the face, squint and take for any `t`.
- **Blinks and breath** keep a held character alive: a blink every 2-5 s at seeded intervals, two frames closed; a slow scale breath of ±1% on the torso.
- **Stepped acting for a hand-made feel:** sample poses at 12 drawings per second (`Math.floor(t * 12) / 12`) while the camera stays smooth. This is on twos at 24 fps; holding each pose for two output frames at 60 fps is 30 drawings per second. [buildwithhanif/claude-animation-skill, references/motion.md]

## Hand-drawn, line art, ink and watercolour

Explainers and story films lean on these looks more than any other; all of them can stay pure functions of time.

**The pen kit.** Copy `templates/pen.js` beside `index.html` (a classic `<script src="pen.js">`: pages render from `file://`, where ES modules are blocked). `pen.draw(name, reveal, (p) => {... }, { boil, t })` draws a named drawing on in mark order: `p.stroke` (tapered brush with a round nib), `p.pencil` (three broken graphite passes), `p.wash` (watercolour that blooms outward, multiplied, with a pooled edge and grain; `{ over: true }` on dark shapes), `p.ring` and `p.box` (hand-drawn, overshooting), `p.text` (a rectangular wipe over filled text, not traced letter strokes; see [writing that builds itself](KINETIC-TYPE.md#writing-that-builds-itself)). Marks are seeded from the drawing's name, never the frame, so a held drawing keeps its marks; a silent first pass counts the marks, so a mid-reveal frame is identical whatever was seeked before (tested with the smoke test's nondeterminism counter).

**The drawing kit.** Copy the three sibling scripts beside the film and load classic scripts in this order: `pen.js`, `drawkit.js`, `drawkit-nature.js`, `drawkit-cast.js`. The base defines `Draw.core`, `Draw.textures`, `Draw.lowpoly` and `Draw.fx`; nature adds `Draw.nature`, `Draw.colony` and `Draw.rigs.ant`; cast adds the other four rigs. Base-only films can omit both extensions; bean drawing also needs `pen.js`. The previous single-file copy must include the extensions when it uses their namespaces. Build heavy surfaces once outside `seek` and blit them every frame; draw what moves per frame. [buildwithhanif/claude-animation-skill, lib/ modules; local browser split]
- `Draw.textures`: `paper(W, H)`, `fibrePaper`, `lightBands(ctx, W, H, t)` (the storybook sky's drifting 40° bands), `sun(ctx, x, y, r)` (fibre-hatched, with rays), `moon`, `star4`, `grass`, `soil(W, H, 'dig'|'day'|'night')`, `nightSky(W, H)`, `fleshCells`, `filmFinish(W, H)` (returns `(ctx, frame) => ...`: cycled grain tiles, a vignette and a ±2.5% exposure flicker; the flicker trips the smoke test's pop counter, so pass `{ flicker: 0 }` or declare it with `--no-pops` and say so in the review log).
- `Draw.lowpoly`: `facetMass(W, H, polygon, { ramp, light, cell, dome, fade, haze })`, `ridge(x0, x1, base, heights)`, `skyGradient`, `facetCloud`, `RAMPS` (canyon, dusk, mesa, forest, ice, water, balloon, cloud).
- `Draw.fx`: `timeWarp`, `shake(t, [[t0, amp]])`, `burstParticles(ctx, t, t0, x, y, { kind: 'spark'|'debris'|'dust'|'confetti'|'ember' })`, `landDust`, `ring`, `flashAlpha`, `comicText(ctx, t, t0, x, y, 'POW!')`, `floatText` (damage numbers), `afterimages`, `starburst`, `heart`.
- `Draw.nature`: `sprout`, `melonLeaf`, `tendril`, `vine`, `blossom`, `bee`, `drop`. `Draw.colony`: `leaf`, `leafPiece`, `egg`, `larva`, `cocoon`, `seedGrain`, `fungus`, `roots`, `nestCanvas(spec)` (chambers and tunnels baked into soil, returning paths a character can walk).
- `Draw.rigs`: `ant.ant(ctx, x, groundY, s, { gait, stride, rear, headDip, jaw, carry, overhead, look })` (a detailed insect with a tripod gait and build-up layers, `ant.antGuides` for construction lines), `chibi.chibi(ctx, x, groundY, s, pose, look)` (a big-head person with joints, styled from a model sheet: hair, glasses, shirt, pocket and pen; walk, run, jump, cheer, point, wave, carry, eyes, brows, lip-sync mouths), `critter.critter` (a blocky action hero: run, jump, flip, squash, punch, kick, hurt, aura), `bug.bug` (a beetle enemy, `horns` for a boss), `bean.person(pen, x, groundY, s, opts, t)` (a bean-bodied person that draws itself on with the Pen; `bust`, `bubble`, `flagCloth`). Place everything else with `Draw.core.at(ctx, x, y, scale, rot, () => ...)`.
The original combined kit reported every namespace in one film with no page errors and nondeterminism 0; that source result does not validate this split. Expired particles now consume the same four seeded draws as live ones, so later particle identities do not change when an earlier particle expires. [buildwithhanif/claude-animation-skill, lib/fx.mjs; local correction]

**Three layers on every surface.** Base (the local colour, often a gentle gradient, light top-left), texture (the material's own pattern: grain, hatch, cells, fibres, bands, veins), edge (outline plus one edge effect: rim light, pooled pigment, a highlight, fine hairs). A flat fill is unfinished. When something has to give, simplify the background, never the hero.

**Line weight and detail fall off with depth:** hero 2.6-3.4 px near-black with full texture; midground 1.6-2.4 px, one texture; background 1-1.4 px or none at 40-60% opacity, grain only; construction guides 1.2-2 px in blue `#5E80CC`, dashed where projected.

**Material recipes that lift a drawing:** a sun of fibre strokes over its gradient, not a smooth disc; a night sky of fine hatch, dots and a few four-point stars; soil in cross-section with hatch, wavy strata and outlined pebbles; leaves with real lobes and pale radiating veins, never plain ellipses; insect shells with a radial gradient, form hatch on the shadow side, a specular streak, a warm rim light and short hairs; eyes with two highlights (one is a dead eye); a contact shadow under everything that stands. Warm light in a dark scene is a multiply darken over the plate plus a glow drawn on top: grading after the fact cannot add a light source.

**Build-up reveal and construction guides.** Draw blue construction shapes first (circles with `+` centres, dashed arcs, rulers with ticks), then sweep a vertical line across the subject: left of it the next stage, right of it the previous one (guides, then ink, then colour, then texture), two clipped draws per frame. Guides explain geometry and growth; fade them as the real thing takes over.

**Effects vocabulary:** burst lines on a pop or a realisation, speed lines behind fast things, impact lines under a landing, dirt clods from a dig, a dashed arc for a thrown thing's path, tally marks or a flipping day/night strip for time passing.

- **Lines that draw on:** an SVG path with `pathLength="1"`, `stroke-dasharray: 1` and `stroke-dashoffset: 1 - p`, where `p` is the draw progress; long figures draw stroke by stroke, in the order a hand would.
- **Rough edges:** rough.js (`roughness`, a fixed `seed` per shape) or p5.brush for crayon, marker and brush strokes. Never let the library pick a random seed, or each frame redraws a different line.
- **Line boil:** a hand-drawn line wobbles because each drawing differs. Change the seed at 12 drawings per second (`seed = base + Math.floor(t * 12)`, on twos at 24 fps) for a living line, or keep one seed for a clean one; never change it every frame (that reads as noise).
- **Boil and grain on DOM and SVG** (no canvas needed): an SVG filter with `feTurbulence` (baseFrequency ~0.022, 2 octaves) into `feDisplacementMap`, its `seed` set to `Math.floor(frame / 5)` (boil at 12 per second at 60 fps), applied to sketchy elements and faded out as the drawing gets more finished; film grain as a full-frame `feTurbulence` (baseFrequency 0.85, desaturated) at ~0.09 opacity in overlay blend, reseeded on twos.
- **Paper and grain:** a paper texture under everything and multiplied over colour fills; fills slightly off-register from their lines.
- **Ink and watercolour:** ink spreads as a noise-displaced mask that grows from where the brush touched; watercolour is soft-edged fills with darker edges (edge darkening), granulation noise, and colours mixed by multiply. Bake heavy noise fields once into images or tables at load.

## Game feel and action

- **Hit-stop:** choreograph in game time and render in real time. The template's `timeWarp([[gameT, seconds], ...])` freezes game time for 2-3 frames on each contact (~0.125 s on a big hit, ~0.3 s on a finisher) while sparks, rings and shake keep running on the real clock; place sound cues with `tw.real(g)` so they land on the freeze.
- **Every hit is five things on one frame:** the target flashes (cap ~0.6 or it becomes a white hole), sparks, an expanding ring, a word or number, a decaying screen shake; plus a sound pair (thump + crack). Knockback reads as a hit only with a hurt face, a back-spin and an invulnerability flicker after.
- **Squash and stretch numbers:** anticipation 1.22 for ~2 frames before a jump, stretch 0.8 in the air, squash 1.35 back to 1 over 0.18 s on landing; a dash is 0.72 stretch, a slight lean, afterimages and speed lines.
- **Particles have a grammar:** each event keeps its own shape, motion and lifetime so the viewer learns them. A pickup: small bright shards spiralling inward, very short. A hit: chips flying away from the contact, short. Dust: soft low-contrast puffs drifting up, medium. A boost: streaks pointing against the motion, while it lasts. A completion: bigger palette pieces, radial or rising, long enough to read. Every beat must still read with the particles turned off.
- **A world camera zoom of ~1.3** around the ground line: a 130 px hero on a 1920 frame reads as an icon.
- **Boss beats:** warning bars, a drop with squash, ring and debris, a roar, a readable attack the hero dodges, a combo, a hit back, a charge (darken, embers), the finisher, the health bar draining, the result card. Music sections change on absolute times computed after hit-stop.

## Pixel art

- **Draw small, scale whole.** Render to an offscreen canvas at a fixed logical size (for example 128×96), then draw it to the stage scaled by the largest integer that fits, centred, with `imageSmoothingEnabled = false` and `image-rendering: pixelated`. A non-integer scale makes uneven pixels.
- **Snap everything** to whole logical pixels: no sub-pixel positions, anti-aliasing, gradients or `shadowBlur`. One fixed palette of ~16-32 colours, every pixel from it; particles step through palette indices (white → magic colour → dark) instead of fading.
- **Smooth parameters, stepped drawing.** Animate pose parameters (an arm angle, a head tilt) with the usual curves, then quantise them to the grid and to 8-12 drawings a second (`Math.floor(t * 12) / 12`), so it reads as hand-made sprite animation while the camera and timing stay smooth.
- **Silhouette first.** A character must read as a solid shape at 1× before any detail; a 1 px rim light from the scene's light source sells it.
