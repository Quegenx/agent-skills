# Depth, footage and data

Real footage and stills, 2.5D, infinite zoom, 3D and low-poly, physics and data, each kept a pure function of time.

## Real footage and images

- **Video in seek(t):** re-encode clips all-intra (`ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -c:v libx264 -g 1 -crf 16 clip.mp4`), load each as a blob URL, set `currentTime` and await `seeked` before drawing. Or extract a JPEG sequence (`ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf fps=30 -q:v 2 frames/%05d.jpg`), swap `img.src` per frame and await `img.decode()`. Either way `seek` becomes `async`; the renderer awaits it.
- **Clips with their own cuts:** run scene detection (`ffmpeg -i clip.mp4 -vf "select='gt(scene,0.3)',showinfo" -f null -`), loop only windows with no cut inside, dissolve 12 frames at the loop seam, and stagger neighbouring tiles' phases so they never change on the same frame.
- **Green-screen footage:** key it once into an image sequence with alpha (`ffmpeg -i clip.mp4 -vf "chromakey=0x00ff00:0.12:0.06,despill=green" frames/%05d.png`), check the edges at 100% on a contrasting colour for a green halo, then composite the frames like any other layer.
- **Real clips carry their own HUDs, cursors and cuts:** keep your overlays clear of them, and keep cut-heavy clips out of moments that should feel calm.
- **A tile the same colour as the page disappears:** give it a 1 px hairline. A white object on white needs a 1 px edge and a faint shadow.
- **A held photograph is never frozen:** a still on screen for more than about a second gets a slow push or pull (scale about 1 to 1.08 with a little pan, on a long ease), and consecutive stills alternate the direction so the cut doesn't repeat the move.
- **Day-to-night relights:** two aligned shots of the same scene, cross-mixed under the control that drives it.
- **Words in depth around a speaker**. Layers back to front: the plate (padded by mirroring the edges, ~240 px a side and more on top, so a camera move never shows an edge), words that sit behind the speaker, the speaker's alpha matte (a person-matting model run once over the clip; check its frame count against the plate's), then words in front. Every caption group gets its own depth and is projected with one lens, so a camera creep or truck pulls the layers apart by parallax; nearer words are bigger and cast a bigger, softer shadow. Depth of field blurs a word by about `6.7 · |D − focus| / D` px at 1080 tall, capped at ~1.2 px for flat captions so they stay readable. A hero word set behind the head keeps at least 60% of its letters visible. Words lead the voice by ~0.2 s and step at 15 fps while the plate and matte move every frame. Captions go over the darker side of the frame; text crossing skin or light clothes gets a dark halo. A variable font gets its axes pinned in CSS (`font-variation-settings`, `font-optical-sizing: none`), or the optical size follows the scale and the word spacing shifts as the camera moves. Fine hair flickers at the matte edge.
- **Captions on supplied talking-head footage**. Every spoken phrase is one of three things: dropped (filler, stutters, self-corrections), on the rail (a plain verbatim subtitle in front, carrying most of the words), or embedded (one promoted peak word composited behind the speaker). Embeds are scarce: at most one per thought, never two on screen together, at least ~0.6 s of air between them; embedding every word only when the brief asks for a purely cinematic, mood-over-meaning treatment. The rail is an overlay on the whole film, not a band you shift the picture up to make room for: keep the composition on the true centre and keep only small critical text (a URL, a legal line) out of the caption line's path. Group words into one phrase per clause or breath: break at a pause of 0.5 s or more, a full stop, a comma followed by 0.25 s, or a discourse reset ("but", "so"), and by 6 words or 2.5 s at most; at least 2 words and 0.5 s on screen; enter ~0.08 s before the first word, leave ~0.6 s after the last or just before the next group; one group at a time; keep each word's own timestamps. Refuse or split first: several speakers or hard cuts, no clear face, no speech (a transcriber invents words over silence), footage that already has burned-in captions, a transcript that doesn't parse as language. Traps: render the captions at the footage's own frame rate or the matte drifts off the person; fade the words or the group, never both (the opacities multiply into a pop); a hidden caption in a flex column still takes up space, so position every caption absolutely; overlay blending vanishes over dark areas and screen blending over bright ones, so sample the brightness under the caption and use plain colour where it is high; a centred word meant to pass behind the speaker must be wider than the speaker by ~400 px or it disappears. Craft: the speaker reads first, the caption second; contrast comes from the scene before a box (blending with the scene's light, then a 2-3 px dark stroke with a soft shadow, then a narrow gradient bar, then dimming the plate locally by 10-15%; a solid white pill is the last resort); the caption's place follows the shot (an upper side column or a crown on a tight close-up, embedded on the back wall in a mid-shot, a lower third on the side away from the speaker in a wide); body caption cap height 3.5-5% of the frame height, hook words 7-9%; one family in two weights, emphasis by weight, one accent colour or size (1.3-1.6×), never italics; the word stagger sets the tone (40 ms urgent, 80 ms conversational, 150 ms documentary, 250 ms and more ceremonial); emphasis is rationed (about 70% plain, 20% lifted, 8% full, 2% climax held ~1.5 s); show 70-85% of the words, folding self-corrections and cutting filler and sound tags like [laughs]; a deliberate pause of 1.5 s or more gets no lingering caption; break the rhythm about once every 30 s (a phrase from the other side, one word at twice the size, a beat with no caption); letterbox or pillarbox bars are caption space.
- **Photos and clips repainted as brushwork** (a photo painting itself, a short clip as a living painting): route to the `p5-paint-animation` community skill (`npx skills add heygen-com/hyperframes-community-skills --skill p5-paint-animation`). Its rules hold for any painted footage: crop to the subject first (a small face never resolves); paint coarse to fine with the subject first, then pull the camera out; build the stroke layout once from the first frame and only recolour it per frame, so the painting doesn't boil while the subject moves; paint at 12 fps; brighten dark footage with gamma only (a saturation boost turns shadow noise into blotches of paint); a camera that follows a writing pen lags it exponentially (gain ~0.14) with its height locked to the baseline, never the pen tip.

## Illustrated stills in 2.5D

When characters or painted worlds come from generated stills (video-brief pattern 14): key characters off a flat `#00FF00` field yourself, upscale every layer, estimate background depth (Depth Anything) and displace by it for per-pixel parallax, place layers at real depths behind one pinhole camera, and move characters with mesh warps (breath, hair, cloth) plus key poses on twos. Supersample, cache frames to disk, and split long renders across processes by time range (`render.mjs full --range`).

## Infinite zoom

A zoom that keeps going (into a poster, through a page, down to a detail) is a stack of nested images, each one the region the next zoom level shows. Upscale each level with an AI upscaler (Real-ESRGAN, 2x or 4x) so it stays sharp at full screen, align each level exactly inside the one before it, and drive one log-space zoom across the whole stack; cross-blend each level into the next over the few frames where both are on screen at the same size.

## 3D and depth

- Three.js is fine when the scene is set from `t` every frame and any shader time uniform is `t`.
- **Build the set, then shoot it.** Block the scene in gray (shapes, scale, camera path, timing) and approve that before materials and lighting; list which models are procedural and which are supplied, and never pull a model whose licence you haven't checked. An interactive scene recorded as video is labelled a recording, not a film.
- Never set `opacity` or `filter` on a `preserve-3d` element: it flattens and both faces show. Fade a wrapper instead.
- A child with `visibility: visible` shows through a hidden parent: use `inherit`.
- Depth comes from overlap, scale, parallax and focus; use it sparingly and coordinated, never as random floating.
- **Build once, set from t.** Create every mesh, geometry, material and texture at load; `seek` only sets positions, rotations, colours and uniforms. Allocating inside `seek` leaks GPU memory over a long render and slows every frame after it; removing a mesh from the scene does not free it (`geometry.dispose()`, `material.dispose()`, `texture.dispose()`). Many copies of one object (a crowd, debris, a field) are one `InstancedMesh`. Keep a small shared material set and vary colour, roughness and scale before adding another.
- **Colour space.** `renderer.outputColorSpace = THREE.SRGBColorSpace`, and mark colour textures `texture.colorSpace = THREE.SRGBColorSpace`; never mark normal, roughness, metalness or data textures sRGB. Getting it wrong reads as washed-out or muddy textures. Choose tone mapping and exposure once for the whole film, never per object.
- **Light it with one recipe.** *Soft diorama:* a low hemisphere fill, one directional key with shadows, warm key against a cooler fill, a blob or contact shadow under the hero. *Graphic:* strong ambient, one key, emissive accents only on what matters. *Night:* low environment light, a moon or rim light, local lights only at landmarks, fog for depth, exposure set so surfaces still read. Fake extra light with emissive materials rather than adding shadow-casting point lights.
- **Shadows explain grounding, height or danger.** Few casters and receivers, the directional light's shadow camera fitted tightly to what the shot shows, the smallest map that holds the edge, none on particles, small props or distant scenery. A crowd gets blob shadows. Acne at a shallow light angle takes `shadow.normalBias`, at the cost that "shadows may appear distorted" (https://threejs.org/docs/pages/LightShadow.html).
- **Imported models (glTF).** Normalise scale and forward axis once at load; clone a skinned model with `SkeletonUtils.clone`, never reuse one live hierarchy twice; check texture sizes and the licence. Drive a model's own animation from t with `mixer.setTime(t)` on its `AnimationMixer`, never `mixer.update(delta)`, which carries state between frames.
- **Signs and logos in a 3D world:** draw the real logo file onto a canvas texture on a plane and give it a subtle bloom; keep the ground under a title dark enough that reflections never show through the letters; a rain or particle scene needs light denoising before it compresses into a GIF (8 fps, ~540 px wide).
- **A follow camera without carried state.** Aim at the subject a little ahead (`pos(t + 0.3)`) and place the camera on the subject's path a little behind (`pos(t - 0.25)` plus the offset), so it trails and leads like a damped chase camera while staying a pure function of t. Never parent the camera to a jittery body. Check that it never passes through geometry or shows the edge of the world.

- **Post passes and helpers with their own clock** (checked in three.js r186 source). `composer.render()` with no argument reads a wall-clock timer for its delta: always pass a delta, and drive time uniforms yourself. `FilmPass` adds each delta to its `time` uniform (state): set `uniforms.time.value = t` before rendering. `GlitchPass` re-seeds from `Math.random` every render and fires on a frame counter: write the glitch as your own pass seeded from `t` (`TECHNIQUES.md`, a glitch accent). `AfterimagePass` feeds the last frame back and `TAARenderPass` with `accumulate` builds up over still frames: replay them at a fixed step (`SHADERS.md`) or leave them out. `MeshSurfaceSampler` uses `Math.random` unless given `setRandomGenerator(seeded)`.
- **Text and lines in a 3D scene.** `CSS2DRenderer` and `CSS3DRenderer` (examples) place real DOM elements at 3D points or planes, so labels stay sharp, follow the camera and remain readable to the smoke test: render them in `seek` right after the WebGL frame, with the same camera. WebGL draws every `Line` 1 px wide whatever `linewidth` says: a visible stroke is `Line2` with `LineMaterial` (width in pixels, `resolution` set to the canvas size), or a thin tube mesh.

### React Three Fiber and drei

When the product's site already has an R3F scene (reuse its components), or a studio look drei gives for free:
- **Drive it from t.** `createRoot(canvas).configure({ frameloop: 'never', dpr: 1, size, gl: { preserveDrawingBuffer: true } })`, then `advance(t)` in `seek`: in `frameloop: 'never'` R3F sets `clock.elapsedTime` to exactly the `t` you pass. A bare `createRoot` needs `extend(THREE)` first (`<Canvas>` does it), or it throws "not part of the THREE namespace". Make `seek` await a ready promise while Suspense loads the environment.
- **Advance each frame twice.** drei passes that render the scene into a buffer (transmission, contact shadows) can run before the `useFrame` that poses the objects and see the last frame's pose; measured: one advance differed by 2-3/255 depending on the previous seek, two cut it to about 0.005.
- **Seed `Math.random` while the tree mounts.** Float, Sparkles, Stars, Cloud and AccumulativeShadows draw random values at mount, so each render worker builds a different scene and the film flickers between workers; measured: two separate pages differed unseeded and matched exactly seeded. Restore it after mount.
- **Safe on a set clock:** Environment with Lightformers (studio light without an HDR download), ContactShadows, MeshTransmissionMaterial, MeshDistortMaterial, MeshWobbleMaterial, Sparkles motion, CameraShake. **Carry state, don't film them as they are:** Trail, TrailTexture, the controls, Bounds, MotionPathControls, `useAnimations` (drive its mixer with `setTime(t)`), and AccumulativeShadows (bake all its frames before the first seek). Text (troika) fetches a font from a CDN unless given a local file: prefer DOM text over the canvas.
- **Reused components dispose on unmount.** "React Three Fiber will attempt to free resources for you by calling `object.dispose()`, if present, on all unmounted objects"; a site's components that share cached or global assets need `dispose={null}` (it covers the whole subtree), and "Primitives will not dispose of the object they carry on unmount" (https://r3f.docs.pmnd.rs/api/objects).
- **Glass needs something to refract:** transmission over a black ground reads as black glass; put a lit backdrop, a gradient or bright Lightformers behind it.

## Faceted low-poly and extruded type

- **Faceted low-poly poster:** flat vector world, every mass cut into lit triangles from a colour ramp, big cells (50-75 px) with low jitter (~0.22; small cells read as noise), layers fading into haze back to front, columns split into a lit and a shadow side, and the moving character the only outlined thing. Made to loop: every motion periodic in the loop length.
- **3D extruded type around a person:** glyphs on a cylinder around the subject (width = cos(angle)), extrusion as 1 px layers along the inward normal, the front half above a person matte and the back half below it, a flat ring (vertical radius ~11% of horizontal), a slow spin that lands facing camera and holds ~2 s.

## Physics and data

- Physics (falling notes, bouncing balls, collisions) must not be stepped per frame inside `seek`. With a physics library (Matter.js, cannon-es), run the simulation once at load with a fixed timestep and seeded start positions, record every body's position and angle per frame into a table, and have `seek(t)` read (and interpolate) that table. Either use closed-form motion (a parabola, a damped spring) or precompute the simulation once, deterministically, into a table indexed by time.
- A graph and the dot that rides it share one function, or the motion feels fake.
- "Every note comes from a visible collision": compute the collision times first, then generate the music from them, so picture and sound share one source.
- Counters change on the landing frame of the thing that caused them, never a beat later.
- Motion must not lie: a pulsing "live" dot beside "systems operational" on a static page implies a live feed that does not exist. Animate only what is true of the product.
