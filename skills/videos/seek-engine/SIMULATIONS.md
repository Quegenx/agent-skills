# Simulations and documentary worlds

Swarms, agents, machines, maps, terrain and crowds. A simulation that has to integrate is precomputed once, deterministically, and indexed by time; everything else is closed form. Films that import three.js are bundled first with `scripts/bundle.mjs`.

## Simulations: swarms, agents, machines

When the idea is a simulation (hundreds of agents with their own rules, a machine the camera flies through) rather than type and UI, write it as a canvas or three.js page with the same `seek(t)` contract. Lessons from three shipped sims:

- **Simulate once, look up per frame.** Births, flights, gaits, collisions and steering run once at load at 60 Hz into typed arrays; `seek(t)` only reads them. Seed every random choice at setup.
- **Real text as terrain.** Wrap every word of a page in a span, measure the boxes once at load, and hit-test against them: a foot that lands on a word lights it in that agent's colour.
- **Agents that arrive on time.** A "hunter" is steered toward its target so it reaches it at a set time (snapped to a beat); a thread from a world-space point to a screen-space panel (sagging a little) carries what it found.
- **Procedural legs:** each leg has a rest position relative to the body; when the planted foot drifts past a threshold (smaller while idle), it steps to the rest position plus a lead proportional to speed, in two alternating groups, ~0.075 s per step; the knee is the two-bone IK solution farther from the body centre and lifts outward while the foot is in the air. Feet that land are hit-tested against the terrain.
- **Steering that looks alive:** turn toward the target with a capped turn rate (~8 rad/s), slow down when facing away (`× max(0.3, cos(angle error))`), ease speed with an exponential approach, a small sinusoidal wander that fades near the target, short random dwells on arrival.
- **Fast hit tests:** put every word box (or object) into a spatial hash grid of ~80 px cells once; a query checks one cell.
- **The evidence wall:** each find pops a red box around its word with a numbered tag (overshoot scale, ~0.2 s); a curved thread (quadratic Bezier, drawn by length with a dot at its head) runs from the find to its slot on a panel; the value types into the slot behind solid redaction bars that lift character by character; when the last slot fills, the wall behind dims to ~0.6 so the panel reads as the answer.
- **Lines that survive a pull-back:** draw world-space lines at `max(w, w * 0.45 / zoom)` so threads and legs stay visible when the camera zooms far out; connect agents closer than a set distance with alpha falling off with distance.
- **Doubling on the beat:** one agent, then 2, 4 ... 256, each generation on a beat or every two beats; the camera pulls back from one page to the whole wall as the count grows.
- **Explain, don't decorate:** chapters, a one-line caption per step and labels with real (rounded, textbook) numbers turn a simulation into an explanation. A camera that flies *through* the object is worth more than ten orbits; a clipping-plane cutaway shows the inside; an exploded view that comes apart and back together shows the parts.
- **Labels on a moving 3D object:** project each label's anchor every frame, hide it when off-screen or behind the camera, place the text in a column on its side, push labels apart to at least ~70 px vertically, and draw a leader line from text to anchor. When the object explodes, offset each anchor with its part.
- **Chapter focus:** the flows or parts the current chapter talks about burn bright (×1.2) while the rest dim (×0.3). Give the camera a small vibration tied to the machine's power, bigger on the relight.
- **Rotation at a changing speed:** the angle is the integral of the speed, so sum it from 0 to t at a fixed small step inside `seek` (pure, if slower) or precompute a table at load; never accumulate across frames.
- **three.js traps:** bloom eats detail, so keep emissive and additive layers under ~1.0 and raise them only for the hero moment; one NaN pixel blacks out the whole frame through bloom, so clamp every `pow()` base in shaders; glossy metal under a low sun streaks white, so use roughness ≥ 0.45 and envMapIntensity ≈ 0.5 on big surfaces; render with `preserveDrawingBuffer: true` so screenshots read the frame; ACES tone mapping; when overlays cover part of the frame, shift the projection centre into the visible area with `camera.setViewOffset`, and do punch-ins the same way (a crisp re-render of a sub-rectangle) rather than scaling the canvas.
- **Bundling three.js:** it ships only as ES modules, and pages render from `file://`, where module imports are blocked. Write the film as `src.html` plus `src/*.js` with normal imports, then `npm i three esbuild` and `node scripts/bundle.mjs src.html index.html` (every `<script type="module" src>` is bundled and inlined). Tested: a three.js scene bundles, renders, and passes the smoke test's nondeterminism counter. Re-bundle after every edit.

## Documentary worlds: maps, terrain, crowds, light

For history and science films set in a real place, the pattern of a five-minute battle film made entirely in code:

- **The narration is the timeline.** Write the script as scenes of beats, each beat a line with its silence before (`pre`), after (`gap`) and at the scene end (`tail`); voice it, then build every scene's start and every beat's time from the measured lengths. Shots refer to beats by id (`B('t3')`), so re-voicing one line retimes everything after it.
- **Shots return descriptions, not pixels.** Each shot is a function of time returning the camera, light, crowds, effects, overlay and finish; a dissolve renders the outgoing and incoming shot separately and mixes them in the final pass, so neither has to know about the other.
- **Real ground.** Elevation tiles are free with no key: AWS Terrarium, `https://s3.amazonaws.com/elevation-tiles-prod/terrarium/{z}/{x}/{y}.png`, height in metres = `R*256 + G + B/256 - 32768`. The old line called these SRTM-derived at about 38 m at zoom 13. The provider combines DEM sources: distinguish tile spacing (about 19.109 m at the equator, 12.502 m at this film's 49.1375 degrees latitude at zoom 13) from this source's approximately 38.482 m, 512-wide resampled grid and the underlying DEM accuracy. The exact downloaded tiles' contributors were not established here; retain applicable provider/data attribution rather than claiming one DEM. [Tilezen data sources](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/data-sources.md), [AWS Terrain Tiles](https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/), [Tilezen attribution](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md). Bake the field into a compact table once; exaggerate the vertical (×2-3) so a gentle plateau reads on screen, and say so in the credits. Coasts, lakes and rivers for maps: Natural Earth (public domain), clipped to the area.
- **Real light.** Compute the sun's azimuth and elevation for the real date, place and hour, and stage around it (the famous sunrise behind the hill can be literal). Keep light as named presets (night, fog at dawn, sunrise, midday, sunset, dusk, map) holding every lighting, fog, sky and grade value, and blend between presets over time instead of animating twenty numbers by hand.
- **A dotted globe** (`npm i cobe`, about 5 KB): a WebGL globe of land dots with glowing markers and arcs between cities. Its v2 core has no clock of its own: `createGlobe(canvas, opts)` once, then `globe.update({ phi: spin(t), theta, markers, arcs, scale })` in `seek(t)` draws the frame synchronously (the README's `onRender` loop that adds 0.01 to `phi` each frame is the interactive pattern; never carry `phi` between frames). Grow a marker by updating its `size` from `t`; an arc appears whole (its colour is RGB, no alpha), so add it on its beat, timed to a sound. Markers can bind DOM elements through CSS anchor positioning, so city labels stay readable DOM text that follows the globe. (Read from cobe's source; not yet run here.)
- **deck.gl for data on a map**. It runs its own `requestAnimationFrame` loop and a wall-clock timeline for `transitions`, so a film never uses `transitions` or view-state interpolators: in `seek(t)` set the view state and every time-driven prop from `t` (TripsLayer `currentTime`, a `DataFilterExtension` range), then call `deck.redraw('seek')`, which draws now whatever its flags say. Wait before the first seek until `deck.isInitialized` and every layer's `isLoaded` is true, and a MapLibre or Mapbox basemap has fired `idle` (tiles load over the network: pre-load the area at every zoom the camera passes through, and credit the map data). `preserveDrawingBuffer` is on by default. (Read from deck.gl's source; not yet run here.)
- **Maps that move.** A campaign map draws each route with a progress value along a smoothed path, marching dots along the finished part, date stamps that fade in on their beat, region names in wide-spaced type; zoom in log space from the continent down to the field, and let the relief map turn into the real terrain.
- **Labels pinned to the world.** Project a 3D point to the screen every frame and draw the label with a leader tick above it, so names stay on their villages while the camera moves; drop labels that leave the frame.
- **Crowds as formations.** A unit is files × ranks along a path, each person offset by a seeded jitter and animated with their own phase (never in step), the sprite chosen by facing (back view when walking away, mirrored when moving left on screen), flags and drums at fixed slots, volleys rippling back rank by rank, a rout as a spread that grows with time. Draw the figures once at load into an atlas (procedural, deterministic), then place instanced billboards. Everything stays a pure function of time, so frames render in any order and in parallel.
- **Camera like a crew.** Catmull-Rom through camera keys with eased ends, a handheld shake from summed sines (small, and none on a crane), a crane that rises off the ground as the moment opens up. Ignite a line of torches as a wave travelling toward the camera.
- **Bake what only changes with its input.** Terrain shadows, field textures and the sprite atlas are rebuilt only when the sun or the data changes; caching on the input keeps the render a pure function of time.
- **A painterly finish:** a Kuwahara filter at half resolution blended over the frame, a canvas weave, fine grain, a vignette, 2.35:1 bars.
- **Credit the simplifications** on screen or in the README (exaggerated relief, full-dress uniforms, schematic units) and round contested numbers.
