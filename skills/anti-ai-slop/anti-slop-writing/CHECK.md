# The check

Run it on every edit or draft before returning it. Any "no" means fix and run it again.

**Meaning and voice**
1. Is every claim, number, name, quote and example from the source or the user? Missing facts marked `[NEED: ...]`, not invented? In a long document, does it contradict itself anywhere (numbers, dates, names)? Quote each problem and say where it is. Do the links work [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copy-editing]?
2. Would the writer recognize it as their own voice: vocabulary, rhythm, bluntness, humor, uncertainty kept?
3. Were strong human sentences left alone, and is the amount cut proportional to the slop actually present? For each change, "is it measurably better, or just different? Revert changes you can't give a reason for" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copywriting].
4. Does it lead with what the reader needs, keeping setup that adds context or character?

**Patterns** (search the text, don't trust memory)
5. Search for: "not ", "isn't", "no ", "without", "?", ":", "—", "–", " -- ". Is each hit free of contrast reveals, negation lists, self-answered questions, colon reveals and dash clusters?
6. Read every sentence over 20 words. Any trailing pile-on after the main claim? "If it has more than two commas, check for a trailing pile-on" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copywriting]. The other view: copy-editing caps sentences at "Max 3 conjunctions per sentence" and "No more than 25 words (usually)" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copy-editing]; here 20 words is a trigger to read the sentence, not a length cap.
7. Count per section: at most one fragment run, one list of three, and no two capped words from the vocabulary list in one paragraph.
8. No openers, filler transitions, interpretive asides, recap endings, fake-profound kickers or chatbot residue?
9. Formatting: no decorative bold, emoji headings, inline-header bullets, title case, all-caps emphasis, quote clusters or exclamation marks in body text? In a post or email: no broetry, engagement bait, subject-line bait, stock opener or sign-off filler ([TELLS.md](TELLS.md#7-social-posts-and-email))?
10. For Hebrew: register held throughout, one gender approach, no calques or bureaucratic forms from [HEBREW.md](HEBREW.md), gershayim intact?

**The read**
11. Swap test: would the headline or opening line work unchanged for someone else? If so, is it fixed or flagged?
12. Read it aloud. Anywhere it sounds like a press release or a LinkedIn post? A "punchy" LinkedIn launch post still has no contrast reveal, no em dash, no broetry and no bait closer, and its hook is specific [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, social, evals].
13. Second-read test: read each sentence once at speech pace; "if you re-read to parse it, name the subject, the action, and the consequence (kill metaphor verbs and pronouns reaching back several sentences)" [Vercel, writing-guidelines].
14. No new tic introduced by the fixes (every reveal turned into ", because...", every list into "There's nothing to...")?
15. Second-generation scan: "Check the last line of every section for a tidy closer, look for wisdom-shaped sentences, and compare section lengths. If they're all about the same, the draft has perfect balance" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copywriting] ([TELLS.md](TELLS.md#6-second-generation-tells)).

**Output**
16. Edit: full text plus a short What changed. Point edit: only the changed lines, each with a one-line reason. Detect: each pattern named with a quoted line and a fix, no rewrite, no score, no claim about who wrote it.
