# Plain words

Pompous words and the plain ones that replace them, for prose and marketing copy. `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copy-editing]` (`references/plain-english-alternatives.md`, MIT), copied as the source gives it, with its spellings. The source names its own sources: "Plain English Campaign A-Z of Alternative Words (2001), Australian Government Style Manual (2024), plainlanguage.gov". plainlanguage.gov now redirects to https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language; the source still gives the old address. The vocabulary tells and padding phrases this list extends are in [TELLS.md](TELLS.md#3-vocabulary).

## How to use the list

- **Swap only when the plain word says the same thing.** "Replace complex or pompous words with plain English alternatives" `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copy-editing]`. A swap that changes the meaning, or the writer's chosen word, is not an edit: copy-editing's own reading-taste rule says the author's vocabulary "is a voice decision", and its examples "stuff" and "ship" stay.
- **Interface text follows [anti-slop-copy MICROCOPY.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/anti-ai-slop/anti-slop-copy/MICROCOPY.md#rules), not this list.** Several pairs would rename a control's verb: "delete → remove, cross out", "submit → send, give", "select → choose", "complete → fill in, finish", "option → choice". On a control the product's own nouns and verbs stay ([anti-slop-copy MICROCOPY.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/anti-ai-slop/anti-slop-copy/MICROCOPY.md#start-from-what-exists): "New copy uses those words"), and "select" is MICROCOPY's word when both touch and pointer apply.
- **The source disagrees with itself once.** Its SKILL.md says "Implement → Set up"; this list says "implement → carry out, do". Pick the one that matches the sentence.
- **Spelling.** The list is British (authorise, finalise, utilise, scrutinise, visualise, endeavour). Write the spelling the rest of the text uses.
- **A plain word is not yet a fact.** Swapping "facilitate" for "help" fixes the register; a vague claim still needs the fact behind it ([TELLS.md](TELLS.md#3-vocabulary)).

## Phrases to cut

"These phrases often add nothing. Delete them" `[coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copy-editing]`:

- a total of
- absolutely
- actually
- all things being equal
- as a matter of fact
- at the end of the day
- at this moment in time
- basically
- currently (when "now" or nothing works)
- I am of the opinion that (use: I think)
- in due course (use: soon, or say when)
- in the final analysis
- it should be understood
- last but not least
- obviously
- of course
- quite
- really
- the fact of the matter is
- to all intents and purposes
- very

## Prove it

- Every swap keeps the sentence's meaning; none touches a quoted line, a name, a control label or a word the writer chose on purpose.
- A cut phrase leaves a sentence that still reads; "I am of the opinion that" became "I think", and "in due course" became "soon" or the date.
- The edited text uses one spelling system throughout.
