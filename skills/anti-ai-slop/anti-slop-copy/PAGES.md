# Pages

## The hero: problem, picture, path

Someone acts when three things line up, and the hero has to carry all three:
1. **The problem they feel,** in their words. They should think "that's me".
2. **The better state,** concrete enough to picture.
3. **Why this step gets them there:** the mechanism or proof, and the CTA that crosses the gap.

| Beat | Usually lives in | Example (shape only, facts must be real) |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Subhead or a problem-led headline | "Your invoices go out late because they wait for approval in email." |
| Picture | Headline | "Invoices approved and sent the day work ends." |
| Path | CTA plus one line of proof | "Send your first invoice" + "Approvals happen in Slack, in one click." |

The check: "Can a visitor understand what this is and why they should care within 5 seconds?" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, cro]. The subhead "Expands on headline; Adds specificity; 1-2 sentences max" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copywriting]. The headline carries the most weight: Ogilvy held that five times as many people read the headline as the body (*Confessions of an Advertising Man*, 1963, as cited by [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, marketing-council]); that is his claim, not a measured figure here.

Headline shapes that work when every slot holds a real specific:
- outcome without the pain: "File your VAT return without opening a spreadsheet"
- outcome by mechanism: "Know which companies visit your site, from the domains in your logs"
- outcome in a time: "Your refund in 10 days"
- category for audience: "Analytics for Shopify stores"
- what you no longer need: "You don't need an SEO team to rank for your own product"
- the category that does the different thing: "The CRM that fills itself from your inbox"
- the benefit itself, plainly: "Sound clear in online meetings"
- proof, if real and sourced: "4,200 accounting firms close their month with it"
- input to outcome: "Turn {input} into {outcome}", as in "Turn your hard-earned sales into repeat customers" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copywriting]
- product type, audience and use: "{product type} for {audience} to {use}", as in "An online whiteboard for teams to ideate and brainstorm together" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copywriting]

Shapes that read as AI and stay out: "Stop X. Start Y.", "Finally, X that doesn't suck", "What if you could X?", "Say goodbye to X", "X, reimagined", "The future of X". marketingskills' copy-frameworks offers the first three as formulas; this repo keeps them out (its own ai-tells reference bans "Imagine..." and self-answered questions too).

Run the headline through the swap and "Now you can..." tests. Write three to five candidates from different angles (outcome, problem, audience, difference, proof) and keep the ones that survive, never the first one by default.

## A research-led headline pass

[more skills, copywriting-playbook.md] An optional checklist asks whether the headline gives a useful benefit, an actual reason to act now, a distinction competitors cannot also claim and concrete detail. Urgency and numbers still need evidence. Start from a real buyer question, a specific recurring problem or an agreed testimonial fragment where it fits; the existing [headline forms](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/ad-creative/COPY-FORMULAS.md#headlines-hooks-ctas-and-testing) already cover number and question shapes. The guide prefers digits in numeric headlines as a style choice.

**Two exploration defaults.** [more skills, copywriting-playbook.md] proposes 10 or more candidates, shortlisting with that checklist and using data where testing is possible. [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copywriting] uses the three-to-five default above; keep it unless wider exploration is requested. Neither changes [point-edit scope](DRAFTING.md#diagnose-the-situation-first), and experiment design belongs to [ab-testing](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/ab-testing/SKILL.md). The playbook's three-second message target is a practitioner heuristic alongside the existing five-second comprehension check, not a measured attention limit.

## Know which reader you're talking to

The same claim reads differently by risk tolerance. "Ship in a weekend" is speed to a startup and immaturity to an enterprise buyer; "SOC 2 Type II and an uptime SLA" is safety to one and bloat to the other. When a page serves both, don't average the message into mush: split the page or the path, and let each reader get their version of the truth.

The same product, two value propositions (shape only, facts must be real) [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copywriting]: for a startup, "Ship your first integration this afternoon. Pay by card and skip the sales call."; for an enterprise, "SOC 2 Type II, 99.99% uptime SLA, and a named implementation lead. Roll out with confidence."

## Sections, built from what the reader doubts

Order sections in the order the visitor's doubts arise, and include only the ones this product can fill with real material:

| Section | Answers | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | "Do they get my situation?" | The specific friction, its cost in time or money |
| How it works | "Is it complicated?" | The real number of steps, each a verb and an outcome |
| Proof | "Does it work for people like me?" | Named customers, real numbers, quotes with a name and role. In a proof bar, "Customer logos (recognizable > many)" and a "Star rating with review count" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copywriting] |
| Benefits | "What changes for me?" | Each benefit: the outcome, how it happens, proof if any |
| Comparison | "Why not the alternative?" | Honest, specific differences, including where you're not the fit. Against competitors or against "status quo (spreadsheets, manual processes)" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copywriting]; full pages in [comparison-pages](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/comparison-pages/SKILL.md) |
| Objections / FAQ | "What's the catch?" | Questions real buyers ask (from sales, support). The usual ones: "Price/value concerns / Will this work for my situation? / Implementation difficulty / What if it doesn't work?" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, cro]. "Admitting a weakness ('We're not the cheapest, but...') can increase trust and differentiation" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, marketing-psychology], when the weakness is true |
| Pricing | "What will it cost me?" | Real plans, what's in each, who each is for |
| Final CTA | "What now?" | The action, the risk reducer that's true (trial, refund, no card), with its conditions beside it (16 CFR 239.3(b), [offer-design FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/offer-design/FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md#guarantees)) |
| Use cases or "for whom" | "Is this for someone like me?" | Real roles and situations, each with what they do with it |
| Case study | "Has it worked somewhere real?" | A named customer: the problem, what they did, the measured result |
| Demo or product tour | "Show me" | The real product working: a short video, screenshots, an interactive tour |
| Integrations | "Does it fit my tools?" | The tools it really connects to |
| Founder story | "Who's behind this, and why?" | The real reason it exists, with specifics |
| Guarantee | "What if it doesn't work for me?" | The real terms: trial length, refund window, cancellation, stated beside the claim (16 CFR 239.3(b), [offer-design FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/offer-design/FTC-PRICING-CLAIMS.md#guarantees)) |
| Pricing preview | "Roughly what does it cost?" | On a non-pricing page, "Starting price or 'from $X/mo'" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copywriting], the real one |
| Security and compliance | "Is it safe for us?" | Certifications and controls that really exist (an enterprise page's section in [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copywriting]) |
| ROI | "Will it pay for itself?" | "Show the math, not just the conclusion" and "Use conservative estimates (executives discount inflated numbers)" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, sales-enablement]; calculators in [sales-enablement](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/sales-enablement/SKILL.md) |

Vary the composition: not every section is a heading, a subhead and three cards. A section can be one sentence and a screenshot. Let the content decide the shape.

## Page types

- **Homepage:** several kinds of visitors. Lead with the broadest true value, then clear paths by intent (try it, see pricing, talk to sales). Specific, not generic, even when broad. "Handle both 'ready to buy' and 'still researching'" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, cro].
- **Landing page:** one source, one message, one CTA. The headline matches the ad or email that brought them. The whole argument on one page.
- **Pricing page:** answers "which plan is me?". Name who each plan is for, what's in it, what happens at the limits. Recommend a plan only if it's a real recommendation for a stated reader. Not always three tiers with a highlighted middle. The other view, with its persuasion rationale, is in [growth-ideas PSYCHOLOGY.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/growth-ideas/PSYCHOLOGY.md); tier and price decisions are [pricing-strategy](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/pricing-strategy/SKILL.md).
- **Feature page:** the feature, the outcome, a real example or use case, a way to try it.
- **About page:** why this exists and who's behind it, with real details (when, where, why, what you've shipped). No mission-statement filler. Still has a next step.
- **Launch or changelog:** what changed, for whom, how to use it today. One hero change, then the rest briefly. No "we're excited to announce". From merged code to the entry and its drafts: [product-launch SHIPPED-CHANGES.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/product-launch/SHIPPED-CHANGES.md).
- **Blog post:** "Contextual CTAs matching content topic" and "Inline CTAs at natural stopping points" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, cro]. landing-page-ui does it differently: its post template (back link, title, byline, article blocks) has no inline calls ([landing-page-ui CONTENT-PAGES.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/web-ui/landing-page-ui/CONTENT-PAGES.md#blog)); in the owner's sites that is the design.
- **Reference or informational page:** show the method or the sources behind the content where a sales page would show testimonials ([freescan] checks trust signals by page role).
- **Lead magnet page:** [lead-magnets](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/lead-magnets/SKILL.md).

## Writing each part

- **Problem section:** name the moment the problem bites and what it costs. No "We've all been there".
- **Benefits:** a heading that is the outcome, one or two sentences on how, proof if you have it. Vary the shape; not every benefit is a card with an icon.
- **How it works:** as many steps as there really are, each a plain verb and what the person gets: "Connect your bank (about 2 minutes)".
- **Testimonials:** only real ones, with a name, role and company the person agreed to show. The useful ones carry a specific before and after; cut "Great product!". Formats: "quote cards, video, tweet embeds" [coreyhaines31/marketingskills, copywriting]. A results claim needs typical results behind it: "Endorsements claiming specific results usually will be interpreted to mean that the endorser's experience reflects what others can also expect. Statements like 'Results not typical' or 'Individual results may vary' won't change that interpretation" ([FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)); without proof that the result is typical, show "what the generally expected results" are. Collecting reviews only from happy customers misleads if it "substantially skews the favorability" (same page). Star ratings in search results have their own rules: [seo STRUCTURED-DATA.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/seo/STRUCTURED-DATA.md#reviews-and-ratings).
- **FAQ:** only questions people actually ask. If you don't know them, ask the user or leave the section out. Call and support transcripts are where they come from ([content-strategy IDEATION.md](https://github.com/Quegenx/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/marketing/content-strategy/IDEATION.md#call-transcripts)). An FAQ no longer earns a Google rich result: "This feature will no longer appear in Google Search starting May 7, 2026" (Google's notice of 2026-05-08), and on 2026-06-15 Google removed the documentation because "The FAQ rich result feature is no longer shown in Google Search results" ([Search Central changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)). marketingskills' copy-frameworks still lists an FAQ as "Good for SEO".
- **Headings:** make a claim ("Approvals happen where your team already talks"), not a label ("Features").

## Transitions

Use them where they help someone skim, not on every sentence. Plain links ("So", "But", "For example", "That means") beat formal ones ("Moreover", "Furthermore", "That being said"). If the paragraph order is right, most transitions can go.

## Prove it

- The hero names the problem, the better state and the path, and a newcomer can say what the product is within 5 seconds.
- Every section on the page answers a doubt this product can fill with real material; none is there because a template had it.
- Every testimonial is a real, named person who agreed, and any results claim sits beside the generally expected result.
- No guarantee, trial or price claim without its terms beside it.
- The FAQ holds questions buyers actually asked.
