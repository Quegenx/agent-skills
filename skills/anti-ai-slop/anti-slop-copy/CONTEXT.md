# The product context file

One file of real facts that every piece of copy draws from, so nothing has to be invented and nobody repeats themselves. It lives at `.agents/product-context.md` in the project (if an older marketing context file exists, read it and offer to move it).

## Making it

- **Draft from what exists (recommended):** read the README, the live site, landing pages, docs, pricing, package metadata, reviews the user can share. Fill what the sources support, mark everything else `[ASK]`, then show the draft and ask what's wrong or missing.
- **From scratch:** go section by section in conversation, a few questions at a time, confirming each before moving on.
- Push for verbatim customer language. Exact phrases from reviews, calls and tickets are worth more than any polished description.
- Ask for examples: "What's the number one frustration that brings people to you?" beats "What problem do you solve?".
- Skip sections that don't apply (personas for a consumer app, for example).

## Sections

```markdown
# Product context
Version: v1 | Last updated: YYYY-MM-DD

## Product
One line. What it does (2-3 sentences). Category (the shelf buyers look on). Type and business model. Pricing.

## Audience
Who buys (company type, size, stage or consumer profile). Who uses it. The main job they hire it for. Specific use cases.

## Buyers (B2B, if several people decide)
User, champion, decision maker, budget holder, technical evaluator: what each cares about.

## Problems
The friction before they find you, why current options fall short, what it costs (time, money, risk), how it feels.

## Alternatives
Direct competitors, other ways to solve it, doing nothing. Where each falls short, honestly.

## What's different
Capabilities the alternatives lack, how that changes the outcome, why customers choose you.

## Objections and poor fits
The top objections and the true answers. Who this is not for.

## Switching
What pushes them away from today's way, what pulls them to you, what habit holds them, what worries them about changing.

## Customer language
How they describe the problem and the product (verbatim). Words to use, words to avoid, product terms and their meaning.

## Voice
Tone, formality, how direct, a few traits, the language(s) and register (for Hebrew: register and gender approach).

## Proof
Real numbers with their source and date. Customers you may name. Quotes with name and role and permission. Certifications that actually exist.

## Goals
The main conversion action. Current numbers if known.

## Changes
- v1 (YYYY-MM-DD): first version.
```

## Keeping it

On every real change, raise the version, update the date and add a line at the top of Changes saying what changed and why ("v3: repositioned from email tool to deliverability platform after 5 customer calls"). Typo fixes don't need a version. Every copy task reads this file first, and anything it doesn't contain is asked for, not invented.
