# Compose authored product figures and feature grids

Stageshow's framing, bento and background references describe pictures made from product UI. These are optional illustration recipes, not changes to the protected app, landing-page or agent-chat designs. Keep the real product's tokens and anatomy, and use [PRODUCT-ILLUSTRATIONS.md](PRODUCT-ILLUSTRATIONS.md) for story and rehearsal. [stageshow, references/framing.md; references/bento.md; references/backgrounds.md; repository owner-design rules]

## Choose a hero, then its supporting surfaces

[stageshow, references/framing.md, The rules; The layouts]

Choose the focal object and retain what identifies its task: useful chrome, title, context rows, action and result. Supporting surfaces need a different job and visual weight. A satellite belongs across the hero's quiet edge, over chrome or margin rather than useful text. A product's anchored popover, menu or toast remains where the product puts it; it is not a freely placed satellite.

The source starts with one hero and at most one or two satellites. If two are needed, vary their kind and size; an annotation may explain a detail more clearly than another card. Begin frontal and compare any tilt against that version at the actual shown size. Perspective and depth should support the selected picture.

| Recipe | Picture it serves |
|---|---|
| Bottom bleed | A feature-card hero fills the slot and continues below it. |
| Corner bleed | A hero sits beside page copy and continues past two edges. |
| Satellite focus | A small result carries the story while the larger product supplies context. |
| Device pair | A phone and desktop show distinct parts of a cross-device action. |
| Many of one | Repetition communicates scale, with one item carrying the focal detail. |
| Panel over product | A setting and its visible effect remain connected without hiding either. |
| Whole, evenly inset | A complete window remains readable and the brief calls for it. |

These are alternatives, not a requirement to crop every figure. Exact source crop percentages, phone dimensions and shadow tokens remain its production choices.

## Let the final crop decide the geometry

[stageshow, references/framing.md, The rules; Small formats and the page; Phones]

- Crop filler, preserving the action, payoff, cursor target and words needed to understand them. If they do not fit, change the layout or stage instead of slicing the story.
- Measure gutters after wrappers and scaling. Choose a clear inset or deliberate bleed, avoiding tiny accidental cuts and near-tangents.
- Preserve a window or device's recognizable proportions. Crop or recompose instead of squashing it.
- Leave room for every opened overlay throughout the loop. A good resting crop can fail when a menu or result appears.
- Put shadows on the visible shape and keep their extent inside an intentionally unframed composition. A fade crop uses a mask over filler rather than a color overlay over useful content.
- On a narrow host, crop closer, stack or drop a satellite, or change the arrangement. Keep useful content at reading size instead of shrinking an entire dashboard.
- After a move or resize, inspect the crop, overlap and full loop again. Native notification anatomy and readable labels take precedence over fitting a complete tiny phone.

## Design the set before its tiles

[stageshow, references/bento.md, The rules; Kinds of tile; Planning a grid]

Write a tile plan with span, kind, the one idea and words before building. Give the set a lead and contrast neighbors through kind, density, silhouette and visual weight. A component crop, hero UI, real statistic, short statement, object, diagram, collection and licensed photograph offer different roles. Repeating windows and chrome in every tile removes that contrast.

Keep page copy at its native size outside each picture's drawing surface. Each tile owns its own picture and crop; one scale around the whole grid shrinks the headlines too. Measure the actual picture slot so an inner fixed-width drawing does not cut content halfway across a wider tile or leave an unintended strip.

Use a shared frame language for gaps, corners, inset and copy placement while varying the contents. Compose adjacent grounds together and avoid repeating the same string, claim or visual signal without purpose. Real statistics require real evidence. The source's UI-tile fraction, one-chrome recipe, type sizes and five-tile examples are scoped starting choices, not universal counts.

Give motion one place to lead. A supporting tile can make one small change or respond on hover while the lead carries a short story; several competing loops remove the resting focus. Recompose the grid at the width where its content needs it, retaining a clear lead and readable native-size text. [stageshow, references/bento.md, rules 11 to 14]

## Compose foreground and ground together

[stageshow, references/backgrounds.md, Choose the ground for what it does; Objects and drawings as tiles]

A quiet solid can support dense UI; a graphic field can give sparse content direction. Keep useful text over calm ground and separate the foreground by value before adding more effects. Material cues need consistent light, edges and shadows. Photography requires a suitable licensed image; a drawing remains an illustration rather than a claimed photo.

Center a collection by its complete silhouette, including the objects behind its front item. Keep back pieces away from copy. Compare adjacent colors and the whole grid silhouette; changing every accent independently does not establish a coherent picture.

## Prove it

[stageshow, references/framing.md, Checks; references/bento.md, Planning a grid; references/backgrounds.md]

Inspect each tile and the set at widest and narrowest actual hosts. The hero leads, neighbors differ intentionally, words remain readable, the picture owns its crop, overlays stay in frame and supporting surfaces preserve the action and result. Review the full loop after composition changes. A good standalone tile can still be the wrong neighbor.
