# From brief to concept

How to get from the brief to an idea worth building, before any shot exists. Read it when the brief leaves the direction open, and again whenever a review says "tour", "generic" or "weak ending".

## A concept is three lines

| Part | Question |
|---|---|
| **Idea** | What should the viewer believe afterwards? At most 8 words. |
| **Device** | What one thing carries that idea through every shot? (This skill calls it the carrier.) |
| **Grammar** | What rule does the film's form obey? "Everything moves vertically, under gravity." "After the turn, the film only gets quieter." |

A style ("bold kinetic minimal"), a mood ("premium, energetic"), a format ("a walkthrough with captions") or a feature list is not a concept. Grammar is where a film becomes ownable: two competitors can share an idea, rarely a grammar.

## Inventory first

Ideas come from specifics. Fill these into `shotlist.md` before ideating, the first two rows first, because they are where films most often come up short.

| List | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Name | What the name means or evokes: its root, second meanings, the physical thing it names. It often hands you the device and the mark. |
| Non-obvious behaviour | "Unlike the obvious version, it..." This is the proof beat: a film that only shows the naive version would be just as true of a simpler product. |
| Value sentence | "Before: ___. After: ___." in the viewer's words, no feature names. The turn is the gap between the halves. |
| Verbs | Ten verbs the product performs, from literal (sorts, pings) to abstract (steadies, forgives). |
| Objects | The things on its screen, each with its states: a row that resolves, a card that folds, a reading that crosses a threshold. |
| The number | The one quantity that changes: 412 → 1, 42% → 18%. A number can carry a whole film. |
| Pain scene | Time, place and what is on screen at the worst moment. The hook is shot here. |
| Category clichés | The five images every film in this category uses. They are banned for this film. |
| World sound | What the product's world sounds like: a tick, a latch, a pour. The sonic signature and the bookend. |

## Three lenses, three ideas each

Write at least three one-line sketches per lens, then keep one finalist per lens. The first idea in a lens is usually the category cliché; the third is usually yours. Every lens should end up showing the non-obvious behaviour.

- **The verb made literal.** Take a verb and stage the physical process it names, built from the product's own objects (not clip art beside a screenshot). Make the whole frame obey the verb.
- **The pain made visible.** Give the pain a quantity (mass, noise, count, clutter, speed) and let it grow until the frame can't hold it; the turn relieves the same quantity. Keep the problem under ~20% of the film, and show it, never state it in text.
- **A formal device**, married to a verb:

| Device | What it is | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Relay object | one small thing handed from shot to shot, never lost | launch films, many shots |
| Bookend | the same image and sound first and last, one thing changed | before/after promises |
| One take | a single unbroken camera move | depth, scale, flow |
| Container becomes product | the mark or the frame opens into the UI | brand and product in one breath |
| Counter as spine | one number counts through the whole film | products that reduce, sort or add up |
| Split-sync | before and after side by side on one beat | speed and effort claims |
| Rule of the frame | the form obeys the product's rule (only vertical moves; the horizon never tilts) | making a familiar story ownable |
| Scale jump | the same object at 16 px and at full frame | small units that add up |
| Style journey | one character or one motion continues unbroken while the world changes style era by era (cave painting, mosaic, ink, pixel, today) | histories, "through the ages" films, showing range |
| Compressed clock | a long span mapped onto a familiar one (all of history as one day, a year as a minute), with the clock always on screen | timelines, scale of change |

When all nine are weak, run these against the inventory one at a time: make the invisible visible; follow one unit (one alert, one paycheck) through its life; show the absence; borrow a physical law; build the logo from the problem's pieces; constrain (no cuts, no words before halfway, three objects only); do the opposite of the category on purpose.

## Tests that kill weak ideas

| Test | Run it | It fails when |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | swap in a competitor's name; strike every prop another product's film could use | the film still works, or fewer than two owned props remain |
| Deletion | delete the demo beats, then the value beats | the value vanishes with the demo, or the film works without the value (a tour) |
| Muted | describe the film in pictures only | problem → result can't be told without copy or sound |
| Feature word | would a stranger watching muted use the brief's word for the feature? | they would name a neighbouring thing |
| Specificity | would it be just as true of a simpler product (an even split, a plain list)? | yes: rebuild the proof around the non-obvious behaviour |
| Poster | describe the last frame printed on a wall | it doesn't say who made it and what it does |
| One sentence | say the idea aloud | you need "and" (two ideas) or an adjective (a style) |
| Retell | how would a viewer describe it tomorrow? | a style ("it was slick") instead of an image ("the dot that...") |
| Budget | count capabilities and beats against the format | more than 2 capabilities, or more than 10 beats per 30 s |
| Truth | does the product do what the picture shows; do the numbers agree? | a promised behaviour it lacks, or a frame that contradicts the story |

Generic props fail ownership unless the product literally is the thing: rocket, lightbulb, globe with arcs, up-and-right chart, a device floating in a void, a glowing orb or sparkle for AI, shield or padlock, puzzle pieces, gears, stopwatch, magnifying glass, confetti, handshake, bullseye.

Score the survivors 0-3 on ownership, muted clarity, device strength, surprise and buildability; take the best total with ownership at least 2, ties going to buildability. Keep the winner's spine and graft the single best element of each loser (a grammar, a line, an opening image), naming the graft so it doesn't creep back as a second idea. The logline is the film seen, in at most 15 words: device + what it does + the turn + the result.

## Designing the device

Where devices come from, best first: the product's own object with its states (the deposit row, the incident card); the name's meaning; the key number; a primitive of the mark; the accent colour; a sound (only as partner to a visual). A bare dot or line is the lazy default: use one only when it has behaviour only this product has.

A good device is **one** (two split attention), **changes scale** (small enough to travel, able to fill the frame once), **has a state that changes** (it ticks, fills, locks or counts; a device that only moves is a logo on tour), is **owned**, and **ends as the mark** in a launch film or sting, or as the unit the product acts on, resolved, in a feature video or loop.

Write its path as a chain of "becomes", one link per shot, each with its seam: *full-frame iris → ticking dot → the app's now-dot → the send button → flood → the period in the last line → the mark.* It never blinks out at a seam; it changes role, not size, colour or edge quality, across a cut; it already sits where the eye is at every cut; it floods the frame at most once (usually the turn); the target locks before the device lands on it.

**Bookends:** open and close on the same image and sound with exactly one thing changed; that change is the promise. A clock at 3:12 first and 3:13 last says "fixed in a minute" without a word.

## Beats

| Beat | Job | Length | Works when | Fails as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | inside the problem, moving at frame 0 | 1-2 s | felt by 1 s, stated by 2 s | a logo, "Introducing...", a question, a blank frame |
| Problem | the pain as a growing quantity | 2-4 s | a stranger could name it muted | a statement with no picture |
| Turn | the product intervenes: the biggest change so far, on a downbeat | 1-2 s | the strongest event yet (flood, drop, silence then drop) | the product simply appears |
| Proof | one capability as cause → action → result | 4-8 s each, 1-2 a film | the result holds 36 frames + 6 per word before the camera leaves | a feature list |
| Promise | the idea stated once, calling back the hook's words | 2-4 s | it reuses a word from the hook | a slogan unrelated to what we saw |
| Lockup | the device becomes the mark; the name | 3-5 s with tail | the last frame works as a poster | a replayed reveal, a fade to black |

**Cause → action → result** for every product action: something visible makes the action necessary (a typed intent, an arriving event, a threshold crossed); the product's real behaviour in its own UI (no sparkle standing in for "magic"); a changed state still true when paused, held before the camera moves on.

## Arcs by format

| Format | Arc | The device's job | The idea lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch film, 20-45 s | hook → problem → turn → proof ×1-2 → promise → lockup | carries every shot, becomes the mark | the turn at ~20-25%, the callback at the promise |
| Feature video, 8-30 s | situation → cause → action → result (held) → name | the unit the product acts on | the result |
| Feature loop, 4-15 s | rest A → cause → action → result → rest A | its state is the loop | mid-loop; the last frame is the first |
| Logo sting, 3-8 s | gather → assemble with a contact → hit → wordmark → building hold | the mark's primitives | the hit |
| Walkthrough, 20-90 s | cold open on the end result → chapters of cause → action → result → recap → lockup | one record followed through every chapter | each chapter's result |
| Teaser, ≤ 15 s | the effect without the product → the name and a date | a partial glimpse of the mark | the name |
| Before/after | the same shot twice on one camera move: without, then with | the camera move | the difference |

A 30 s launch film: hook and problem ~20%, turn ~10%, proof ~35-40%, promise ~15%, lockup and tail ~15-20%. A loop needs a cyclic story (an inbox that clears and refills), never enter → hold → exit.

**Five launch arcs** to pick one from: problem-agitate-solve when the pain is known and urgent (hook, pain, agitation, solution tease, product, proof, call to action); future pacing when the product sells a new category (imagine, name it, remove the pain, mechanism, outcome); a demo loop when the interface explains itself (a question, the product, demo cycle 1, demo cycle 2, trust); before-after-bridge when an old workflow meets a better one (before, a tease of after, the product as the bridge, step 1, step 2, the wow); a feature-benefit cascade when the product is feature-rich or status-driven (category hook, then each feature paired with its benefit, a climax). Translate every feature into the viewer's gain; never stack raw features. The line's shape implies the shot: a jab whose key word swaps in place wants a word swap; a relatable thought edited mid-sentence wants live typing; a march of milestones ending "until now" wants a pan across stations; "a doc? a database? a wiki? No, all of them" wants options cycling until a hero crashes in; one statistic spoken alone wants a single count-up with no context yet; "it starts as a search bar, it becomes your workflow" wants one small widget transforming in place. Vary the shapes across the film.

## End cards

1. The device becomes the mark: the lockup arrives from the story, not as a new logo animation. It may echo the mid-film reveal, never replay it.
2. Assemble over 60-80 frames, then hold 36-80 frames still (or creeping at most 6%); a longer hold must visibly build (a 10-20% dolly). A card that cuts in complete and sits static reads as dead.
3. A URL or call to action stays readable at least 1.7 s, at the secondary size (44-56 px at 1080p), still while read.
4. The last frame is the poster: the mark and the name, legible 480 px wide.
5. At least 60 frames of tail after the last event while the audio decays to digital silence. Never fade to black: the player freezes on the last frame.
6. Ending on the device alone is elegant but loses recall in thumbnails: play the bookend inside the lockup instead.
7. Nothing else: no row of social icons, store badges or second tagline unless the brief asks.

## Gate before the shot list

The logline is at most 15 words with no "and" joining two ideas; the concept passed ownership, deletion and specificity, results written down, the specificity answer naming the non-obvious behaviour and the beat that shows it; the word count fits the format budget; the device appears in every beat row; the last frame is described and holds the mark and the name.
